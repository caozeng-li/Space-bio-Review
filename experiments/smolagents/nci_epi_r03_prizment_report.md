# Scientific Project Review

**Project:** Immune-Regulating MHC Class I-Like Proteins and Colorectal Cancer Risk  
**Principal Investigator:** Anna Prizment, University of Minnesota  
**Mechanism:** R03  
**Review date:** 2026-10-28 (evaluating as prospective)

---

## Overall Assessment

This R03 proposal addresses a well-motivated question: whether circulating MHC class I-like proteins (MICA, MICB, ULBP1–4)—ligands of the activating NKG2D receptor—measured in prediagnostic plasma predict subsequent colorectal cancer (CRC) risk, and what factors determine their levels. The biological rationale is sound. NKG2D ligands are stress-induced immune surveillance molecules; their proteolytic shedding produces soluble forms that can act as decoys, downregulating NKG2D and impairing anti-tumor immunity. Most prior human evidence concerns expression or soluble levels after cancer diagnosis; prospective prediagnostic data are sparse. The proposal therefore addresses a genuine knowledge gap.

The study is a secondary analysis of existing ARIC cohort resources: SOMAscan proteomics at up to three visits, genotypes, lifestyle data, and cancer outcomes. The design is essentially a nested prospective study within a well-characterized cohort. The scientific contribution—first prospective evaluation of six NKG2D ligands jointly, with repeated measures, in relation to incident CRC—is novel and potentially informative for immune surveillance mechanisms in colorectal carcinogenesis.

However, the proposal has several material limitations that collectively reduce confidence that the work as currently specified can deliver definitive evidence on its central hypotheses. The most important concern is measurement validity: the proposal relies on SOMAscan aptamer-based relative quantification of MHC class I-like proteins without evidence that these specific aptamers have been validated against established immunoassays for MICA, MICB, or ULBP proteins. The power for change analyses and the long-term analysis is modest. The Mendelian randomization (MR) component, presented as a secondary aim, lacks critical detail on instrument selection and power. The factor-score approach, while statistically defensible, may obscure protein-specific signals in a family where different ligands have distinct biological roles (transmembrane vs. GPI-anchored; differential shedding mechanisms). These issues are individually manageable in an exploratory R03 but collectively affect the inferential yield.

The project remains a worthwhile exploratory investment that could generate hypotheses for larger validation studies. The recommendation is conditional support, contingent on addressing the assay validation concern before full-scale analysis.

## Scientific Value: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The question has clear scientific importance. Immune evasion is a hallmark of cancer, and the NKG2D–NKG2DL axis is a central innate immune surveillance mechanism. The external review by Tan, Spillane, and Maher (Biology, 2023) confirms that "NKG2D deficiency increases susceptibility to some types of cancer" in mouse models and that "the prognostic impact of NKG2DL expression in human cancer is variable," with a complex balance between membrane-bound and soluble forms. The proposal correctly distinguishes the question from prior prognostic studies: rather than asking whether soluble MICA levels correlate with advanced disease, it asks whether levels in initially cancer-free individuals predict future CRC.

The contribution is primarily descriptive and associational—establishing prospective associations and their determinants—which is the stated goal of an R03 and appropriate for the stage. If positive associations emerge, they would provide the first prospective human evidence linking circulating NKG2D ligands to CRC development, opening a line of inquiry distinct from the existing prognostic literature. The inclusion of Black participants (approximately 23% of the ARIC analytical sample, per the related ARIC proteomic publication by Wang et al., 2026) addresses an understudied population in this field.

The proposal also contributes methodological value: applying factor analysis and marginal structural models to repeated proteomic measurements in a cancer epidemiology setting. The long-term objective—informing CRC prevention and early detection—is appropriately distant; the immediate contribution is establishing whether these proteins merit further investment.

The value is moderated by the unresolved question of whether SOMAscan aptamers validly measure the biologically relevant forms of these proteins. Soluble MICA, for instance, can be generated by ADAM-mediated cleavage and exosomal release; whether an aptamer captures both forms, and with what affinity relative to standard ELISA, is not addressed. The proposal acknowledges that "later validation of individual protein measurements using standard methods" is planned only after associations are observed. This sequential approach is reasonable for discovery but means that the core results from this R03 will be measured with an unvalidated assay for the target proteins.

A further nuance: the proposal hypothesizes that *higher* prediagnostic levels predict greater risk, based on the immune-evasion model of soluble ligand acting as a decoy. But the NKG2DL literature also shows that membrane expression can support immune recognition and that soluble forms may simply reflect tumor burden. The direction of association is genuinely uncertain—and the study is appropriately powered to detect associations in either direction—but the proposal does not fully discuss the interpretative challenge if, for example, lower levels are associated with risk (consistent with reduced immune surveillance capacity).

## Innovation: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The proposal has genuine novelty in several respects. First, no prior prospective study has examined prediagnostic circulating MICA, MICB, or ULBP1–4 in relation to incident CRC. The external search confirms this gap: there are no published prospective nested case–control or cohort studies of soluble MICA and CRC incidence in initially cancer-free populations. The related publications that exist—Onyeaghala et al. (PLOS ONE, 2019, the investigators' own pancreatic cancer work), Kopp et al. (J Clin Immunol, 2009, MICA-TM alleles and CRC progression), and Burkes et al. (J Clin Med, 2026, sMICA in lung cancer)—all concern prevalent disease, prognosis, or case–control designs that cannot establish temporality.

Second, the simultaneous measurement of six related proteins with repeated measures over two decades is a distinctive feature. The repeated-measures design (Visits 2, 3, and 5) permits assessment of both short-term (3-year) and long-term (approximately 20-year) trajectories, which no prior study of these proteins has done. The use of marginal structural models to address time-dependent confounding in the change analysis is methodologically appropriate, though not itself novel.

Third, the integration of genetic determinants (SNPs and microsatellites) with protein levels and cancer risk in the same cohort provides triangulation that few studies can offer.

The novelty is incremental rather than transformative. The six proteins belong to a well-studied family; MICA in particular has been extensively investigated in cancer, though predominantly in prognostic contexts. The A5.1 microsatellite–soluble MICA relationship is already established from the investigators' pancreatic cancer work (Table 2). The proposal extends these observations to CRC and to a prospective design, which is a meaningful but not paradigm-shifting advance.

The ULBP1–4 proteins are less studied than MICA and MICB. Their inclusion is a strength of the proposal, though the biological understanding of circulating ULBP proteins—particularly which are GPI-anchored and thus primarily released via extracellular vesicles rather than ADAM cleavage—adds complexity that the proposal acknowledges but does not fully integrate into the analytical plan.

## Rigor: C (Moderate) — Confidence: B (High)

The design has several strengths. ARIC is a well-characterized prospective cohort with standardized data collection, long follow-up, and rigorous cancer ascertainment via state-registry linkage. The SOMAscan platform has documented quality control, with median CV of 6.2% across 220 runs in version 4. The proposal includes appropriate sensitivity analyses (excluding early diagnoses, testing proportional hazards assumptions, inverse-probability weighting for missing data). The statistical power for Aim 1a (baseline proteins) is adequate: 80% power to detect HR 1.24 per SD with 360 cases.

Several concerns reduce the rigor rating:

**1. Assay validity for the target proteins.** The proposal provides SOMAscan pilot data (median CV 5%, Spearman 0.89, ICC 0.96 in 42 ARIC participants) but these metrics describe technical reproducibility, not agreement with a reference method for MICA, MICB, or ULBP proteins. The external literature (Haslam et al., Proteomics, 2022) demonstrates that correlations between SOMAscan and Olink platforms for the same proteins are highly variable, with only 14.7% of shared proteins showing r > 0.75. The Candia et al. (Sci Rep, 2022) technical assessment of SOMAscan v4.1 documents potential specificity issues. The proposal does not cite any validation of the specific MICA/MICB/ULBP aptamers against ELISA or other established methods. This is the most significant gap in the current plan.

**2. Factor-score approach and biological heterogeneity.** Combining six proteins with different structures (MICA and MICB are transmembrane proteins with three extracellular domains; ULBP1, ULBP3 are GPI-anchored; ULBP2 can be either; ULBP4 is transmembrane) and different shedding mechanisms into a single factor score may obscure distinct biological signals. The proposal anticipates correlations of 0.4–0.55, which is moderate. If, for example, MICA shows a positive association with CRC risk while ULBP1 shows no association or an inverse one, the factor score could be null despite a real MICA signal. The proposal plans exploratory individual-protein analyses, which mitigates this concern, but the primary analysis and power calculation are based on the factor score.

**3. Power for change analyses and long-term follow-up.** Aim 1b has 306 cases for the short-term change analysis and only 56 cases for the long-term (Visit 5) analysis. The long-term analysis is explicitly described as "exploratory," which is appropriate, but the short-term analysis also has limited precision: the detectable HR of 1.26 per SD is modest, and the quartile comparison requires HR 1.65. For a secondary analysis of existing data, this is a constraint of the resource rather than a design flaw, but it limits what can be concluded from null results.

**4. Power discrepancy in categorical analyses.** The proposal states that the "categorical power example uses tertiles and HR 1.58, whereas the analysis description specifies quartiles." This is a minor inconsistency; quartiles with a smaller reference group would require a somewhat larger effect size for the same power, or else power would be slightly lower than stated.

**5. Mendelian randomization component.** The MR aim is described as an "exploration" and "secondary," which is appropriate. However, several details are missing. The proposal states that "genetic variants that are strongly associated with the relevant proteins and associated with CRC will be examined as instruments" and that "the team is identifying such variants in ARIC and replicating associations in CRC datasets available through dbGaP." This approach risks winner's curse: selecting instruments from the same dataset used for the MR analysis inflates first-stage F-statistics and biases causal estimates. The proposal does not address whether instruments will be identified in independent samples, which is standard practice. The use of `ivprobit` in Stata rather than more modern MR methods (inverse-variance weighted, MR-Egger, weighted median) is noted. The power for MR with binary outcomes and 360 cases is likely limited, and the proposal does not provide MR-specific power calculations.

**6. Multiple testing and model selection.** The Bonferroni correction for Aim 2 (α = 0.05/6 ≈ 0.008) is conservative for six correlated proteins. The proposal's approach to covariate selection—"characteristics associated with a biomarker in univariate analysis will enter multivariable models"—is a common but potentially problematic strategy that can introduce bias through collider stratification or overfitting.

**7. Missing data handling.** The proposal describes inverse-probability weighting if missingness exceeds 10% but does not discuss the likely pattern or extent of missing proteomic data across visits, nor whether the Visit 2 assays (described as "underway") will have different missingness patterns from the completed Visit 3 and 5 assays. Attrition between Visit 2 (n ≈ 12,589) and Visit 5 (n ≈ 6,538) is substantial and may be non-random with respect to both protein levels and CRC risk, potentially introducing selection bias in the long-term analysis.

## Feasibility: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The project is highly feasible in its core operational aspects. All data already exist or are being generated through mechanisms independent of this R03. No new participant recruitment, specimen collection, or laboratory assays are required. The ARIC infrastructure—data management, cancer ascertainment, genomic data, proteomic measurements—is established and has supported numerous publications, including the recently published proteome-wide cancer analysis (Wang et al., 2026). The analytical methods (Cox regression, mixed models, marginal structural models, instrumental variable analysis) are within the team's expertise and use standard software (SAS, Stata).

The 24-month timeline is realistic for a secondary data analysis with three aims. Months 1–3 for IRB and data extraction; 4–12 for Aim 1; 13–18 for Aim 2; 19–24 for Aim 3 and manuscript preparation.

The critical feasibility question is whether the SOMAscan panel actually contains aptamers targeting the six specified proteins with adequate performance characteristics. The proposal states that "the full study will use version 4 measurements of 4,931 proteins, with the six prespecified MHC class I-like proteins as its focus." The SOMAscan v4 (7K) panel measures approximately 7,000 aptamers targeting ~6,400 proteins. The proposal does not explicitly confirm that all six proteins are represented on the panel, though this is implied by the pilot data. If one or more of the six proteins are not on the panel or have poor aptamer performance, the study's scope would be reduced. This is a factual premise that should be verified before full analysis.

The reliance on the ARIC coordinating center for data access introduces a procedural dependency, but this is standard for ARIC ancillary studies and the investigators have existing ARIC collaborations.

## Ethics and Safety: A (Excellent) — Confidence: A (Very High)

The project uses existing de-identified data with no participant contact or additional specimen collection. The proposal describes appropriate privacy protections: investigators receive coded research IDs only, with the identity linkage retained by the ARIC Coordinating Center. Data transmission, handling, and distribution follow established protocols. Results will be shared under NIH policy.

No material ethical or safety concerns are identified. The study does not involve vulnerable populations beyond the standard protections already in place for the ARIC cohort. The use of existing data maximizes the scientific return on participant contributions without imposing additional burden.

---

## Management: B (Good) — Confidence: C (Moderate)

The PI has relevant expertise in molecular cancer epidemiology, as evidenced by the prior MICA–pancreatic cancer publication (Onyeaghala et al., 2019). The team is described as combining "molecular cancer epidemiology, computational genetics, and biostatistics," which matches the required skill set.

The schedule is plausible: 24 months with overlapping analysis and manuscript preparation. The tasks are sequential but could be parallelized if needed.

Confidence in this rating is moderate because the proposal does not provide individual team member CVs or specify the roles and time commitments of each investigator. The R03 mechanism typically supports a single PI with limited staff; whether the described scope (three aims with complex statistical methods, genetic data, and proteomic data integration) can be accomplished by the available personnel within the budget is not documented. The proposal states that SOMAscan measurements at Visit 2 are "underway," creating a dependency on external data generation whose timeline is not controlled by this project.

## Overall Recommendation: Conditional Support

The proposal addresses a genuine knowledge gap with a well-motivated biological rationale, leverages exceptional existing resources, and could yield the first prospective evidence on circulating NKG2D ligands and CRC risk. The combination of six proteins, repeated measures, genetic data, and two population groups is a distinctive scientific opportunity that warrants investment at the exploratory R03 level.

Support is conditional on the following premise, which should be verified before the main analyses proceed:

1. **Assay validation for the target proteins:** The investigators should confirm that the SOMAscan aptamers for MICA, MICB, ULBP1, ULBP2, ULBP3, and ULBP4 are present on the version 4 panel and demonstrate acceptable agreement with established immunoassays (e.g., ELISA) for at least MICA and MICB in a subset of ARIC samples. If validation data are unavailable, the study should include a plan to generate them within the project period, or alternatively, acknowledge that the findings are contingent on aptamer performance and frame the results as hypothesis-generating with explicit caveats.

This condition is substantive because the scientific value of the associations depends on whether the measurements reflect the biologically relevant analytes. Without it, positive findings would require independent validation before publication, and null findings would be uninterpretable (they could reflect either true absence of association or poor assay performance). Addressing this early would strengthen the study's contribution without altering its design or scope.

Additional recommendations that would improve the plan but are not conditions of support:

- Consider pre-specifying individual-protein analyses as sensitivity analyses with appropriate multiple-testing adjustment, rather than treating the factor score as the sole primary analysis.
- Address the MR instrument selection issue by identifying instruments from external GWAS or by using split-sample approaches.
- Provide a more detailed plan for handling the substantial attrition between Visit 2 and Visit 5.
- Explicitly confirm that all six proteins are represented by aptamers on the SOMAscan v4 panel and report their technical performance characteristics (CV, signal-to-background) from the ARIC QC data.
