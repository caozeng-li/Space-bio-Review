# Scientific Project Review

**Proposal:** "Hair Extensions: Using Hair Levels to Interpret Adherence, Effectiveness and Pharmacokinetics with Real-World Oral PrEP, the Vaginal Ring, and Injectables"

**Principal Investigator:** Monica Gandhi, University of California San Francisco  
**Mechanism:** R01  
**Review date:** 2026-10-28 (prospective assessment)

---

## Overall Assessment

This proposal seeks to evaluate hair antiretroviral concentrations as cumulative exposure and adherence biomarkers across three distinct delivery modalities—oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC) pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) in East Africa (SEARCH), the dapivirine vaginal ring (MTN-025/HOPE), and long-acting injectable cabotegravir for HIV treatment (ACTG A5359). The project is anchored in three existing, well-resourced parent studies, which profoundly shapes its feasibility. The overarching hypothesis—that pairing a long-term cumulative measure (hair) with shorter-term measures (plasma, self-report, residual ring drug) will better characterize exposure patterns and predict outcomes than any single snapshot—is sensible and supported by substantial preliminary evidence.

The proposal's principal strengths are: (1) a mature body of prior work establishing dose–hair concentration relationships for TFV, validated by directly observed therapy studies and replicated across treatment and prevention contexts; (2) a pragmatic, cost-efficient sampling design that concentrates laboratory assays on informative cases (seroconverters and renal-decline participants with matched controls) while retaining population inference through sampling weights; (3) the integration of three delivery platforms—oral, topical, and injectable—within a single coordinated project, which enables cross-modality comparison of hair as a universal adherence metric; and (4) collection within active, funded parent studies, reducing recruitment and infrastructure risk.

The principal limitations are: (1) the cabotegravir hair assay lacks the extensive dose–response validation that exists for TFV, and the proposed plasma–hair correlation study in a treatment population with adherence challenges may not straightforwardly generalize to prevention populations with different pharmacokinetic profiles; (2) all three aims rely on case–control sampling nested within cohorts where HIV seroconversion is the primary outcome, and the power for Aim 1 (100 expected cases) and Aim 2 (60 expected cases) to detect modest exposure–outcome associations is constrained; (3) the hair-to-plasma calibration for dapivirine is not established by a directly observed dosing study, unlike TFV, leaving dapivirine hair interpretation substantially dependent on cross-sectional correlations with residual ring drug and plasma—measures that themselves have known limitations; (4) several important analytical details—particularly the full validation characteristics of the dapivirine and cabotegravir hair assays beyond spiked chromatograms—are stated only in outline.

The project as proposed makes a **worthwhile contribution** to HIV prevention and treatment science by systematically extending hair-based pharmacokinetic monitoring across delivery modalities. However, the combined weight of design constraints in Aims 2 and 3—particularly the absence of directly observed dosing calibration for dapivirine and cabotegravir hair assays and the modest power for HIV outcome analyses—limits confidence that all three aims can achieve their stated inferential goals with the current design.

## Scientific Value: **B (Good)** — Confidence: **B (High)**

The question is important. Adherence remains the central challenge limiting the real-world effectiveness of biomedical HIV prevention. Across oral PrEP, vaginal rings, and long-acting injectables, the gap between product availability and consistent use determines population-level impact. Objective adherence measures that capture different time windows are needed both for interpreting trial results and for clinical monitoring during implementation. The proposal addresses this need directly.

The attainable knowledge contributions differ across aims:

**Aim 1 (SEARCH/TDF-FTC):** This is the most mature component. The dose–hair TFV relationship is already established by a directly observed therapy study (Liu et al., *PLoS ONE*, 2014, PMID:24421901), which demonstrated a "strong linear relationship between frequency of dosing and tenofovir levels in scalp hair" with an estimated 76% increase in hair level per two-fold dose increase. Hair TFV/FTC has already been associated with renal function decline during PrEP (Gandhi et al., *Lancet HIV*, 2016, PMID:27658870, showing "a monotonic association between percentage decrease in creatinine clearance and the number of doses...as estimated by hair concentrations"). The added contribution here is evaluating hair levels as predictors of HIV seroconversion and renal toxicity within a large, population-based PrEP program in East Africa with diverse participant characteristics and delivery settings (clinic-based versus community-based). This context—real-world PrEP delivery in generalized epidemic settings—is independently valuable beyond the biomarker validation.

**Aim 2 (HOPE/dapivirine ring):** The contribution is more exploratory. Hair dapivirine has not been calibrated against directly observed ring use. The proposal will compare hair dapivirine with plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report, but none of these comparators is a gold standard. Residual ring drug can vary with use conditions and handling; plasma reflects recent exposure. Demonstrating that hair dapivirine correlates with these measures and with HIV acquisition would establish it as a feasible cumulative exposure biomarker, but the absence of a dose–response calibration limits the quantitative interpretation achievable within this study.

**Aim 3 (ACTG A5359/cabotegravir):** This aim addresses an emerging need. Long-acting cabotegravir is being deployed for both treatment and prevention, and pharmacokinetic monitoring tools are needed, particularly for populations with adherence challenges or missed visits. The proposal's use of segmental hair analysis (0.25 cm portions for weekly resolution) to track exposure between injections is conceptually innovative and would provide a granularity not available from trough plasma alone. However, the proposal states this aim on a treatment population to "inform possible future use in injectable prevention"—a valid but indirect route to the ultimate application.

A noted limitation across all aims is that the knowledge contribution is primarily descriptive and correlational: establishing how hair levels relate to other measures and to outcomes, rather than testing whether hair-based monitoring interventions improve outcomes. This is an appropriate scope for the project's stage, but the proposal sometimes overstates the direct clinical utility of the findings, describing them as supporting "development of tailored adherence interventions" without specifying how the biomarker data would be acted upon in real time.

## Innovation: **B (Good)** — Confidence: **B (High)**

The project's innovation lies in its integrative scope rather than in fundamentally new measurement technology. Hair antiretroviral measurement is an established technique from the Gandhi laboratory and others, with published applications to protease inhibitors, atazanavir, and TFV/FTC in treatment and prevention (Gandhi et al., *AIDS*, 2009, PMID not provided in full; Gandhi et al., *Clinical Infectious Diseases*, 2011; Koss et al., *AIDS*, 2015). The proposal itself acknowledges this foundation.

The innovations are:

1. **Cross-modality comparison:** Systematically applying hair measurement across oral, topical ring, and injectable delivery within a single project. No prior study has done this in a coordinated fashion. This enables direct comparison of how well hair captures exposure from fundamentally different pharmacokinetic profiles—daily oral dosing with rapid absorption and elimination, sustained local release from a ring, and intramuscular depot with prolonged absorption.

2. **Segmental analysis for injectables:** Aim 3's use of 0.25 cm hair segments to reconstruct weekly exposure patterns between monthly cabotegravir injections is genuinely novel. While segmental hair analysis has been used forensically and in a case report of PrEP seroconversion (Thaden et al., *AIDS*, 2018, PMID:29683856), its systematic application to long-acting injectable monitoring in a cohort of 150+ participants has not been reported.

3. **Integrated exposure-pattern classification:** The proposal's framework for combining short-term (plasma) and long-term (hair) measures to classify adherence patterns—sustained use, intermittent use, recent "white-coat" dosing—has been suggested in prior work but has not been implemented systematically across three delivery platforms. This pattern-based approach moves beyond single-threshold adherence categorizations.

The proposal is not, however, attempting to develop new hair assays de novo. The TFV/FTC hair assay is already validated (relative error and CV <15%). For dapivirine and cabotegravir, the proposal displays extracted ion chromatograms from spiked hair samples, demonstrating feasibility of detection, but the full validation parameters (precision, accuracy, recovery, matrix effects, stability) are not detailed. This places the dapivirine and cabotegravir components closer to method adaptation than method development, which is appropriate for the project's scope but limits the degree of innovation claimed.

Relative to independent prior work: the 2016 review by Stalter et al. (*J Int AIDS Soc*, PMID:27142091) identified hair drug levels as one of four promising approaches for vaginal ring adherence measurement, but noted it required further development. The present proposal takes that recommended next step. The Spinelli et al. 2020 review (*Curr HIV/AIDS Rep*, PMID:32424549) describes hair ARV levels as an established cumulative adherence metric; the current proposal extends this to cabotegravir and dapivirine, which were not covered in the prior review's evidence base.

## Rigor: **C (Moderate)** — Confidence: **B (High)**

The rigor of the proposed design varies substantially across aims and analysis components.

**Strengths:**

- **Sampling design:** The case–control sampling with known sampling fractions, applied across all three aims, is a genuine strength. By assaying all seroconverters and a random sample of controls, the design concentrates resources on informative observations. The use of sampling weights to recover population-level inference is statistically sound and has been validated in the survey-sampling literature. The certainty sampling of renal-decline participants in Aim 1 further enriches informativeness. This approach avoids the cost of assaying ~83,200 samples while preserving the ability to estimate exposure–outcome associations.

- **Analytical methods for TFV/FTC:** The LC-MS/MS methods for hair TFV/FTC are well-characterized, with validated ranges (0.002–0.400 ng/mg for TFV, 0.02–4.0 ng/mg for FTC) and QC performance (CV <15%). These are published and reproducible.

- **Statistical approach for Aim 1:** The discrete-time Cox model via pooled logistic regression is appropriate for the longitudinal data structure with time-varying exposure. The handling of undetectable values (setting to detection limit and adding that limit before log transformation) is a defensible approach that reduces the influence of noise near zero.

- **Multiple comparison framework:** Each aim explicitly compares hair with plasma, self-report, and (for Aim 2) residual ring drug, providing internal validation of the hair measure against available alternatives.

**Weaknesses and concerns:**

- **Power for HIV outcome analyses:** The power calculations use simplified single-observation approximations. For Aim 1, 80% power for an OR ≥2.1 comparing lowest to upper three quartiles (2.6% vs. 1.2% seroconversion) is reported. This is a large effect size. Detecting more moderate but clinically meaningful exposure–outcome gradients (e.g., OR 1.5) would require substantially more events. With 100 expected seroconversions, the study is adequately powered only for large effects. The proposal acknowledges that repeated observations "supply additional information," but does not quantify the gain in effective sample size from repeated measures. For Aim 2, the stated detectable effect is a relative risk of 2.9 comparing lowest to upper quartiles (6.6% vs. 2.3%), which is even larger—this may reflect realistic expectations given the modest efficacy of the dapivirine ring (27–31% in phase III), but it means the study can only detect very large adherence–outcome gradients.

- **Absence of dapivirine and cabotegravir hair dose–response calibration:** This is the most significant rigor concern. For TFV, the Liu et al. (2014) directly observed therapy study provides the critical calibration linking dose frequency to hair concentration. No equivalent exists for dapivirine or cabotegravir. For dapivirine, the relationship between ring use (continuous, intermittent, removed) and hair incorporation is unknown—factors such as local versus systemic drug distribution, the proportion of drug reaching systemic circulation from vaginal delivery, and the efficiency of hair incorporation for dapivirine are uncharacterized. For cabotegravir, the proposal estimates a hair value of 66 ng/mg corresponding to 4× protein-adjusted IC90 in plasma, but this conversion factor appears to be an extrapolation rather than an empirically derived relationship. Without calibration, hair concentrations can be ranked (higher vs. lower) but cannot be interpreted quantitatively in terms of dosing behavior.

- **Confounding in exposure–outcome analyses:** The proposal adjusts for measured covariates, but unmeasured confounding between adherence behavior and HIV risk is well-documented. Participants with low adherence (low hair levels) may also have higher-risk behaviors, partner characteristics, or health-seeking patterns that independently increase HIV acquisition risk. The proposal does not discuss instrumental variable approaches, negative controls, or sensitivity analyses for unmeasured confounding. The comparison of hair with plasma and self-report within the same models partially addresses this (if all measures point in the same direction, confounding is less likely), but this is not a formal solution.

- **Segmental hair analysis assumptions:** The interpretation of 0.25 cm segments as "weekly average exposure" rests on the assumption of 1 cm/month occipital hair growth. Individual variation in hair growth rate (reported ranges of 0.6–1.4 cm/month), the catagen/telogen proportion, and the possibility of external contamination or cosmetic treatment are not discussed. The 2025 review by Adav & Ng (*Exp Mol Med*, PMID:41145616) explicitly identifies "hair growth variability, contamination and lack of standardized techniques" as challenges limiting hair analysis. These limitations are inherent to the technique, not a defect of this proposal, but they should be acknowledged and their impact on segmental interpretation discussed.

- **Forward selection for multivariable models (Aim 1):** Forward selection is known to produce biased coefficient estimates, inflated R², and unstable variable selection. The proposal could strengthen this by specifying cross-validation, penalized regression, or Bayesian model averaging instead.

- **Aim 3 virologic suppression analyses:** These are described as "exploratory" and use random-effects logistic models. With 150 participants in the long-acting arm and an unknown proportion experiencing virologic failure, the power to detect exposure–suppression relationships is not quantified. The proposal appropriately labels these as exploratory, but the contribution of this component to the overall project is correspondingly uncertain.

## Feasibility: **A (Excellent)** — Confidence: **A (Very High)**

This is the proposal's strongest criterion, and the rating reflects the exceptional implementation platform.

**Parent study infrastructure:** All three aims leverage existing, funded clinical studies with established sites, staff, regulatory approvals, and participant cohorts. SEARCH is a large, well-resourced community-randomized trial (NCT01864603) with demonstrated capacity to collect hair samples (95% acceptability in prior work). MTN-025/HOPE is an established open-label extension with four-country infrastructure. ACTG A5359 is an NIH-funded trial with central laboratory support through the ACTG network. The proposal adds specimen collection and analysis to studies that are already running; it does not need to recruit participants, establish sites, or maintain follow-up independently.

**Hair collection feasibility:** The proposal documents strong prior acceptability: 112/113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit in early SEARCH PrEP rollout, and approximately 95% acceptance in a separate community study of 837 participants. Collection takes approximately 90 seconds, requires no phlebotomy, and specimens are stable at room temperature. Staff training for different hairstyles is planned. The exclusion of completely bald scalps is a minor limitation acknowledged in the ethics section; axillary/pubic hair is explicitly excluded as a substitute, which is appropriate given different growth characteristics and drug incorporation kinetics.

**Laboratory capacity:** The Gandhi laboratory has established LC-MS/MS methods for hair TFV/FTC and has demonstrated feasibility for dapivirine and cabotegravir detection in spiked hair. The inclusion of Benet (pharmacokinetics), Gerona (analytic chemistry), and Bacchetti (biostatistics) provides relevant expertise. The case–control sampling strategy limits the number of assays to approximately 5,200 person-visits for Aim 1, 1,680 for Aim 2, and a manageable number for Aim 3 (150 participants × 7 time points ≈ 1,050 samples, though segmental analysis multiplies this). This total assay burden (~8,000–10,000 hair analyses plus plasma) is feasible within the project period.

**Schedule:** SEARCH follow-up spans approximately three years, HOPE one year plus exit, and A5359 through week 76. The staggered timing across studies is manageable.

**Capacity building:** The plan to conduct capacity-building work with African laboratories in the final project year is appropriate and would support future independent use of hair monitoring in the settings where it is most needed.

**Critical dependencies:** The project is critically dependent on the parent studies maintaining their planned enrollment, follow-up, and seroconversion rates. If SEARCH or HOPE experience lower-than-expected HIV incidence, the power for outcome analyses deteriorates. The proposal's power calculations assume specific seroconversion numbers (100 for Aim 1, 60 for Aim 2); substantial deviation would affect Aim 2 more than Aim 1, given the smaller numbers. This is a genuine risk but is mitigated by the fact that the parent studies are independently funded and managed.

## Ethics and Safety: **B (Good)** — Confidence: **B (High)**

The added research procedure—small-volume occipital hair collection—is minimally invasive. Risks are appropriately characterized: minor accidental scalp injury, confidentiality concerns, and inconvenience. The 90-second collection time poses negligible participant burden.

**Informed consent:** Hair collection is included in parent-study consent. For SEARCH, ages 15–17 require parental co-consent, which is appropriate. HOPE and A5359 involve adults only. The proposal states that participants with completely bald scalps cannot provide specimens, and that axillary/pubic hair will not substitute—this is both scientifically appropriate (different drug incorporation kinetics) and ethically sound (avoiding more invasive collection).

**Existing ethics approvals:** SEARCH and HOPE have parent-study ethics approvals and monitoring boards. A5359 arrangements are described as "being established." This is a gap but not a decisive concern: the proposal's timing relative to A5359 start-up is consistent with pending rather than absent ethics review. The review notes this as something to confirm rather than a barrier.

**Data practices:** Blinded hair-concentration datasets will be made publicly available after planned analyses. Findings will be shared with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders. These are appropriate commitments. The proposal does not detail a formal data-sharing plan (repository, timing, access conditions), but the intent is clearly stated.

**Adverse event monitoring:** Collection completeness, adverse events, and confidentiality breaches will be reviewed monthly and reported through study leadership. This is proportionate to the low-risk nature of the procedure.

**Potential concern:** Hair drug concentration data could theoretically be misused to penalize participants (e.g., in legal or employment contexts) if confidentiality is breached. The proposal addresses confidentiality generally but does not discuss specific protections against secondary use of biomarker data. This is a generic concern for pharmacologic adherence monitoring rather than a specific defect of this proposal.

## Management: **B (Good)** — Confidence: **B (High)**

**Leadership and expertise:** The team is well-constituted. Gandhi has led the hair antiretroviral measurement program for over a decade, with a publication record spanning dose–response validation, clinical outcome associations, and method development. The named collaborators bring complementary expertise: Havlir (SEARCH), Baeten (MTN/dapivirine ring), Castillo-Mancilla (ACTG A5359/long-acting ART), Benet and Gerona (pharmacokinetics and analytic chemistry), and Bacchetti (biostatistics). This covers the needed domains.

**Coordination:** The project adds specimen analysis to three parent studies with existing coordination structures. Study coordinators will receive collection training and monthly data-quality review. The management model is additive rather than requiring new coordination infrastructure, which reduces administrative risk.

**Resource allocation:** The proposal does not include a detailed budget (consistent with the review instruction not to require one), but the resource model—leveraging parent study infrastructure with added funding for specimen analysis—is efficient. The case–control sampling design further concentrates resources.

**Unspecified elements:** The proposal does not detail: (1) the specific division of statistical analysis responsibilities across the three aims; (2) a timeline with milestones for assay validation, enrollment completion, and analysis; (3) a project management or governance structure for decision-making across the three study teams. These are typical of a research narrative rather than a full administrative application and do not indicate incapacity. However, the multi-study, multi-site nature of the project would benefit from a more explicit coordination plan.

**Capacity building:** The plan to work with African laboratories in the final year is mentioned but not elaborated. Which laboratories, what specific capacity (assay performance, interpretation, quality assurance), and what sustainability model are not specified. This component's feasibility cannot be fully assessed on the information provided.

## Recommendation: **Conditional support** (*conditional_support*)

The project addresses an important scientific question with a well-validated measurement platform (hair TFV/FTC), leverages exceptional parent-study infrastructure, and brings an efficient sampling design. The integrative scope across three delivery modalities is a genuine strength that no other single project provides.

Support is conditional on the following premises being addressed, because they affect the inferential contribution of Aims 2 and 3:

1. **Dapivirine and cabotegravir hair assay validation:** The proposal should clarify the validation status of the dapivirine and cabotegravir hair LC-MS/MS methods beyond spiked-sample chromatograms. Key parameters include precision, accuracy, recovery, matrix effects, stability, and the lower limit of quantification in hair matrix. If these are already validated (e.g., through the Gandhi laboratory's unpublished work), this concern is resolved. If not, the first project period should include formal method validation before proceeding to case–control analyses.

2. **Interpretive framework for dapivirine hair levels:** Without a directly observed ring-use calibration study, the proposal should articulate how dapivirine hair concentrations will be interpreted. The planned comparisons with plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report can establish convergent validity, but the limits of each comparator should be explicitly discussed. A sensitivity analysis assessing how conclusions would change under different assumptions about the hair–dose relationship should be planned.

3. **Power and precision for secondary analyses:** The HIV outcome analyses in Aims 1 and 2 have limited power except for large effects. This should be explicitly acknowledged, and the proposal should commit to reporting confidence intervals rather than relying on significance testing alone. The descriptive and correlational analyses (adherence patterns, measure comparisons) are adequately powered and should be presented as the primary knowledge contributions, with outcome analyses framed as important but exploratory.

4. **Segmental analysis assumptions:** The proposal should discuss the impact of inter-individual hair growth rate variability on the interpretation of 0.25 cm segments as weekly exposure windows and consider sensitivity analyses using alternative growth-rate assumptions.

These conditions are addressable within the existing design and do not require fundamental redesign. If met, the project would provide a valuable descriptive and methodological contribution to HIV prevention and treatment monitoring across delivery platforms.
