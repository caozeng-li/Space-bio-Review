# Scientific Project Review

## Proposal: Developing and Testing Models of the Auditory System with and without Hearing Loss

**Principal Investigator:** Laurel H. Carney  
**Mechanism:** R01  
**Review date:** 28 October 2026  

---

## Overall Assessment

This proposal advances a mechanistic account of auditory masking and spectral coding grounded in neural fluctuations—slow modulations of instantaneous auditory-nerve firing rate shaped by peripheral nonlinearities—that are transformed by amplitude-modulation-sensitive midbrain neurons into population-rate codes. The work addresses a recognized limitation of energy-based and envelope-power-spectrum models: their inability to explain tone-in-noise detection when overall level is roved or equalized. The proposal is ambitious in its three-way integration of computational modeling, human psychophysics, and awake-rabbit inferior-colliculus (IC) electrophysiology, and it targets a genuinely important question—how sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) changes the cues listeners use, with potential implications for hearing-device strategies that go beyond audibility restoration.

The project's principal strengths are a clear theoretical framework developed over more than a decade of the PI's programmatic work; a strong track record of published model–physiology–behavior comparisons (Maxwell et al., 2020, JASA; Leong et al., 2020, Ear and Hearing; Fan et al., 2021, Hearing Research; Wang et al., 2021, J. Neurosci.); and the innovative ambition to build individualized, listener-specific models rather than pooling subjects by audiometric category. The extension from simultaneous notched-noise masking to forward masking and to natural spectral slopes in pinna cues and fricatives represents a meaningful widening of scope.

The weaknesses are structural rather than theoretical. The proposal attempts to cover three broad aims—tonal detection cue characterization, simultaneous and forward masking, and pinna/fricative spectral coding—within a design whose sample size and measurement plan for the central individualized-modeling objective are underspecified. The psychophysical timeline appears implausibly compressed for the stated throughput. Critical decisions about which model features are fixed versus fitted, and about how the individualized model will be validated against independent data, receive less attention than the theoretical argument. The forward-masking aim, in particular, sketches a large agenda of published-paradigm replication and new psychophysics without clearly prioritising the most diagnostic comparisons vis-à-vis a fluctuation-based account. The pinna-cue aim appropriately describes itself as laying a basis for subsequent binaural work in SNHL, which is a legitimate stage, but the relationship between the proposed IC recordings and the human behavioural predictions for elevation perception remains indirect.

On balance, the project pursues a scientifically valuable and conceptually innovative direction. The current plan provides a sufficient route for several components—particularly the Aim 1 cue characterization and the model-based reanalysis of published notched-noise datasets—but requires clarification and re-scoping of the forward-masking and individualized-modeling plans before the full programme can be assessed as adequately supported.

**Overall recommendation:** Conditional support, subject to specific factual premises regarding sample size adequacy, the timeline for individualized model construction and validation, and the prioritisation of diagnostic forward-masking comparisons.

## Scientific Value: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The scientific value of this project rests on two distinct contributions: a **conceptual** revision of what auditory-filter estimates from notched-noise masking actually measure, and a **practical** route toward individualized models of hearing that could inform hearing-device fitting beyond the audiogram.

The conceptual contribution is substantial. The standard interpretation of notched-noise masking thresholds treats them as revealing the bandwidth of peripheral auditory filters (Glasberg & Moore, 1990). The PI's group has shown that a single physiological model depending on neural-fluctuation cues, not energy at the filter output, can simultaneously account for fixed-level notched-noise thresholds, roving-level notched-noise thresholds, and profile-analysis results (Maxwell et al., 2020, JASA). The Maxwell et al. (2022) extension further demonstrated that the same fluctuation-based account reproduces the increase of apparent filter bandwidth with frequency and level, and filter asymmetry—features previously interpreted as properties of the cochlear filter itself. These are peer-reviewed, publicly available findings. They do not merely challenge one experimental paradigm; they raise the possibility that a whole class of psychoacoustic "filter" estimates reflects subcortical population processing rather than basilar-membrane mechanics alone. This is a revision with consequences for how hearing impairment is characterised, because SNHL changes the nonlinearities that shape neural fluctuations (cochlear amplification, saturation, synchrony capture), not just audibility.

The practical contribution—individualized models predicting complex-task performance from simple psychophysical characterization—is attractive but remains prospective. The preliminary evidence (Leong et al., 2020) demonstrates that listeners with SNHL show larger rove effects, consistent with a shift from envelope to energy cues, and that this shift can be frequency-dependent. The small pilot of five listeners, with four showing frequency-dependent cue use, provides proof of concept but limited statistical anchoring. The proposal correctly identifies that between-listener variability motivates direct characterization. The scientific value of establishing whether individualized cue-use profiles predict consonant identification or notched-noise thresholds would be high, but that value is contingent on the study having adequate power to detect these relationships—an issue discussed under Rigor.

The proposal's descriptive aim for the pinna-cue work is more modest: it seeks to test whether peripheral fluctuation profiles represent spectral slopes and whether interaural fluctuation differences code elevation, explicitly describing itself as "laying a basis for subsequent studies." This is an appropriate stage for exploratory work, and the value is proportionate.

The question—how SNHL changes the cues listeners use for detection and spectral coding—is important both for basic auditory neuroscience and for translational audiology. The attainable knowledge would be a mechanistic, physiologically grounded alternative to the power-spectrum model, with explicit predictions about individual differences. That is a good contribution, though not at the level of a paradigm shift until the individualized predictions are convincingly validated across tasks.

## Innovation: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The project's innovation lies in three elements: (a) the neural-fluctuation mechanism itself as an alternative to energy-based accounts of masking, (b) the extension to forward masking and natural spectral slopes, and (c) the individual-listener modeling approach.

The neural-fluctuation account is the PI's own line of work, developed since at least Davidson et al. (2009). It is distinct from the influential envelope-power-spectrum model of Dau et al. (1996), which operates on the acoustic envelope after peripheral filtering. The Carney account emphasises that peripheral nonlinearities—particularly inner-hair-cell saturation and synaptic adaptation—transform the acoustic envelope into a neural fluctuation representation that can differ substantially in magnitude and even direction from the acoustic envelope. The proposal's emphasis on instantaneous-rate fluctuations (not the acoustic envelope) and on the population code across modulation-tuned IC neurons is a specific, testable mechanistic claim. This is not a routine extension of existing models; it is a competing framework.

The extension to forward masking is the most novel component within this proposal. The proposal argues that different filter-bandwidth estimates from simultaneous and forward masking might be unified within one fluctuation-based account, without invoking separate temporal-processing mechanisms. This is an interesting conjecture, and the published literature on forward-masking filter estimates (Oxenham & Plack; Oxenham & Shera; Lopez-Poveda & Eustaquio-Martin, 2013) has indeed documented discrepancies with simultaneous-masking estimates that remain debated. However, the proposal's mechanism for forward masking is sketched rather than specified: the idea that post-masker fluctuations interact with modulation tuning is plausible, but the proposal does not articulate a quantitative forward-masking model at the level of detail provided for simultaneous masking. This makes the innovation claim for Aim 2b more aspirational than demonstrated. Distinguishing this from a genuine theoretical advance will depend on execution.

The individual-listener approach is innovative for this field. Most hearing-loss research pools listeners by audiometric category and reports group means. The proposal's insistence on within-listener prediction across tasks, with models constrained by audiometry, DPOAEs, and behavioural cue estimates rather than fitted to each task, is methodologically progressive. The Maxwell et al. (2020) model was not fitted to human thresholds yet reproduced their trends—this gives credibility to the cross-task prediction strategy.

The pinna-cue and fricative extension is less innovative per se—spectral-slope coding has been studied extensively in both the sound-localization and speech-perception literatures—but applying the fluctuation framework to natural stimuli with steep spectral slopes is a logical and worthwhile extension of the core idea.

## Rigor: C (Moderate) — Confidence: B (High)

The proposal's core computational and physiological methods are well-specified and grounded in established practice. The Zilany et al. (2014) AN model is a standard tool; the IC model (same-frequency inhibition–excitation with band-enhanced, band-suppressed and hybrid MTFs) has been tested against physiological data (Fan et al., 2018, Acta Acustica; Fan et al., 2021, Hearing Research); the signal-detection framework (response derivatives normalized by variability, combined across populations) is appropriate. The use of trends across stimulus parameters rather than single-threshold fitting as a model-rejection criterion is a methodological strength.

Several aspects of the design, however, raise concerns about whether the intended inferences can be supported.

**Sample size and individual-differences analysis.** The proposal states 15 control and 30 SNHL listeners (with a secondary figure of 40 total). For Aim 1, the characterization of individual cue-use profiles is descriptive and the sample is adequate for estimating central tendencies and variability. For the critical Aim 2 prediction—whether individual cue use from Aim 1 predicts notched-noise thresholds or consonant identification—the analysis is inherently correlational. With 30 SNHL listeners, a correlation of approximately r = 0.5 would be detectable at 80% power (two-tailed α = 0.05). Weaker but still meaningful relationships (r ≈ 0.3–0.4) would be underpowered. The proposal does not present a power analysis or specify the minimum effect size considered scientifically meaningful. This matters because the central claim—that individualized models outperform audiogram-based predictions—requires demonstrating reliable individual-level prediction. Without adequate power, null results would be uninformative rather than evidence against the model.

**Psychophysical timeline.** The proposal estimates "no more than three hours" for the Aim 1 characterization (five frequencies × two levels × two roving conditions × four repeats, with quality-control repetition). Even with efficient interleaving, this is optimistic. The Leong et al. (2020) study, using a similar paradigm at four frequencies and fewer level conditions, required substantial testing time per participant. The 12–15 hours total per listener across all aims may also be tight if Aim 2 and Aim 3 testing are added. If testing is curtailed or fewer conditions are collected, the individualized model constraints will be weaker.

**Model specification for individualization.** The proposal states that inner- and outer-hair-cell parameters will be adjusted using audiometry and DPOAEs, but does not specify the mapping from these measures to model parameters. Audiometric threshold alone underdetermines the split between inner- and outer-hair-cell dysfunction, and DPOAEs provide information about outer-hair-cell function primarily at moderate levels. The proposal does not discuss identifiability: can the available measures uniquely constrain the model parameters, or will multiple parameter sets produce equally good fits to the Aim 1 data? The cross-task prediction is the strongest test, but if the model is underdetermined at the characterization stage, a failure to predict Aim 2 results could reflect underconstrained parameters rather than a flawed mechanism.

**Forward-masking design.** Aim 2b proposes to cover published results "across level, frequency and delay; roving-level and additivity paradigms; high-level off-frequency maskers; and notched-noise forward masking"—a very large parameter space. The proposal does not prioritise which of these published findings are most diagnostic for distinguishing the fluctuation account from temporal-processing alternatives. The IC recording plan for forward masking will relate responses to each neuron's modulation tuning, but the proposal does not specify how many forward-masking conditions can be tested per neuron given the limited holding time. The example manipulation—adding masker modulation predicted to raise thresholds—is a reasonable mechanistic test but only one of many possible comparisons.

**Physiological sampling.** The proposal aims for approximately 200 neurons per stimulus condition, acknowledging the need to sample varied tuning. This is a reasonable target given four rabbits recorded over 12–24 months each. However, the proposal does not discuss how neurons will be sampled with respect to the parameter space (frequency, MTF type, binaural properties) or how missing cells in the sampling grid will be handled. The model predictions depend on population responses; undersampling of particular MTF classes or frequency regions could bias the comparison.

**Pinna-cue and fricative validation.** The analysis plan for Aim 3 is less developed than for Aims 1–2. The proposal will compare model predictions with published consonant-confusion matrices and conduct new consonant-identification testing, but the mapping from IC population responses to predicted confusion patterns is not specified. The relationship between IC fluctuation sensitivity and elevation perception is indirect: the proposal records neural sensitivity to interaural fluctuation differences but does not collect behavioural elevation judgments in rabbits or humans within this project. This limits the strength of inference from the physiological data.

These are not fatal weaknesses—exploratory and descriptive aims can tolerate more uncertainty—but they reduce confidence that the full set of stated inferences, particularly the individualized cross-task predictions, can be delivered as described.

## Feasibility: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

The core activities are within the PI's demonstrated capabilities. The laboratory has the necessary equipment, infrastructure, computational resources, and consultant expertise. The PI has an established track record of integrated modeling–physiology–psychophysics work using the same species (Dutch-belted rabbit), the same recording preparation (awake, tetrode), and closely related stimuli.

**Modeling and computation.** The computational components use established models (Zilany et al., 2014) and modeling strategies already prototyped in Maxwell et al. (2020, 2022). Access to university high-performance computing is stated. This component is low-risk.

**Physiology.** The awake-rabbit tetrode preparation is operational in the PI's laboratory, with published results using comparable stimuli (Fan et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2021). Probe-tube calibration, binaural delivery through custom earmolds, and the characterization battery (frequency tuning, response map, MTF, ITD/ILD sensitivity) follow procedures used in prior publications. The passive-listening task is straightforward. The main feasibility risk is yield: sampling 200 neurons per condition across the required stimulus space within the planned animal numbers and recording duration. This is achievable but demanding.

**Psychophysics.** The adaptive tracking procedures and notched-noise paradigms are standard. The laboratory has sound-attenuated booths and appropriate equipment. The critical feasibility constraint is participant throughput. With 45 listeners (15 control + 30 SNHL), each requiring 12–15 hours, the total testing burden is approximately 540–675 participant-hours. Over five years, with parallel testing of multiple participants, this is feasible, but the compressed timeline for Aim 1 characterization (three hours per listener across five frequencies and two levels) may require adjustment or acceptance of fewer conditions.

**Recruitment.** The SNHL criteria (thresholds exceeding two of 26 dB at 2 kHz, 30 dB at 3 kHz, or 35 dB at 4 kHz, but ≤60 dB HL from 250–8000 Hz) define a mild-to-moderate sloping high-frequency loss—a common clinical profile. Recruitment of 30 such listeners plus 15 age-matched controls appears feasible at a major university centre, though the between-ear symmetry requirement (≤15 dB difference) will reduce the eligible pool.

**Critical dependencies.** The individualized-modeling pipeline has a critical dependency on the adequacy of the audiogram + DPOAE + behavioural cue estimate for constraining model parameters. If additional measures prove necessary to disambiguate inner- versus outer-hair-cell contributions, the characterization protocol would need revision. The cross-task prediction strategy is a further dependency: if Aim 1 models fail to predict Aim 2 thresholds, the project can still contribute descriptive data, but the central theoretical claim would be weakened.

## Ethics and Safety: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

**Human participants.** The proposal describes institutional review procedures, written consent, sound-level safeguards, frequent breaks, and anonymised reporting. These are appropriate and proportionate. The exclusion criteria and screening battery (audiometry, bone conduction, tympanograms, DPOAEs, QuickSIN) are clinically standard. Testing at up to 100 dB SPL is within safe exposure limits for the brief stimulus durations typical of adaptive psychophysical procedures, and the explicit ceiling mitigates risk of overexposure.

**Animal subjects.** The procedures described—veterinary supervision, anaesthesia for surgery, postoperative analgesia, gradual habituation to the recording chair, health and behaviour monitoring, and optional histological verification—follow accepted standards for chronic electrophysiological recording in rabbits. The passive-listening task involves no aversive stimulation or behavioural training. The animal justification anticipates four rabbits used over 12–24 months each, which is within normal limits for chronic tetrode preparations.

**Unspecified items.** The proposal does not state whether institutional animal care and use committee (IACUC) and institutional review board (IRB) approvals have been obtained or are pending. For a prospective review, this is a procedural note rather than an ethical concern—approval would be required before work begins but the absence of documentation in the supplied summary does not indicate non-compliance.

**Data and code sharing.** The commitment to share models and data through the laboratory website, with MATLAB/C code, compiler instructions, readme files and executable examples, is good practice. The proposal does not specify a data repository with persistent identifiers, which would be stronger, but the stated plan is adequate.

## Management: B (Good) — Confidence: C (Moderate)

The five-year timeline and the integration of modeling, physiology, and psychophysics within each aim are sensible in principle. The PI is an established investigator whose laboratory has produced the preliminary work underpinning this proposal. Consultants covering audiology, hearing-loss psychophysics, phonetics, and theoretical psychoacoustics fill expertise gaps appropriately.

Several aspects of the management plan are, however, underspecified:

**Coordination across aims.** The proposal states that all human participants will complete Aim 1 characterization before participating in Aim 2 or Aim 3, and that individualized models from Aim 1 will predict Aim 2 and Aim 3 results. This creates a sequential dependency: Aim 2 psychophysics cannot begin until Aim 1 models are built, and the models cannot be built until sufficient Aim 1 data are collected and analyzed. The proposal does not describe the workflow for model construction—whether it will be automated or require investigator judgment per listener—or provide a timeline showing when the transition from characterization to prediction testing occurs. If model construction is labour-intensive, the pipeline could become a bottleneck.

**Staffing and responsibilities.** The proposal text available does not detail staffing (postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, research assistants) or assign responsibility for specific components. The PI's track record suggests capacity to manage integrated projects, but the absence of explicit role assignments limits confidence that the parallel streams (physiology and psychophysics for three aims) can be sustained simultaneously.

**Go/no-go decision points.** The proposal does not identify decision points where course corrections would be made—for example, if the individualized models from Aim 1 do not adequately predict Aim 2 notched-noise thresholds, does the forward-masking work proceed as planned or is it re-scoped? Such contingencies are relevant given the sequential dependency structure.

**Schedule detail.** The five-year span is stated but not broken down by aim, year, or milestone. The management narrative is appropriately concise for a scientific proposal but does not permit confident assessment of whether the workload can be completed within the stated period.

These gaps reduce confidence in the management judgement to moderate, while the overall structure and PI track record support a good rating. The recommendation condition regarding clarified timeline and prioritisation addresses this criterion.

## Recommendation: Conditional Support

The project pursues a scientifically important and conceptually distinctive line of investigation with a strong theoretical foundation and demonstrated feasibility of its core methods. The individualized-modeling objective is innovative and, if successful, would provide a framework for personalised hearing assessment that goes beyond the audiogram.

Support is conditional on three premises whose resolution would substantially affect the likelihood that the full set of stated aims can be delivered:

1. **Sample size and power.** That the proposed sample (30 SNHL, 15 control) provides adequate statistical power for the correlation between individual cue-use profiles and complex-task performance that is central to Aim 2, or that the project accepts a more descriptive level of inference for the individualized predictions if power proves insufficient.

2. **Timeline and model-construction workflow.** That the Aim 1 characterization can be completed within the proposed testing duration per listener, and that a feasible workflow exists for constructing individualized models in time to inform the design of Aim 2 and Aim 3 psychophysical experiments.

3. **Diagnostic prioritisation in forward masking.** That the forward-masking component is focused on a manageable set of conditions selected for their capacity to distinguish the fluctuation-based account from temporal-processing alternatives, rather than attempting comprehensive coverage of the published forward-masking literature.

These are not redesign requirements; they are clarifications of how the current plan maps onto the stated inferences. With these premises addressed, the project would merit full support as a coherent, innovative programme likely to advance understanding of auditory processing with and without hearing loss.
