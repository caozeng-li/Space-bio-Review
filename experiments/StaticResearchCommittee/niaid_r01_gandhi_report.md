# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project will evaluate antiretroviral concentrations in hair as cumulative exposure, adherence, and pharmacokinetic biomarkers across three HIV prevention and treatment delivery methods: oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC) PrEP in East African communities, the dapivirine vaginal ring among women in four African countries, and long-acting injectable cabotegravir in a treatment population with prior adherence difficulties. Hair measurements will be paired with plasma, self-report, and residual ring drug in a unified analytical framework to characterize exposure patterns—such as recent versus sustained use—and to estimate drug levels associated with HIV protection, renal safety, and virologic suppression [1]. The most valuable expected knowledge includes exposure thresholds for oral PrEP effectiveness and renal outcomes, the first systematic evaluation of hair as a cumulative measure for the vaginal ring, and segmental pharmacokinetic monitoring for the injectable, together building an integrated approach that spans delivery modalities and clinically relevant subgroups [1].

Key strengths include a validated and published TFV/FTC hair assay with an established dose–response calibration supporting Aim 1, high sample-collection acceptability demonstrated in the same settings, an efficient case–control sampling design with appropriate weighting, and a strong investigative team with direct access to the three well-characterized parent studies. For Aim 3, a validated cabotegravir hair assay and a published clinical case report provide a credible, if early, measurement basis. The principal limitation is that Aim 2 depends on a dapivirine hair assay documented only at the spiked-hair stage, without published validation in clinical samples; the interpretation of hair dapivirine levels will therefore remain partly inferential until calibration data are generated. Aims 2 and 3 also lack the dose–response calibration that Aim 1 enjoys, which tempers the definitiveness of their exposure–outcome conclusions. The outcome analyses are powered to detect relatively large exposure contrasts, limiting the precision of threshold estimation. The ethical and safety arrangements are proportionate and largely in place, with only the A5359 protocol still under establishment.

The proposal brings together a mature oral-PrEP hair monitoring platform with two nascent but promising applications for ring and injectable delivery. The integrated hypothesis and the access to three high-quality parent studies create an unusually informative comparison that could shape how adherence is measured in real-world PrEP programs. The trade-off is clear: Aim 1 is ready to deliver, while Aims 2 and 3 are best understood as measurement-development exercises that will generate essential calibration data rather than definitive monitoring tools. On balance, the achievable contributions—effectiveness-linked thresholds for oral PrEP, the first systematic hair dapivirine data, and a pharmacokinetic foundation for injectable cabotegravir—justify the investment, provided the assay development gap for dapivirine is closed before clinical samples are committed. Support is conditional on confirming a validated dapivirine hair assay with documented performance characteristics in clinical hair samples before HOPE clinical specimens are analyzed.

**Overall conclusion: Conditional support**

## Scientific value

**The proposal addresses a question of clear importance for HIV prevention and treatment delivery. The attainable knowledge is strongest for Aim 1 and for the integrative comparison across modalities; Aims 2 and 3 provide valuable but earlier-stage measurement-development contributions.**

The central scientific contribution is an integrated evaluation of hair antiretroviral concentrations as cumulative exposure biomarkers paired with shorter-term measures—plasma, self-report, and, for the dapivirine ring, residual drug in returned rings—across three delivery methods and three distinct clinical populations [1]. This pairing is designed to distinguish adherence patterns that no single measure can resolve: sustained use, recent dosing before a clinic visit, and intermittent earlier exposure [1]. The proposal explicitly hypothesizes that combining long-term and short-term measures will better describe exposure patterns and predict outcomes than any single snapshot [1], a framing that generates specific, testable predictions about what combinations of hair, plasma, and residual ring drug levels should be observed under different use scenarios [1]. No published study has systematically evaluated these pattern hypotheses across oral, ring, and injectable modalities, making the integrative design itself a meaningful knowledge product.

**Aim 1 is positioned to deliver the most definitive evidence.** The foundation is a validated dose–response calibration demonstrating a strong log-linear relationship between directly observed dosing frequency and hair TFV concentrations, with minimal inter-individual variability in dose effect [2]. A hair TFV concentration of 0.021 ng/mg reliably distinguished 2 from 7 doses/week with no overlap [2]. Building on this calibration, Aim 1 will implement a formal nested case–control design with three randomly sampled controls per seroconverter, using weighted pooled logistic regression to relate hair TFV/FTC levels quantitatively to HIV acquisition while controlling for risk factors [1]. A parallel analysis using certainty sampling of participants with substantial renal decline will assess the relationship between hair TFV exposure and renal-function changes [1]. This shifts the group's prior work—which used hair TFV descriptively in 25 SEARCH seroconverters [3]—to a formal inferential framework linking hair concentrations to two clinically important outcomes. The knowledge to be gained includes the first population-based estimates of hair TFV/FTC levels associated with HIV protection and renal safety in a real-world African PrEP delivery setting, and comparative performance data for hair versus plasma and self-report as co-predictors of seroconversion.

**Aim 2 addresses an explicitly identified gap.** A 2016 review by Stalter and colleagues concluded that "the assessment of drug uptake with vaginal drug delivery is needed" for hair monitoring [4]. No published study has subsequently evaluated hair dapivirine as a cumulative ring-exposure measure. The proposal will generate the first systematic data on whether hair dapivirine concentrations correlate with plasma dapivirine, residual ring drug, and self-reported use, and will provide the first estimates of hair dapivirine levels associated with lower HIV acquisition risk [1]. The value of Aim 2 lies primarily in generating these first calibration data, since no dose–response relationship between ring use and hair dapivirine has been established. The HIV outcome analysis can detect a relative risk of 2.9 comparing lowest to upper quartiles of hair exposure [1], which is sufficient to identify strong associations if they exist but cannot resolve more modest exposure–outcome gradients.

**Aim 3 extends hair monitoring to a delivery method where pharmacokinetic variability is clinically relevant.** Injectable concentrations may fall near or beyond the scheduled dosing interval. A single published case report demonstrated proof of principle that segmental hair cabotegravir analysis can confirm exposure retrospectively [5]. Aim 3 will provide the first systematic, prospective characterization of hair–plasma cabotegravir correlations in approximately 150 participants, with 0.25 cm segmental analysis interpreted as approximately weekly exposure windows [1]. The proposal will also generate exploratory estimates of the relationship between hair cabotegravir and virologic suppression [1]. The value is in establishing pharmacokinetic feasibility and providing hair-to-plasma correlation estimates (expected 95% CI 0.73–0.85 around a single-time-point correlation of 0.8 [1]) that would inform monitoring strategies for future injectable prevention studies.

**The capacity-building component**—working with African laboratories associated with SEARCH and MTN-025 in the final project year [1]—adds practical value by beginning to establish local capability for this monitoring approach in high-incidence settings. Blinded hair-concentration datasets will be made publicly available after planned analyses [1], supporting secondary analyses and assay development by other groups.

## Innovation

**The proposal extends hair antiretroviral monitoring substantially beyond published work. The highest originality is in Aims 2 and 3 and in the integrative pattern analysis across delivery modalities; Aim 1 provides a meaningful but more incremental advance over the group's prior descriptive work.**

**Aim 2 is the most original component**, directly addressing a stated gap in the literature. The 2016 review by Stalter and colleagues explicitly identified drug measurement in hair from vaginal ring users as an unmet need [4], and no published study has subsequently filled this gap. The preliminary extracted-ion chromatograms for dapivirine spiked into hair [1] indicate unpublished method development at the UCSF Hair Analytical Laboratory. The proposal would therefore generate the first systematic evaluation of hair dapivirine as a cumulative exposure measure for a vaginally delivered antiretroviral, the first comparison of hair dapivirine with plasma dapivirine, residual ring drug, and self-report in the same participants, and the first estimates of hair dapivirine concentrations associated with lower HIV acquisition risk. This is a genuine methodological and evidentiary advance rather than an extension of existing measurement tools.

**Aim 3 addresses a question for which only a single case report exists.** The Koss 2024 report demonstrated segmental hair cabotegravir analysis in one individual who seroconverted on CAB-LA, confirming cabotegravir exposure retrospectively and showing proof of principle that hair segments can reflect different exposure time windows (0.190 ng/mg versus 0.950 ng/mg in adjacent segments [5]) [5]. The proposal would extend this from a single forensic application to a systematic pharmacokinetic monitoring study, correlating hair concentrations with trough plasma levels, characterizing segmental changes between monthly injections, and distinguishing trajectories of consistently low exposure from rapid decline after initially adequate exposure [1]. The refinement of 0.25 cm segmentation interpreted as approximately weekly exposure windows is beyond anything yet reported in the peer-reviewed literature for injectable cabotegravir. While a validated cabotegravir hair assay with published performance characteristics (LLOQ 0.00625 ng/mg, linear range 0.00625–3.20 ng/mg) exists from the same laboratory [5], its application to systematic pharmacokinetic monitoring with segmental analysis in a prospective cohort is novel.

**The integrative analytical framework constitutes a further original contribution.** Published work has described moderate correlations between hair and plasma or electronic monitoring (r = 0.29–0.61) and noted their complementary character [6], but no study has systematically tested pattern hypotheses across oral, ring, and injectable modalities in a unified design. The proposed comparisons of hair, plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report within the same participants for each delivery method create an unusually informative dataset for understanding what each measure captures and how they might be combined in real-world monitoring programs.

**Aim 1 provides a meaningful but incremental advance.** Gandhi's group has already published the core dose–response calibration [2], used hair TFV descriptively in 25 SEARCH seroconverters [3], demonstrated hair–DBS concordance (r = 0.76 [7]), and shown a monotonic association between hair TFV levels and creatinine clearance decline [8]. The originality of Aim 1 lies in moving from this descriptive foundation to a formal weighted case–control design with sampling-fraction weighting, pooled logistic regression linking time-varying hair levels to seroconversion, and a parallel certainty-sampled renal analysis—all within a large community-based African PrEP delivery setting [1]. The shift from qualitative exposure characterization in a small number of seroconverters to a population-based inferential framework with clinical outcomes is a substantive methodological advance, even though the underlying measurement platform is established. The capacity-building plan to work with African laboratories in the final project year [1] adds a further implementation-oriented innovation by beginning to transfer the technology to settings where it could have the greatest public health impact.

## Rigor

**The proposal is well-designed for its stated aims, with a mature measurement foundation for oral PrEP and appropriate analytical strategies across all three aims. The principal limitation is the absence of published dose–response calibration for hair dapivirine and cabotegravir, which constrains the definitiveness of the exposure–outcome conclusions for Aims 2 and 3.**

### Aim 1: Strong measurement and statistical foundation

The TFV/FTC hair assay is fully validated with published performance parameters: LLOQ of 0.00200 ng/mg for TFV and 0.0200 ng/mg for FTC, intra-day CV 5.4–12.6% and inter-day CV 6.4–13.5% for TFV [9]. The directly observed dosing calibration study established a log-linear dose–hair relationship with an estimated 76% increase in hair TFV per two-fold dose increase (95% CI 60–93%), with estimated random effect variance for the dose effect of zero, indicating consistent dose effects across individuals [2]. Between-subject coefficients of variation were 25–34% across dosing levels [2]. This calibration provides a legitimate basis for interpreting hair TFV/FTC levels as approximate cumulative exposure measures in a real-world PrEP setting.

The nested case–control design is statistically sound. Rather than assaying all approximately 83,200 potential specimens, the proposal selects all seroconverters and three randomly sampled controls per case, yielding approximately 5,200 person-visits for the HIV outcome analysis, with an additional approximately 1,300 visits from participants with substantial renal decline for the renal analysis [1]. The use of known sampling fractions with outcome-enriched groups and weighting preserves population inference within a single analytical framework [1]. The discrete-time Cox approach implemented through weighted pooled logistic regression appropriately handles time-varying hair levels and repeated observations [1]. The power calculation—80% for an odds ratio of ≥2.1 comparing the lowest hair-exposure quartile with the upper three—is conservative, since it treats only one observation per person while the repeated-measures design provides additional information [1].

The renal analysis plan addresses confounding through adjustment for age, sex, baseline clearance, hypertension, diabetes, concomitant drugs, and site, and uses the directly observed dosing data to inform interpretation of TFV hair levels [1]. Covariates are extensive and clinically relevant, including demographics, region, marital and polygamy status, occupation, education, alcohol use, circumcision, stigma, STIs, delivery setting, and self-perceived HIV risk [1]. Values below the limit of quantification are handled by setting undetectable TFV/FTC to the detection limit with an offset before log transformation [1], a standard approach that avoids discarding low-exposure information.

### Aims 2 and 3: Measurement constraints limit inferential strength

The three aims differ considerably in measurement readiness, as summarized below.

| Aspect | Aim 1 (TDF/FTC) | Aim 2 (Dapivirine ring) | Aim 3 (Cabotegravir) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Assay validation | Published, full | Spiked-hair only | Published, partial |
| Dose–response calibration | Established (DOT study) | Absent | Absent (hair-to-plasma ratio assumed) |
| Outcome analysis type | Confirmatory (effectiveness, renal) | Exploratory calibration | Exploratory (PK correlation) |
| Power for primary contrast | OR ≥2.1, quartile comparison | RR ≥2.9, quartile comparison | Correlation 0.8, 95% CI 0.73–0.85 |

For Aim 2, the proposal shows extracted-ion chromatograms for dapivirine spiked into hair [1] but does not reference a published validated method for dapivirine in clinical hair samples. Without a dose–response calibration study establishing how hair dapivirine concentrations correspond to ring use duration, patterns of ring removal, or systemic exposure, the interpretation of hair dapivirine levels will remain partly inferential. The comparisons with plasma dapivirine and residual ring drug provide an internal reference but cannot substitute for a calibration that links a known exposure condition to the resulting hair concentration. The power calculation (80% for a relative risk of 2.9 comparing lowest to upper quartiles, based on approximately 60 seroconversions and 240 controls [1]) is adequate for detecting strong associations, but the strength of inference about those associations depends on measurement properties that have not yet been established.

For Aim 3, the situation is more advanced but still incomplete. A validated cabotegravir hair assay exists with LLOQ 0.00625 ng/mg and linear range 0.00625–3.20 ng/mg [5], and a published case report demonstrated segmental analysis in a clinical context [5]. However, the proposal uses a candidate protective threshold of 66 ng/mg hair, corresponding to four times the protein-adjusted IC₉₀ of 0.66 µg/mL in plasma [1]. This implies an assumed hair-to-plasma ratio of approximately 100:1, but the empirical basis for this ratio is not specified. The segmental analysis approach (0.25 cm portions interpreted as approximately weekly exposure [1]) is logically derived from known hair growth rates, but the precision with which hair segments can resolve intramuscular injection pharmacokinetics—including the influence of variable growth rates, hair-cycle phases, and drug partitioning—has not been systematically characterized. The virologic suppression analyses are explicitly described as exploratory random-effects logistic models [1], which is appropriate for this stage and correctly calibrates expectations.

### Design choices are defensible for each aim's goals

The integrative comparisons across delivery modalities—testing whether the same pattern hypotheses hold across oral, ring, and injectable products—remain a scientifically valid goal even with the measurement limitations of Aims 2 and 3, since the internal comparisons of hair with plasma, self-report, and residual ring drug within each aim provide partial validation of the hair measures themselves. The design does not overclaim what Aims 2 and 3 can deliver, though the proposal's description of Aim 2 as evaluating hair dapivirine as a "long-term ring-exposure measure" [1] overstates what can be concluded without prior calibration.

## Feasibility

**The project is broadly implementable, with a mature laboratory and sampling infrastructure for Aim 1, a validated assay and early clinical precedent for Aim 3, and a single material dependency—the dapivirine hair assay for Aim 2—that requires resolution before HOPE clinical samples are committed.**

### Laboratory capability and assay maturity

The UCSF Hair Analytical Laboratory has developed and validated LC–MS/MS methods for multiple antiretrovirals in hair, including tenofovir, emtricitabine, atazanavir, efavirenz, nevirapine, and cabotegravir, as well as anti-tuberculosis drugs such as isoniazid [9] [5] [10] [11]. Successful technology transfer to an independent laboratory in India has been demonstrated, with generally high agreement [12]. This track record provides strong grounds for confidence in the laboratory's ability to execute the proposed analyses.

For Aim 1, the measurement pathway is fully established. The TFV/FTC hair assay has published validation parameters and has already been used operationally within SEARCH: among participants self-reporting adherence at week 24, 66% had hair tenofovir concentrations reflecting at least four doses per week, and 44% reflecting seven doses per week [13]. Collection feasibility is confirmed by high acceptability in the same communities: 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit in the early SEARCH PrEP rollout, and approximately 95% provided samples in a separate SEARCH-community study [1].

For Aim 3, a validated cabotegravir hair assay exists with LLOQ 0.00625 ng/mg and linear range 0.00625–3.20 ng/mg [5]. A published case report from the same group demonstrated that segmental hair analysis can distinguish cabotegravir exposure across different time windows [5], providing clinical proof of principle for the segmental approach described in the proposal [1]. The 150-participant sample with seven sampling timepoints yields approximately 1,050 hair analyses, a volume well within the laboratory's demonstrated throughput.

### The dapivirine assay gap

The material dependency is Aim 2. The proposal shows extracted-ion chromatograms for dapivirine spiked into hair [1] but does not cite a published validated method for dapivirine in clinical hair samples. No published calibration study establishes the relationship between dapivirine ring use and hair concentrations. This is a development gap, not a fundamental obstacle: the same laboratory team (Gerona, Okochi, Kuncze, Louie, Benet) has a track record of developing and validating new hair analyte methods, including atazanavir [10], isoniazid [11], and cabotegravir [5]. The proposal timeframe permits method development concurrent with sample collection, since HOPE visits occur at months 1–12 and laboratory selection after outcome ascertainment can be scheduled later [1]. The efficient sampling strategy—assaying approximately 1,680 samples from 240 women out of approximately 12,600 potential specimens [1]—concentrates resources on informative cases and reduces the cost of any assay re-optimization.

### Operational logistics

The added procedure is minimal: approximately 50–100 strands cut close to the occipital scalp, distal end marked for orientation, enclosed in foil and a labeled bag with desiccant, stored at room temperature [1]. Room-temperature storage and shipping simplify multi-country logistics substantially relative to cold-chain requirements. Staff training for different hairstyles is described, and monthly data-quality review of collection completeness and rates is planned [1]. The total assay volume across all three aims is approximately 9,000–10,000 hair analyses, consistent with a specialized analytical laboratory's throughput over a multi-year project. The schedule alignment—SEARCH follow-up spanning approximately three years, HOPE ring use one year plus exit, and the A5359 regimen through week 76 [1]—is compatible with a typical funding period. The capacity-building work with African laboratories in the final project year [1] is appropriately positioned as a longer-term investment rather than a dependency for the primary aims.

## Ethics and safety

**The ethical and safety arrangements are proportionate to the low-risk nature of the added procedure, with established approvals and monitoring for two of the three parent studies and only minor administrative steps remaining for the third.**

**The procedure and its risks are appropriately characterized.** Hair collection requires approximately 90 seconds and involves cutting roughly 50–100 strands from the occipital scalp beneath the upper hair layer [1]. The proposal acknowledges the material risks explicitly: minor accidental scalp injury, confidentiality concerns, and inconvenience [1]. These risks are minimal by any reasonable standard and are comparable to those of routine clinical procedures such as capillary blood sampling. The acknowledgement that completely bald scalps cannot provide the planned specimen, and that axillary or pubic hair will not substitute [1], represents a minor and correctly identified exclusion rather than an ethical concern—it is simply a biological constraint on the measurement method.

**The ethical infrastructure is largely in place.** SEARCH and HOPE have parent-study ethics approvals and monitoring boards [1], providing independent oversight of the research contexts into which hair collection is introduced. Consent documentation includes hair collection, ensuring that participants are informed of this specific procedure [1]. The SEARCH protocol permits ages 15–17 only with parental co-consent, while HOPE and A5359 involve adults [1], an appropriate differentiation of consent requirements by developmental stage. The one outstanding administrative item is that A5359 ethics arrangements are described as "being established" [1]. This is a procedural step rather than an ethical deficiency: the proposal identifies it transparently, and the timeline permits resolution before hair collection begins in the injectable arm.

**Participant acceptability is directly evidenced in the target settings.** In the early SEARCH PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit, and in a separate SEARCH-community study approximately 95% of participants provided samples [1]. These figures—from the same communities and study infrastructure as the proposed Aim 1—are the most relevant operational evidence that the procedure is feasible and non-coercive in context. Published acceptability data from African PrEP trial settings confirm high acceptance rates (100% at week 8, 96% at week 16 for active-arm participants in one earlier study [14]), though the more contemporary SEARCH data [1] provide the most direct precedent for the current proposal.

**Oversight arrangements are proportionate and pragmatic.** Study coordinators will receive collection training with demonstrations for different hairstyles [1], addressing a practical concern that could otherwise lead to inconsistent or unsuccessful collections. Monthly data-quality review of completeness and collection rates is planned, with retraining when necessary [1]. Adverse events and confidentiality breaches will be reported through study leadership, and trial-network laboratory data systems will support specimen quality tracking [1]. These arrangements establish feedback loops for identifying and correcting problems without imposing disproportionate administrative burden on a low-risk procedure.

**Data handling and dissemination provisions are appropriate.** Blinded hair-concentration datasets will be made publicly available after planned analyses and publications, and findings will be shared with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders [1]. The plan to make data public supports secondary analyses and assay development while the prior commitment to community and policy communication addresses the legitimate interest of participants and host communities. The ethical justification for the work rests on the potential value of the knowledge to be gained: better tools for monitoring PrEP and treatment adherence in populations at high risk of HIV acquisition and transmission. Hair monitoring offers practical advantages for resource-limited settings—collection requires no phlebotomy, and storage and shipping can occur at room temperature [1]—that could make objective adherence assessment more feasible in programs serving the very communities from which the study participants are drawn.

## Management

**The investigative team is well-constituted and brings together the necessary expertise across domains, with direct access to the three parent studies. The principal management gaps are the absence of an explicit timeline for dapivirine assay validation and resolution of A5359 protocol arrangements, both of which are manageable with standard project oversight.**

**The team composition maps expertise efficiently onto each aim.** Monica Gandhi leads an established hair-analytical program at UCSF with an extensive publication record on hair antiretroviral monitoring spanning oral tenofovir-based PrEP, antiretroviral treatment, and anti-tuberculosis therapy [9] [11] [8] [3]. This core laboratory capability—validated LC–MS/MS methods for multiple analytes, demonstrated technology transfer to an independent laboratory in India [12], and operational experience within the SEARCH study [13]—provides the essential technical foundation for all three aims. Diane Havlir and the SEARCH team provide direct access to the East African community-based PrEP cohort that underpins Aim 1, including the established specimen collection infrastructure, the randomized community design, and the longitudinal outcome ascertainment that makes the nested case–control design possible [1]. Jared Baeten and MTN collaborators supply equivalent access to the HOPE open-label dapivirine ring extension study across four African countries, with its monthly visit schedule and existing hair, plasma, and used-ring collection procedures [1]. Jose Castillo-Mancilla brings expertise on the injectable cabotegravir study (ACTG A5359), a treatment population with prior adherence difficulties that provides a distinctive context for pharmacokinetic monitoring [1]. Leslie Benet and Roy Gerona contribute pharmacokinetic and analytical-chemistry expertise, while Peter Bacchetti provides statistical design and analysis leadership [1].

**The laboratory selection strategy is operationally sophisticated.** Assays are deferred until after outcome ascertainment, with all seroconverters and randomly sampled controls selected for analysis, and known sampling fractions incorporated in weights to preserve population inference [1]. This concentrates resources on informative cases while recovering population-level estimates—an approach validated in the statistical literature on case–cohort and nested case–control designs. The total assay volume, estimated at approximately 9,000–10,000 hair analyses across all three aims, is realistic for a specialized analytical laboratory over a multi-year project.

**Two management items require attention.**

| Resource/arrangement | Status | Implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TFV/FTC assay and SEARCH access | Operational; published and in use [9] [13] | Aim 1 ready to proceed |
| Cabotegravir assay | Validated and published [5] | Aim 3 technically feasible |
| Dapivirine assay | Spiked-hair only [1]; no published clinical validation | Requires milestone-driven development before HOPE samples committed |
| A5359 ethics arrangements | "Being established" [1] | Procedural step; timeline permits resolution |
| Capacity-building | Planned final year [1] | Not a dependency for primary aims |

First, the proposal does not specify a timeline with milestones for dapivirine assay validation—defining acceptance criteria for precision, accuracy, linearity, and extraction recovery in clinical hair samples before committing irreplaceable HOPE specimens. The laboratory's track record [10] [11] [5] provides confidence that the method can be developed, but explicit milestones would strengthen accountability. Second, A5359 ethics arrangements remain under establishment [1]. This is a procedural matter rather than a substantive risk, given that the parent study must obtain ethics approval before enrolling participants, but coordination between the ancillary hair study and the parent protocol should be confirmed before hair collection begins.

**The schedule is well-aligned** with the parent studies: SEARCH follow-up spans approximately three years, HOPE ring use one year plus exit, and the A5359 regimen through week 76 [1]. The capacity-building plan to work with African laboratories in the final project year [1] is appropriately positioned as a longer-term investment that does not create dependencies for the primary aims. Monthly data-quality review of collection completeness and rates with retraining provisions [1] provides routine operational oversight. The team composition, the efficient sampling design, and the established laboratory capability together provide a credible basis for delivering the proposed work.
