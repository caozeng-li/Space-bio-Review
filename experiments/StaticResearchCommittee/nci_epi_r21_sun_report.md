# Project review

## Overall assessment

This R21 proposal aims to develop statistical methods for estimating immune-cell composition from paired gene expression and DNA methylation data of bulk tumor samples, and for using those estimates in prognostic survival models while accounting for deconvolution uncertainty. The project's most valuable expected contributions are a principled likelihood framework that jointly exploits two omics modalities, formal estimation of cell-size and transcriptional-activity factors from cross-modality discrepancies, and a joint survival model that treats immune cell fractions as latent variables shared between the omics and survival components. The work is motivated by the clinical importance of the tumor immune microenvironment and the recognized limitations of current single-modality deconvolution and two-stage analysis pipelines. The intended outputs are open-source R packages and applied analyses of TCGA colon and lung cancers.

The proposal is supported by preliminary TCGA analyses showing expected immune-composition associations with hypermutation status, empirical checks on distributional assumptions using purified cell data, and promising performance of the maximum-likelihood approach in a known-mixture methylation experiment. The investigative team possesses strong, complementary expertise in statistical genomics, methylation analysis, and cancer applications. The principal limitations are: the practical added value of the joint multi-omics likelihood over existing single-modality or consensus methods is not benchmarked against a direct multi-omics comparator; the identifiability of the proposed multiplicative cell-size factors depends on conditions whose robustness is not fully examined; and the parametric survival model chosen for computational efficiency may constrain the generalizability of the prognostic signatures. The two-year schedule is ambitious for delivering fully validated tools for both aims.

Weighing the evidence, the proposal presents a coherent exploratory pathway with genuine scientific value and a generally sound implementation plan, supported by relevant preliminary data and investigator expertise. The principal scientific risk is that the combined likelihood and joint survival model may not demonstrate a large practical advantage over simpler multi-omics combinations or single-modality methods within the constraints of the two-year award. Additionally, the uncertainty regarding the identifiability of cell-size factors and the adequacy of parametric assumptions slightly weakens the rigor criterion. Nevertheless, the project proposes to answer well-posed questions with appropriately scaled deliverables. The identified limitations are characteristic of an exploratory R21 and do not negate the project's core rationale. The attainable knowledge, even if incremental, would meaningfully advance methodological practice in tumor immune microenvironment analysis.

## Scientific value

**Good scientific value: the project targets two genuine methodological gaps in tumor immune microenvironment analysis — joint multi-omics deconvolution and uncertainty-aware survival modeling — and can produce knowledge that, even if incremental, would meaningfully improve current practice.**

The proposal identifies three specific limitations of prevailing deconvolution workflows that its methods directly address. First, blood-derived reference profiles may misrepresent the molecular state of immune cells within the tumor microenvironment; the coordinate-ascent algorithm allows the abundance of tumor samples to refine those references [1]. Second, cell size and transcriptional activity cause RNA proportions to diverge from cell-number proportions [1]; the framework exploits systematic differences between expression-derived and methylation-derived fraction estimates to infer multiplicative correction factors [1]. Third, conventional two-stage analyses treat estimated cell fractions as fixed covariates in survival regression, ignoring estimation uncertainty and potentially biasing associations [1]; the joint model treats fractions as latent variables shared between the omics likelihood and the survival component [1].

Each of these problems is real and consequential. Monaco et al. (2019) demonstrated that mRNA abundance varies substantially across immune cell types and that scaling factors cannot be reliably recovered from RNA-seq data alone without external validation [2]. The literature also documents that measurement error in biomarkers leads to biased regression estimates and distorted diagnostic measures, with joint modeling of longitudinal and survival data recognized as a valid correction strategy [3]. The proposal therefore addresses questions with established practical importance.

The attainable knowledge — whether joint modeling of expression and methylation meaningfully improves immune deconvolution accuracy, and whether treating fractions as latent variables yields better-calibrated prognostic models than the dominant two-stage approach — is worth having. The project's scope as an exploratory R21 is appropriately calibrated: it does not overpromise clinical discovery but aims instead to produce likelihood-based methods, open-source R software, and applied analyses of TCGA colon and lung cancers that establish feasibility and comparative performance [1].

The principal limitation affecting scientific value is that the incremental contribution cannot be fully assessed at the proposal stage. The project will compare the proposed method against expression-based and methylation-based deconvolution methods [1], but these are single-modality comparators. The central scientific question — whether combining modalities in a formal joint likelihood outperforms simpler multi-omics combinations, such as consensus estimates across separate deconvolutions — is not benchmarked against a direct multi-omics comparator. This does not negate the value of the proposed work, but it means the magnitude of practical improvement the joint framework can deliver remains an open empirical question that the project itself must answer. If the formal likelihood proves only marginally better than a simpler consensus approach, the scientific advance would be narrower than the proposal suggests. This uncertainty is characteristic of exploratory methodology research and is appropriately addressed through the planned comparisons with existing single-modality methods; it constrains, but does not eliminate, the contribution.

## Innovation

**Good innovation: the proposal adds genuine, coherent methodological advances — joint likelihood estimation of cell-size factors exploiting cross-modality discrepancies, latent-variable survival modeling that propagates deconvolution uncertainty, and fused-lasso regularization informed by hierarchical cell-type clustering — though the individual components extend rather than transform existing practice.**

The proposal's novelty rests on a specific combination of elements that together address recognized limitations in tumor immune deconvolution. The first innovation is the formal, likelihood-based estimation of multiplicative cell-size and transcriptional-activity factors from the systematic discrepancy between expression-derived and methylation-derived fraction estimates [1]. The rationale is conceptually clean: because DNA content per diploid cell is approximately constant, methylation-derived fractions should better reflect cell-number proportions, and their divergence from RNA-derived fractions provides information about cell-type-specific transcript abundance. This approach differs from the a posteriori mRNA-content renormalization introduced by EPIC (Racle et al., 2017), which applies externally measured mRNA-per-cell values to convert predicted RNA proportions into cell-number estimates and demonstrated that most other methods could also benefit from such a step [4]. The proposal's contribution is to estimate these factors internally within the joint likelihood rather than relying on external measurements, though both approaches share the core conceptual insight that RNA proportions do not equal cell-number proportions.

The second advance is the joint survival model that treats immune cell fractions as latent variables shared between the omics likelihood and the survival component, rather than as fixed point estimates [1]. The two-stage problem — deconvolve first, then regress survival on the estimates — is widely practiced but rarely addressed with formal uncertainty propagation. Recent Bayesian methods such as BayesPrism (Chu et al., 2022) provide posterior distributions for cell fractions [5], and multi-omics factor models such as MOFA and its derivatives organize variation across omics layers [6] [7], but neither directly embeds deconvolution within a survival likelihood. The proposal's approach of multiplying the omics likelihood by survival contributions conditional on the same latent fractions is a natural extension of joint modeling principles to this domain, and its novelty lies in the specific application rather than in any single statistical component.

The third element is the fused-lasso penalty with hierarchical clustering to stabilize survival coefficient estimation across molecularly similar cell types [1]. Using dendrogram ordering from both omics modalities to determine the fusion penalty structure is a sensible application of an established regularization technique, though its dependence on clustering quality is acknowledged by the investigators.

The proposal's originality must be contextualized against prior work. Expression-based deconvolution is a mature field, with CIBERSORT (Newman et al., 2015) demonstrating support-vector regression for closely related hematopoietic subsets [8] and EPIC incorporating tumor-infiltrating reference profiles with mRNA-content renormalization [4]. Methylation-based deconvolution has well-established methods and expanded reference libraries covering 12 leukocyte subtypes [9], with systematic benchmarks indicating robust performance for myeloid cells but limited accuracy for lymphocyte populations [10]. The proposal's contribution is the integration of these two modalities into a single likelihood, an approach that is not unique to this proposal — MOADE (Sun et al., 2025) performs multi-omic deconvolution using a multimodal autoencoder that jointly models RNA and methylation, learns a shared cellular space, and estimates modality-specific cell-size factors [11] — but MOADE uses a fundamentally different deep-learning framework and appeared after this proposal would have been prepared. The proposal's likelihood-based approach offers complementary strengths, including explicit parameter interpretability, formal uncertainty quantification, and the ability to deconvolve new samples with a single modality after training [1].

The innovation is therefore incremental but genuine: the combined framework adds useful capabilities beyond single-modality methods, and the joint survival model represents a worthwhile departure from dominant two-stage practice, even if the parametric survival specification limits generality. The principal constraint on novelty is that several individual advances were partially anticipated (EPIC for mRNA-content renormalization) or are being pursued through alternative frameworks (MOADE for multi-omics integration), so the aggregate contribution depends on the empirical demonstration that the likelihood-based integration and cross-modality correction yield practical gains that simpler alternatives do not achieve.

## Rigor

**Moderate rigor: the likelihood framework is carefully specified and the validation plan is multi-layered, but material concerns remain about the identifiability of the proposed cell-size factors, the adequacy of distributional assumptions under realistic conditions, and the comparison strategy against multi-omics alternatives.**

The proposal's research design has several commendable features that support the intended inferences at an exploratory R21 stage. The likelihood framework is mathematically explicit and coherent, with expression modeled as log-normal and methylation as normal at individual CpGs, mixture moments properly derived for both modalities [1], and the four-source likelihood combining tumor expression, tumor methylation, purified-cell expression, and purified-cell methylation correctly specified [1]. The coordinate-ascent algorithm that alternates between estimating fractions and re-estimating cell-type profiles is a standard and appropriate strategy for latent-variable problems of this structure, allowing the many tumor samples to refine blood-derived references [1]. The compositional log-fraction predictor with a sum-to-zero constraint [1] follows established principles for compositional data, and the use of cross-validated C-index rather than hypothesis testing for evaluating prognostic performance [1] is appropriate for a prediction-focused aim.

The validation plan incorporates the three standard benchmark types used in the field: simulated mixtures with known ground truth, experimental datasets with flow-cytometry-established composition, and published measurements of immune cells isolated from TIME to assess tumor-context expression profiles [1]. This aligns with the comprehensive evaluation framework recently published by Amblard et al. (2025), which tested 20 methods across simulated, in vitro, and in vivo datasets [12].

The preliminary work provides credible evidence that core modeling assumptions are tenable in limited settings. The examination of 9,401 CpGs across five broad cell-type categories supported the normal approximation for within-cell-type methylation distributions [1]. TCGA colon-cancer analyses recovered expected associations between hypermutation status and immune composition, with Wilcoxon p-values of 0.046 for B cells and 0.005 for monocytes [1]. The two-component feature mixture to distinguish informative from noise features [1] is supported by preliminary experiments showing improved robustness under probe perturbation — residual sum of squares increased only from 0.029 to 0.055 with the mixture-based method versus 0.043 to 0.155 for nonnegative least squares [1].

However, several aspects of the design limit confidence in the inferences the project can produce.

**Identifiability of cell-size factors.** The proposal's most distinctive methodological contribution — the formal estimation of multiplicative cell-size and transcriptional-activity factors — is asserted rather than demonstrated. The proposal states that such factors "are identifiable when tumor sample number exceeds immune-cell-type number" [1], but identifiability depends on more than degrees of freedom. Disentangling multiplicative factors from cell-type fractions in the joint likelihood requires sufficient variation in cell-type proportions across samples and sufficient signal in the methylation data to anchor the fractions. If methylation data have limited power to distinguish certain closely related cell types — a known limitation documented by Cakmak et al. (2026), who found that methylation-based approaches showed "limited accuracy for lymphocyte populations" in cross-platform comparisons [10] — then the methylation anchor may be weak precisely where it is most needed.

**Distributional assumptions.** The normal approximation for methylation beta values at individual CpGs is pragmatic but only partially validated. Beta values are bounded in \[0,1\] and exhibit heteroscedasticity; the preliminary checks on 9,401 CpGs across five broad categories in 86 samples [1] may not generalize to the full set of CpGs and the 28 planned cell types. Moreover, the simulation strategy generates bulk tissues "by weighted mixtures of measured cell-specific expression and methylation" [1], but if these simulations use the same distributional assumptions (log-normal expression, normal methylation), they will not reveal sensitivity to assumption violations. More informative would be simulations with non-normal, bimodal, or zero-inflated features.

**Comparison strategy.** The proposal states that the method will be compared with existing expression-based and methylation-based deconvolution methods [1], but these are single-modality comparators. The scientific question is whether combining modalities in a formal joint likelihood outperforms using either alone. A simple multi-omics consensus approach — for example, averaging or taking a weighted consensus across separate expression and methylation deconvolutions — would provide a more informative baseline for evaluating the specific contribution of the complex likelihood machinery. The proposal does not name direct multi-omics comparators. This omission means the practical advantage of the joint framework may remain unclear even after the proposed evaluations.

**Parametric survival specification.** The parametric survival model (exponential given as example [1]) is chosen for computational efficiency, but the consequences of hazard misspecification for the fused-lasso coefficient estimates and the C-index comparisons are not discussed. If the true hazard deviates substantially from the parametric form, the joint model may not outperform a two-stage approach even when uncertainty propagation is genuinely beneficial — a negative result that would be difficult to interpret.

These concerns do not make the project infeasible, but they weaken the strength of evidence it can produce within its two-year scope. The attainable inferences about the benefits of joint modeling depend on conditions that the proposal does not fully examine, and a negative result would be ambiguous: it could reflect genuine absence of added value, or limitations of the parametric assumptions and comparison strategy. For an R21 exploratory grant, these limitations are manageable, but they justify the moderate rating.

## Feasibility

**Good feasibility: the project relies on existing public data and well-established computational frameworks, and the investigative team possesses the necessary statistical genomics expertise, though the two-year schedule is ambitious for delivering fully validated tools for both aims.**

The proposal can be carried out with the methods, data, and resources it describes. All primary data are already publicly available — TCGA provides matched expression, methylation, and survival data for colon and lung cancers as well as pan-cancer cohorts, while reference expression data include more than 800 samples covering 28 cell types from published datasets, and methylation references are assembled from six Illumina 450k studies [1]. No new data collection, participant recruitment, or wet-laboratory work is required, which eliminates the most common source of feasibility risk in biomedically oriented projects. The Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center provides the statistical and computing infrastructure for TCGA-scale matrix operations [1].

The computational approach builds on well-established statistical frameworks. The coordinate-ascent algorithm alternating between fraction estimation and profile re-estimation [1] is a standard strategy for latent-variable problems with the proposed structure. The preliminary work demonstrates that core computational components are already functional: the known-mixture validation using six methylation samples with five cell types and 362 informative CpGs showed that the maximum-likelihood approach outperforms nonnegative least squares (residual sum of squares 0.029 vs. 0.043) and is more robust to probe perturbation (0.055 vs. 0.155) [1]. The distributional checks on 9,401 CpGs [1] and the TCGA colon-cancer hypermutation analysis [1] further confirm that the investigators have applied their methods to the target data types.

The sequential schedule — Aim 1 (composition estimation) in Year 1, Aim 2 (joint survival model) in Year 2 [1] — is logically structured because Aim 2 depends on the estimation framework from Aim 1, multiplying the omics likelihood by survival contributions conditional on the same latent fractions [1]. The open-source R package deliverable with computationally intensive components in C or C++ and Rcpp integration is explicit and standard [1].

Three dependencies introduce uncertainty that affects feasibility within the two-year scope. First, the identifiability of the multiplicative cell-size and transcriptional-activity factors depends on conditions that are asserted (sample number exceeding cell-type number [1]) rather than systematically examined. If methylation data provide weak signals for distinguishing closely related lymphocyte populations, the methylation anchor required to disentangle cell-size factors from fraction estimates may be insufficient, potentially requiring more development time than budgeted. Second, the joint survival model in Aim 2 introduces substantial additional complexity — the blockwise coordinate ascent updating omics parameters, survival parameters, and shared latent fractions iteratively [1] — within a single year. The parametric hazard specification (exponential example [1]) is chosen for computational efficiency, but if this assumption proves inadequate and a semiparametric formulation becomes necessary, the development timeline would extend. Third, the proposal mentions desirable extensions — support for single-modality deconvolution after training, and extension to additional molecular modalities [1] — without corresponding development milestones. These capabilities, if pursued, could divert effort from the core aims.

These dependencies do not make the project infeasible. An R21 exploratory grant is designed to accommodate this level of technical risk, and the existing preliminary implementations demonstrate that the investigators can make progress on both aims within the proposed period. The principal feasibility concern is that the deliverables may converge toward proof-of-concept implementations rather than the fully validated tools described [1], particularly for the joint survival model. This outcome would still produce useful knowledge about the viability of the joint modeling approach and would be consistent with the exploratory nature of the mechanism.

## Ethics and safety

**Excellent ethics and safety: the project uses only existing public, de-identified omics data with no participant contact, and the data-protection arrangements are proportionate and complete.**

The study is exclusively computational, relying on publicly available datasets — TCGA, ENCODE, and published immune-cell reference panels — with no new participant recruitment, specimen collection, or direct human contact [1]. All data are de-identified prior to deposition in their respective repositories, and the investigators explicitly state they will not seek identifying information or attempt re-identification. This places the project squarely within the category of secondary analysis of existing, anonymized data, which poses negligible residual risk to the original participants. The proposal correctly identifies that the study qualifies for Exemption 4 under the Common Rule, consistent with the regulatory treatment of research using publicly available, de-identified biospecimen-derived data when no re-identification is attempted [1].

The data-storage arrangements are appropriate for the sensitivity level of the information. Data will be stored on designated secured machines accessible only to project personnel [1]. TCGA and related datasets contain molecular measurements (gene expression, DNA methylation) and limited clinical covariates such as survival time and censoring indicators, but no direct identifiers. The primary risk vector is the theoretical possibility of re-identification through linkage with external data sources, but the investigators' commitment to not seeking identifying information and the institutional security infrastructure reduce this to a remote possibility that is proportionate to the scientific aims. No additional data-protection measures — such as a formal data use agreement beyond the standard TCGA access requirements or an institutional privacy review beyond the exemption determination — appear necessary for work at this level of identifiability.

No direct benefit to the original participants is anticipated or claimed; the proposal correctly characterizes the expected benefit as methodological and scientific [1]. This is appropriate for a methods-development project that aims to produce statistical tools and applied analyses rather than clinical interventions or individual-level findings. The project involves no biosafety, chemical, or radiation hazards, as all work is in silico. There are no animal subjects. The open-source R package deliverables [1] are planned for public release, which raises no ethical concerns and is consistent with standard practice in computational methods development.

The proposal identifies no conflicts of interest, dual-use concerns, or community-level risks from the research outputs. The methods are statistical tools for immune composition estimation and survival modeling; they do not produce actionable clinical predictions, individual-level diagnostic results, or findings that could be misapplied to stigmatize population groups. The potential downstream applications in epidemiological and translational studies [1] are foreseeable but remote from this exploratory methodology project, and the ethical implications of those future uses would be addressed in the governance frameworks of the studies that adopt the tools.

One minor gap is that the proposal does not specify whether the secured machines described for data storage are part of a formally audited institutional information-security program with access logging, encryption standards, and breach notification procedures. However, Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center is a major research institution with established data-security infrastructure, and the absence of these administrative details from a brief ethics statement within an R21 application does not indicate a material deficiency. The described arrangements — secured, access-restricted machines — are proportionate to the risk profile of de-identified molecular data.

## Management

**Good management: the investigators possess complementary expertise in statistical genomics and methylation analysis, the sequential schedule is logically structured, and the open-source software deliverables are well-defined; the distribution of responsibilities is not detailed, but the documented expertise and institutional resources are sufficient for an exploratory R21 project of this scope.**

The project is led by Wei Sun (Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center), who contributes expertise in statistical genomics, copy-number analysis, expression analysis, and software development, with co-investigator Michael Wu providing complementary expertise in methylation analysis and association methods [1]. This core pairing covers the two principal data modalities — gene expression and DNA methylation — that the likelihood framework must integrate, and the statistical traditions — mixture modeling, coordinate-ascent optimization, penalized regression — on which the proposed methods build. The clinical collaborators, Ulrike Peters (colorectal cancer) and McGarry Houghton (lung cancer), supply domain knowledge for the initial cancer-type applications [1], though their specific roles in the methodological development work are not elaborated. For a methods-focused R21 where the primary deliverables are statistical tools and TCGA analyses rather than clinical translation, the clinical collaborators' contribution is appropriately positioned as consultative rather than central.

The expertise coverage is sufficient for the proposed work. The PI's background in statistical genomics and copy-number analysis is directly relevant to the tumor-purity inputs that the deconvolution framework requires [1], and the co-investigator's methylation expertise supports the development and validation of the normal-mixture methylation component, including the cell-type-specific quantile normalization planned for harmonizing the six reference datasets [1]. The preliminary work — including the known-mixture methylation validation [1], the TCGA colon-cancer hypermutation analysis [1], and the distributional checks on 9,401 CpGs [1] — demonstrates that the investigators have already implemented core computational components and applied them to the target data types, reducing the risk that fundamental development obstacles will emerge during the award period.

The two-year, two-aim schedule is logically sequenced: Aim 1 (composition estimation) occupies Year 1, and Aim 2 (joint survival model) occupies Year 2 [1]. This sequential dependency is appropriate because the survival model multiplies the omics likelihood from Aim 1 by survival contributions conditional on the same latent fractions [1]; the deconvolution framework must be functional before the joint model can be developed and validated. The open-source R package deliverable with computationally intensive components in C or C++ and Rcpp integration [1] is explicit, standard for the field, and matched to the TCGA-scale data volumes the project will process. The Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center provides the necessary statistical and computing infrastructure for TCGA-scale matrix operations [1]. No specialized equipment, wet-laboratory facilities, or external data-access negotiations beyond standard TCGA and ENCODE access are required, which simplifies project management.

Two aspects of the management plan are less fully specified but do not threaten feasibility at the R21 scale. First, the distribution of labor between the PI and co-investigator — who will lead the expression-model development, the methylation-model development, the coordinate-ascent implementation, and the TCGA analyses — is not detailed. For a two-investigator methods project with closely integrated aims, this level of operational detail is not unusual at the proposal stage, and the complementary expertise suggests a natural division: Sun leads the expression likelihood and joint survival components, Wu leads the methylation component and reference-data assembly. Second, the extensions mentioned as capabilities — support for single-modality deconvolution after training, and extension to additional molecular modalities [1] — appear as desirable features without allocated development milestones. If pursued, these could compete for effort with the core aims; the investigators would benefit from prioritizing the primary deliverables and treating these extensions as contingent on progress.

The publication and dissemination plan — methods, analyses, and software published through standard scientific channels [1] — is appropriate for a methodology project. The open-source R package format with Rcpp integration ensures that the tools will be accessible to the computational biology community that would use them. These management arrangements are adequate for delivering the stated aims within the proposed two-year period, with the principal uncertainty being the ambition of the joint survival model relative to the single year allocated to Aim 2 — a concern that falls under feasibility rather than management per se, but one that the investigators should monitor during execution.
