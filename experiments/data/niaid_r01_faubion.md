# Inflammatory Cascades Disrupt Treg Function through Epigenetic Mechanisms

**Principal investigator:** William A. Faubion, Mayo Clinic  
**Mechanism:** NIH R01

## 1. Project overview

This project investigates how inflammatory signals alter the epigenetic machinery that maintains regulatory T cell (Treg) function in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Its central hypothesis is that the histone methyltransferase EZH2 is required for Treg homeostasis and that inflammatory signaling disrupts EZH2, contributing to intestinal inflammation. The immediate objective is to characterize the relationship between FOXP3, EZH2, and IL6 signaling. The longer-term goal is to establish a mechanistic basis for Treg cell therapies that retain suppressive function in an inflamed intestine.

Three linked studies will determine how FOXP3 and EZH2 repress target genes, identify cytokine-dependent modifications that alter EZH2 activity, and test whether interrupting this pathway preserves Treg function during active experimental colitis. Mechanistic experiments in cell lines and primary T cells will be connected to conditional mouse models and adoptive-transfer experiments.

## 2. Background and rationale

FOXP3 is central to Treg differentiation and immune suppression. Nevertheless, FOXP3-positive cells are present within inflamed IBD lesions, where their regulatory activity may be impaired and some cells express inflammatory cytokines. Their presence alone therefore does not establish effective immune regulation. In animal models, Tregs generally prevent colitis more efficiently than they treat established inflammation. Understanding how an inflammatory environment changes their function is important for cell therapy.

EZH2 catalyzes histone H3 lysine 27 methylation as a component of Polycomb repressive complexes. Two related questions motivate this study: how epigenetic mechanisms control expression of the **FOXP3 gene**, and how EZH2 acts with **FOXP3 protein** to control downstream genes in established Tregs. Earlier work from the laboratory concerns KLF-dependent regulation of the FOXP3 locus. The current project principally addresses the second question, together with the effect of inflammatory signaling on EZH2 enzymatic activity.

Several inflammatory pathways, including IL1β, TNFα, and IL6, can impair Treg development or function, whereas IL10 can enhance it. Phosphorylation of EZH2 by different kinases can change its histone methyltransferase activity, substrate preferences, or complex membership. IL6 is the model inflammatory input for this proposal. The working model is that IL6 activates JAK2, which modifies EZH2, weakens repression of inflammatory gene networks, and impairs Treg-mediated suppression. RORA and IRF4 provide target genes through which chromatin, transcriptional, and functional effects can be connected.

Selective modification of Tregs offers a different approach from systemic cytokine blockade. IL6 also supports epithelial homeostasis. We will therefore test cell-intrinsic interventions that preserve suppressive function while leaving IL6 responses in other tissues intact.

## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **Define the FOXP3–EZH2 complex required for repression of immunoregulatory gene networks.** Map interacting domains, test their contribution to target-gene repression, and establish their importance for Treg function in vitro and in vivo.
2. **Determine how inflammatory signals modify EZH2 enzymatic activity.** Identify and test IL6-dependent phosphorylation sites in the EZH2 SET domain, with particular attention to JAK2 and Y641, and connect these changes to transcription and Treg suppression.
3. **Test whether inhibiting IL6-to-EZH2 signaling sustains Treg function in intestinal inflammation.** Compare receptor-deficient and EZH2-modified Tregs in treatment of established colitis and evaluate ex vivo editing of the IL6 receptor as a potential route toward cell therapy.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

The laboratory has characterized KLF-dependent regulation of FOXP3 and TGFβ pathways. KLF10 can support FOXP3 expression through different chromatin-modifying partners, including Sin3-HDAC and PCAF, and KLF14 also influences Treg differentiation. These studies provide transcriptional, chromatin, and experimental-colitis expertise for the proposed work.

Conditional loss of the EZH2 SET domain under control of the FOXP3 promoter produces a severe inflammatory phenotype. In the reported survival study, approximately 80% of FOXP3ΔEZH2 animals died by 35 days; the survival dataset included 107 mice. Survivors examined at eight weeks were highly susceptible to a normally subclinical 1% DSS challenge. The DSS comparison included 16 mice and used blinded histological assessment. Mutant Tregs retained commonly used surface markers and showed features of activation, but produced less IL10, more IFNγ and IL17, and suppressed responder-cell proliferation poorly.

For the IL6 studies, IL6-receptor-deficient responder cells allow effects on wild-type Tregs to be examined without attributing responder-cell IL6 signaling to Treg dysfunction. In this setting, IL6 impaired suppression. IL6 also reduced EZH2-associated H3K27 methyltransferase activity in primary lymphocytes and cell lines. A JAK2 inhibitor blocked this effect more consistently than AKT or MAPK inhibitors. Motif prediction and mass spectrometry identified candidate SET-domain phosphorylation sites, including Y641. A structural model predicts that modifying Y641 can disrupt an interaction with the substrate lysine. In an EZH2-deficient cell system, a Y641E substitution had lower methyltransferase activity than wild-type EZH2 or Y641F.

## 5. Research design and methods

### Aim 1: FOXP3–EZH2 interactions, target-gene repression, and Treg function

**Map the molecular interaction.** FLAG-EZH2 and HIS-FOXP3 deletion constructs will be expressed in T cell lines and primary cells. Co-immunoprecipitation will map interacting regions, including FOXP3 zinc-finger and forkhead domains and EZH2 WD/SET-associated regions. Proximity ligation will confirm association in cellular context. The laboratory reports approximately 20% nucleofection efficiency in primary cells and greater than 75% transduction with its adenoviral approach. These established methods will be used according to the experimental system.

**Test target-gene repression.** RORA and IRF4 promoter–luciferase reporters will test how FOXP3 dosage and wild-type or mutant EZH2 affect expression. Constructs that disrupt FOXP3/EZH2 association will distinguish interaction-dependent repression from other functions of each protein. Chromatin immunoprecipitation will assess H3K27me3 and recruitment of EZH2 and FOXP3 at target promoters. Conditional EZH2-deficient Tregs will be reconstituted with wild-type or mutant EZH2. Crossing to a CAR-transgenic background provides an experimental route for efficient adenoviral delivery. The combined assays connect protein association, chromatin occupancy, histone modification, and transcription.

**Test suppressor function.** In vitro assays will combine naïve FOXP3-negative CD4-positive responders with titrated Tregs under anti-CD3/anti-CD28 stimulation. Wild-type EZH2 and interaction-defective mutants will be compared for rescue of EZH2-deficient Treg activity. Responder proliferation is the functional endpoint.

For the prevention model, approximately 350,000 naïve CD4-positive CD45RB-high cells will be transferred to RAG-null recipients, alone or with 100,000 Tregs. The existing inducible EZH2-deletion model permits deletion after transfer and helps separate mature Treg function from developmental effects. In the reported preliminary experiment, tamoxifen was given for five days and recipients were evaluated at six weeks. Planned rescue experiments compare wild-type EZH2 with interaction-defective constructs. Outcomes include disease activity, blinded colon histology, cytokines from mesenteric lymph-node cells, and colonic supernatants.

The expected result is that disruption of the relevant interaction will impair repression of RORA/IRF4 and weaken suppression. Alternatives include an indirect association through EED or SUZ12; their depletion followed by co-immunoprecipitation would test this possibility. If FOXP3 is not required for EZH2 targeting, the investigators will examine EZH2-associated long noncoding RNAs and use functional knockdown to test their contribution to localization and repression.

### Aim 2: IL6-dependent phosphorylation and EZH2 function

**Identify the signaling mechanism.** Primary Tregs and Jurkat/HAP-1 cell systems will be stimulated with IL6 at 10 ng/ml. EZH2 modifications will be measured by mass spectrometry and immunoblotting. A phospho-Y641 antibody has been developed for targeted analysis. JAK2 will be manipulated using AZD1480, siRNA, and a dominant-negative construct; constitutively active JAK2 provides a gain-of-function comparison. Tregs with FOXP3-directed IL6Rα deletion provide a receptor-specific negative control. The design tests whether IL6-dependent EZH2 phosphorylation requires JAK2 and Treg-intrinsic IL6 receptor signaling.

**Establish effects on enzymatic activity.** Six candidate phosphorylation sites in the SET domain will be evaluated systematically. Serine-to-aspartate and tyrosine-to-glutamate substitutions are used as phosphomimetic constructs; serine-to-alanine and tyrosine-to-phenylalanine substitutions are used to prevent phosphorylation. A CRISPR/Cas-generated EZH2-deficient HAP-1 system permits comparison of transfected constructs without endogenous EZH2 activity obscuring the result. Candidate effects will then be tested in primary FOXP3ΔEZH2 Tregs after adenoviral reconstitution, with H3K27 methyltransferase assays performed on nuclear extracts.

Y641 is the initial feasibility example. The hypothesis predicts that Y641E will reduce activity and that Y641F will maintain activity during IL6 exposure.

**Figure 15.** Histone methyltransferase activity of wild-type EZH2 and Y641 substitutions.

**Connect molecular effects to whole-cell function.** Genomically integrated RORA and IRF4 luciferase reporters in Jurkat cells will test whether IL6-induced transcription depends on EZH2 modification. This avoids relying only on episomal reporters. Wild-type and mutant EZH2 will be introduced into EZH2-deficient Tregs, followed by suppression assays with and without IL6. An activity-deficient construct is expected to de-repress target reporters and impair suppression even without IL6, whereas a phosphorylation-resistant construct is expected to preserve repression and suppression during IL6 exposure.

We will also consider other mechanisms. IL6-dependent modification might change EZH2 substrates or protein partners rather than simply abolishing activity. Complex isolation and partner/substrate analysis would examine this possibility. Other inflammatory inputs, including TNF-family receptors and IL1 pathways, or protective IL10 signaling could be examined with the same mechanistic pipeline if the results warrant them.

### Aim 3: Treg function during established intestinal inflammation

Human CD4-cell expression profiles show altered IL6-related networks and EZH2 targets in Crohn’s lesions; 379 of 457 differentially expressed EZH2 target genes were upregulated in the reported comparison. Conditional EZH2-deficient Tregs also showed inflammatory pathway changes. Pharmacological EZH2 inhibition worsened DSS colitis in a preliminary eight-animal experiment. These observations motivate a treatment experiment in which cells encounter active inflammation, rather than only a prophylactic co-transfer experiment.

**Figure 17.** Inflammatory expression profiles and effects of EZH2 inhibition in experimental colitis.

**Treat active colitis using receptor- or EZH2-modified Tregs.** RAG-null recipients will first receive naïve effector cells. Four weeks later, animals with established colitis will receive titrated doses of 100,000–300,000 wild-type or IL6Rα-deficient Tregs. Parallel experiments will compare FOXP3ΔEZH2 Tregs reconstituted with wild-type EZH2, Y641E, or Y641F. The central comparison is whether interrupting IL6 receptor signaling or maintaining EZH2 activity improves treatment relative to wild-type Tregs. Disease activity, histology, and the cellular/cytokine measurements developed for the transfer model provide readouts.

**Test ex vivo editing.** The project will edit IL6Rα in primary wild-type Tregs using the established TALEN platform in collaboration with the institutional genetics core. Transiently transfected cells will be selected through the accompanying fluorescent marker, receptor loss verified by flow cytometry, and edited cells expanded using the laboratory’s Treg culture methods. These cells will then be tested in the same established-colitis treatment setting. The experiments will test the potential of ex vivo modification for Treg therapy.

Alternative explanations include resistance of effector cells to suppression rather than an intrinsic Treg defect. Colitis induced with IL6Rα-mutant effector cells and treated with wild-type Tregs will help distinguish these mechanisms. EZH1-dependent maintenance of H3K27 methylation is another possible contributor if EZH2 does not explain the findings. Because Y641F has been associated with lymphoproliferative conditions in other settings, we will investigate long-term function and toxicity; rescue experiments in mice lacking functional Tregs are a possible way to study persistence and adverse effects.

## 6. Implementation, resources, and schedule

The five-year project combines the PI’s intestinal immunology and Treg expertise with epigenetics, structural modeling, proteomics, and genome-editing collaborations. Available resources include flow cytometry and cell sorting, confocal imaging, sequencing and gene-expression cores, proteomics, mouse genetics, biochemical instrumentation, and established tissue-culture facilities. Domain mapping and mechanistic assays provide reagents and interpretation for the later rescue and treatment experiments.

The animal section estimates 2,139 mice over five years, including experimental donors, recipients, and breeding animals. It specifies both sexes for the principal donor assays, generally at four to six weeks, with approximately 150 wild-type, 100 IL6R-mutant, and 100 EZH2-deficient donor animals per year for recurring biochemical work. Individual in vitro assays are planned in triplicate. The transfer-colitis calculation uses four RAG-null recipients per treatment group, repeated three times; the stated power rationale is 90% power for a difference of 1.25 standard deviations under one-way ANOVA.

## 7. Ethics and data practices

Animal work is subject to Mayo Clinic IACUC review in an accredited centralized facility with veterinary oversight. Study animals will be weighed and examined daily; predefined time points, loss of more than 20% of starting body weight, or a moribund appearance are endpoints. Animals will receive analgesic support after adoptive transfer and approved euthanasia procedures. Human Crohn’s disease and control tissue-expression data form part of the preliminary scientific basis; the proposed cell-treatment studies are in mice.

The resource-sharing plan makes generated cell and mouse lines available to other investigators under institutional and NIH guidelines and provides for deposition of resulting peer-reviewed publications in PubMed Central.
