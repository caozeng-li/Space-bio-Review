# Image-Guided Cochlear Implant Programming: Pediatric Speech, Language, and Literacy

Principal investigators: Rene H. Gifford and Stephen Camarata. Mechanism: R01.

## 1. Project overview

This project will evaluate image-guided cochlear implant programming (IGCIP) in children and investigate how changes in auditory function relate to speech recognition, phonological awareness, reading, speech production, and language. A double-blind, randomized waitlist-control design will compare immediate IGCIP with programming deferred for 12 months, with both groups followed for 24 months. The intervention uses each child's cochlear anatomy and electrode location to deactivate electrodes predicted to provide redundant or extracochlear stimulation, while preserving as many useful active channels as possible.

The study will enroll 72 children, 36 per randomized group, with an intended final analysis sample of 60, or 30 per group. The work tests both short-term auditory benefits and more distal developmental relationships. Analyses of language and literacy are not contingent on a significant average intervention effect, because individual variation in auditory change can still inform the proposed pathways.

## 2. Background and rationale

Cochlear implantation has substantially improved children's communication opportunities, but many early-implanted children still show persistent language and reading difficulties. Auditory deprivation before implantation contributes, and the quality of the subsequent electrical signal may continue to constrain development. Children with prelingual deafness often demonstrate poorer spectral resolution than adult implant users, and spectral resolution does not consistently predict their speech recognition. They may rely more heavily on temporal-envelope or combined spectrotemporal information.

This distinction matters for programming. A strategy emphasizing fine spectral information may differ from one maximizing temporal-envelope detail. We will measure the auditory mechanisms that support children’s performance and compare spectral and temporal contributions. IGCIP offers an anatomically informed intervention that can alter the electrode-neural interface and provide a controlled source of change for testing auditory and developmental relationships.

IGCIP begins with an atlas based on ten micro-CT scans of human cadaveric cochleae. Scala tympani, scala vestibuli, and the modiolus are delineated in the atlas, which is fitted to the individual's preoperative clinical scan using the visible bony cochlear outline. A postoperative scan identifies the electrode-array centerline and supports a three-dimensional electrode model. Registering the scans locates each electrode relative to frequency-mapped neural endings. Distance-versus-frequency curves then identify electrodes whose stimulation regions overlap those of neighboring contacts.


Figure 2. CT-based electrode reconstruction and distance-versus-frequency analysis. Three-dimensional views locate contacts relative to the cochlear neural interface; each colored distance curve represents one electrode. Local overlap guides selective deactivation.

An additional purpose is detection of extracochlear electrodes, which may leave assigned acoustic frequencies without effective intracochlear delivery. Deactivating such contacts and redistributing the acoustic spectrum may be especially relevant to children's acquisition of high-frequency speech cues.

Language and literacy outcomes involve several possible paths. Better auditory input may improve speech recognition, which may support phonological awareness (PA), language, and reading. Alternatively, improved language representations may help children develop phonological structure despite limited spectral resolution. Lexical restructuring provides a rationale for studying reciprocal and mediated relationships instead of treating spectral resolution as a sufficient explanation of reading. Measuring these domains together over time will test direct and indirect pathways and characterize untreated as well as treated growth.

## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **Auditory function.** Compare spectral, temporal, and spectrotemporal resolution and speech recognition between immediate and deferred groups. Test whether IGCIP produces short-term gains at two to six months, particularly for recognition in noise, and whether resolution changes predict recognition gains after baseline and working-memory adjustment.
2. **Phonological awareness and reading.** Test whether differential auditory and speech-recognition growth predicts PA growth and whether PA mediates reading development. Cross-lagged and path analyses will examine individual changes even if the treatment contrast in Aim 1 is not significant.
3. **Speech and language.** Compare receptive and expressive language and speech production across groups and time. Test whether auditory improvements, speech recognition, and/or PA mediate gains in clinical scores and subclinical acoustic measures while accounting for cognition, memory, and relevant child and family characteristics.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

The team studied 41 pediatric CI users aged 4.4-17.9 years, with a mean of 9.7, after IGCIP. In the experimental ear, quiet speech tests showed significant improvement in 16%, no change in 79%, and worsening in 5%. Across sentence-in-noise conditions, 28% improved, 69% were unchanged, and 3% worsened. In the bilateral aided condition, no child showed a significant decrement and 24% improved in sentence recognition in noise. Thirty-one of 41 chose to retain the IGCIP map. These preliminary data support feasibility and show substantial variability in benefit, motivating the randomized comparison.

For 19 children with BKB-SIN data, the experimental-ear mean improved from 13.0 to 11.8 dB SNR and the bilateral mean from 8.2 to 6.8 dB. Of 23 children followed for one year, 16 retained the map and showed continued group-level improvement; the seven who did not retain it returned to baseline or showed nonsignificant change.

Spectral modulation detection was not associated with speech recognition in the 41 pediatric participants, unlike the laboratory's findings in 542 adults. A 14-child temporal-modulation pilot at 128 Hz showed associations with word and sentence recognition, and a five-child pre/post dataset suggested an association between temporal and word-recognition benefit. These small samples motivate the spectral-versus-temporal comparison without resolving it.

A four-year-old case had two extracochlear basal electrodes. After their deactivation plus one apical deactivation, the input spectrum was allocated across intracochlear contacts from 100-8,500 Hz. Word recognition improved from 48% to 72% after six weeks. Standard articulation scores did not change, but acoustic analysis detected greater high-frequency speech energy and improved vowel precision. Another child showed no recognition benefit yet improved consonant precision and duration. These cases support including acoustic outcomes that can change independently of conventional scores.

## 5. Research design and methods

### Participants, allocation, and baseline control

Children will be 6-12 years old with prelingual deafness, bilateral moderate-to-profound sensorineural loss, at least one CI, and implantation before age three. In unilateral CI users, the nonimplanted ear must also have at least moderate-to-profound loss. Typical nonverbal cognition is required; a standard score below 85 excludes participation. Autism, neurological disorder, general cognitive impairment, major temporal-bone abnormalities such as common cavity or ossification, and onset of moderate-to-profound loss after age two are exclusion criteria. Preoperative CT must be available, and postoperative imaging must be available or obtained through the linked imaging protocol after consent.

The statistician will generate a computer-based permuted-block allocation schedule with blocks of four. Identical imaging, IGCIP-plan generation, and assessment procedures are specified for both arms. Participants and outcome assessors are intended to remain blind, with allocation access restricted to the PIs and statistician, who will not administer or score assessments.

Before baseline, conventional clinical maps will be optimized to provide CI-aided thresholds of 20-25 dB HL from 250-6,000 Hz. Electrically evoked stapedial reflex thresholds will verify upper stimulation levels where feasible; behavioral measures substitute when middle-ear conditions prevent them. Contralateral hearing aids will be verified by real-ear measurement. If map optimization is required, baseline testing waits at least two months. Equipment and listening checks occur at every study visit, reducing the chance that hardware malfunction explains a score change.

Child and family covariates include chronological age, ages at diagnosis and implantation, etiology, daily device use from data logging, nonverbal cognition, gender, maternal education, socioeconomic status, family size, and preschool environment. These support interpretation of developmental variation and potential confounding.

### Intervention and assessment schedule

Distance-versus-frequency analysis will identify electrodes whose local minimum electrode-to-modiolus distance is encompassed by adjacent contacts. The planned minimum-error neural-network procedure seeks to remove redundant or extracochlear contacts while maximizing the number of active electrodes, specified as more than eight. The hypothesis is reduced channel interaction and improved spatial selectivity, not a fixed deactivation count.

For bilateral users, only one CI receives IGCIP: the poorer-performing ear, or the second implanted ear when performance is similar. The unmodified ear and bilateral condition provide complementary within-child comparisons. The immediate arm receives IGCIP after baseline; the deferred arm receives it at the 12-month visit. Both arms attend baseline and months 2, 6, 12, 14, 18, and 24. Thus the design yields 12 months of untreated growth and 12 months of treated growth in the deferred arm, and 24 months after treatment in the immediate arm.

### Auditory function and speech recognition

Resolution tasks will use broadband noise from 125-8,000 Hz at 65 dB SPL in sound field with a three-interval, two-alternative forced-choice format. Spectral modulation detection compares flat and modulated spectra at 0.5 and one cycle per octave. Temporal modulation detection compares a flat envelope with sinusoidal modulation at four, 32, and 128 Hz, sampling the low-rate plateau and higher-rate decline of the modulation-transfer function. Thresholds are expressed as 20 log m, where m is modulation index. Spectrotemporal tasks combine one cycle per octave with four- or 32-Hz modulation and estimate spectral depth at each rate.

Two-down/one-up tracking targets 70.7% correct. Time-locked cartoon images and touchscreen responses support child engagement, with auditory and visual feedback. These nonlexical tasks will test which auditory dimensions change with programming and whether they account for recognition gains.

Speech tests will assess each CI alone and the bilateral aided condition. CNC monosyllables, nonword repetition, BabyBio sentences, and BKB-SIN provide complementary recognition measures. Speech is presented at 60 dB SPL in quiet and 65 dB SPL in co-located noise, with fixed-noise BabyBio testing at +5 dB SNR. An adaptive HINT task presents sentences from zero degrees with semidiffuse noise from 45-315 degrees, fixed at 72 dB SPL, and varies speech to estimate 50% correct. Multiple lists permit repeated testing without item-list repetition. Nonwords reduce lexical support and may be more sensitive to altered auditory detail.

Auditory Skills Checklist, PEACH, and PedsQL 4.0 questionnaires will measure perceived auditory function and quality of life. These complement objective thresholds and recognition scores rather than substitute for them.

### Language, speech production, cognition, PA, and reading

Receptive language will be measured with ROWPVT-4, PPVT-4, TACL-4 vocabulary/morphology/elaborated-sentence subscales, and the CELF-4 receptive composite. Expressive language uses EOWPVT-4, SPELT-3, and the CELF-4 expressive composite. Multiple tests distinguish vocabulary from grammar and reduce dependence on a single score.

Speech production includes GFTA-3 and recorded samples from the Renfrew Bus Story, Ling six sounds, and repetition of a familiar song. Automated acoustic analyses will characterize harmonic-to-noise ratio, fundamental-frequency distribution, articulation precision or entropy, envelope modulation, formants and vowel space, long-term spectrum, speaking rate, and low-frequency energy related to nasality. Manual analysis will examine selected affricate/stop and fricative distinctions, whole-word variability, and atypical errors. These measures can detect changes not yet reflected in standardized articulation scores.

Leiter-3 provides nonverbal cognition. Working memory will be characterized with Woodcock-Johnson IV Numbers Reversed, serial recall of six nonrhyming high-frequency CVC nouns through touchscreen picture selection, and a visuospatial sequence task using six screen locations. Spoken-number and word recognition will be checked to separate failed access from failed recall. The combination distinguishes age-normed span, use of phonological structure, and nonverbal memory.

PA measures include CTOPP-2 and TAPS-3 plus a developmentally ordered battery of nonword repetition, initial-consonant discrimination and choice, final-consonant choice, phoneme deletion, and backwards words. Reading assessment uses WRMT-III and GORT-5 to characterize decoding and comprehension. The broad battery addresses the hypothesis that children can reach similar reading outcomes through different auditory and linguistic pathways.

### Statistical analysis, power, and alternative outcomes

Analyses will follow intent-to-treat principles, with type-I error controlled at no more than .05. Score distributions and trajectories will be inspected, reliability assessed using Cronbach's alpha, and general linear mixed or multilevel models used for group differences in change. Baseline outcome values, speech recognition, and working memory will be included where specified, because randomization does not guarantee identical initial scores. Group-by-time contrasts test whether immediate treatment changes trajectories relative to waiting. We hypothesize a near-zero waitlist slope and a positive immediate-treatment slope.

Bootstrapping will generate 95% confidence intervals for descriptive estimates, intervention effects, and path coefficients. A multivariate approach addresses correlated outcomes. Cross-lagged models compare changes in one domain with later changes in another and generate tentative causal hypotheses for further research. Child and family covariates will be considered in these analyses.

With 30 analyzed participants per arm, the design provides 80% power at two-sided alpha .05 for effects of 0.32 adjusted for baseline (Cohen's d equivalent .67) or 0.35 unadjusted (d equivalent .74). A total of 60 permits detection of a path correlation near .35. Detectable differences between path coefficients are estimated at .4-.5, depending on their values and correlations. The path analyses emphasize effect-size estimation and mechanistic understanding.

Item missingness will follow instrument rules. Without a specified rule, a participant completing at least 75% of an instrument will receive an available-item mean. Patterns of missing assessments will be examined by group and participant characteristics. We expect most missing assessments to be nonrandom and do not plan imputation for these assessments. Statistical bootstrapping will be used in analyses with incomplete repeated assessments.

If an IGCIP plan is identical to the clinical map, we will withdraw the participant because no programming intervention exists to test. If performance declines, restoration of the prior clinical map is an available safety response. Lack of an average auditory benefit does not prevent testing whether variable auditory, PA, or recognition changes predict language and production trajectories. Nonresponders will remain informative, including continued outcome follow-up where possible.

## 6. Implementation, resources, and schedule

Gifford will oversee auditory perception and recruitment; Camarata will oversee speech, language, and literacy. The team includes otology, image analysis and engineering, biostatistics, and consultants in phonological processing and speech production. A project manager and trained research staff will coordinate visits and assessments, with monthly team meetings. Recruitment draws on Vanderbilt's large pediatric implant program and children becoming age eligible during the project.

The five-year schedule begins with staff training, assessment preparation, allocation generation, and active recruitment. Staggered cohorts complete the seven-visit, two-year sequence, with recruitment concentrated where feasible around school breaks. Follow-up, analysis of blinded data, and dissemination proceed while later cohorts enroll. Later years complete the longitudinal assessments and integrated auditory-developmental analyses.

## 7. Ethics and data management

Parents provide consent and children assent under institutional procedures. The intervention uses clinical programming tools, but potential risks include reduced speech recognition and exposure to postoperative CT. Existing clinically obtained scans will be used when available; outside-implanted children may receive low-dose imaging through the linked approved protocol. Radiation oversight and institutional review apply. Baseline map optimization and equipment checks help separate intervention effects from correctable clinical problems.

At baseline and 12 months, staff will establish smartphone word-recognition scores through Bluetooth or direct input. Caregivers repeat testing at home one month later. A decline exceeding the 95% test-retest interval triggers an option to restore the prior map or continue for one additional month until the next scheduled visit. Withdrawal because of poor performance may require unmasking for that participant, while two-year outcome observation may continue. Adverse events will be documented and reported, and independent monitoring will be organized if the IRB requires it.

REDCap and a centralized repository will store coded data, including imaging and behavioral measurements. The PIs retain a separate identity master list; paper records are secured, electronic files protected, and access limited to research personnel. Records will be retained for at least six years after study completion. De-identified data, methodological detail, and raw data in supplements or shared repositories will support reproducibility. Families will receive reports of their children's assessments, and findings will be disseminated to scientific and clinical audiences.
