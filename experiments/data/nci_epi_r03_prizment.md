# Immune-Regulating MHC Class I-Like Proteins and Colorectal Cancer Risk

**Principal investigator:** Anna Prizment, University of Minnesota  
**Application mechanism:** R03

## 1. Project overview

This project will determine whether circulating MHC class I-like proteins precede colorectal cancer (CRC) and identify environmental and genetic determinants of their levels. It will perform secondary analyses in the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) cohort, integrating repeated plasma proteomics, genotypes, lifestyle measurements, and incident cancer follow-up. The six proteins are MICA, MICB, ULBP1, ULBP2, ULBP3, and ULBP4. The principal hypothesis is that higher prediagnostic levels and greater increases in these proteins are associated with higher subsequent CRC risk.

The long-term objective is to understand immune surveillance and evasion sufficiently to inform CRC prevention and early detection. This study will establish prospective associations and their determinants; later work will validate assays, replicate findings, and evaluate incremental screening prediction.

## 2. Background and rationale

MHC class I-like proteins act as ligands for activating receptors, notably NKG2D on natural killer and T cells. Their expression on stressed or malignant cells can support immune recognition and tumor-cell killing. Shedding into the circulation can reduce cell-surface ligand availability and downregulate NKG2D, weakening that response. Thus, membrane expression and soluble circulating protein have distinct biological roles. MICA, the best-studied family member, provides the principal mechanistic example.

Higher soluble MICA has been associated with advanced disease and poorer prognosis in several cancers. Most previous CRC studies concern progression after cancer is present. The proposed question is different: whether levels in initially cancer-free people predict later CRC. Protein variants offer a related route to studying these mechanisms. Extracellular SNPs can affect receptor affinity, while transmembrane microsatellite variants may affect shedding. For example, prior studies identify rs1051792 (Val129Met) as affecting NKG2D affinity and A5.1 as influencing soluble MICA levels.

The proposed combination of six related proteins, repeated measurements, and genetic data provides an opportunity to examine both protein trajectories and their determinants. The inclusion of Black and White participants also allows assessment in populations not well represented in prior MHC class I-like protein studies. If informative, these proteins may eventually complement colonoscopy or other biomarkers.


## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **Prediagnostic proteins and CRC:** assess associations of subsequent CRC with (a) protein levels at the study baseline, ARIC Visit 2 in 1990–1992, and (b) short- and long-term changes before diagnosis. Higher levels and larger changes are hypothesized to predict greater risk.
2. **Determinants of protein levels:** identify demographic, lifestyle, and genetic factors associated with (a) baseline levels and (b) change over time in cancer-free participants. Candidate determinants include age, sex, race, smoking, BMI, diabetes, and functional SNPs and microsatellite variants.
3. **Secondary causal exploration:** use genetic instruments and Mendelian randomization to explore the relation between genetically predicted protein levels and CRC.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

Previous work by the investigators linked MICA variants to soluble MICA in a pancreatic-cancer case–control study. Among cancer-free participants, one and two A5.1 copies were associated with 24% and 38% higher soluble MICA, respectively. Preliminary ARIC analyses identified MICA SNP associations with CRC, and the team is investigating SNPs and microsatellite repeats in related genes. These findings motivate the prospective six-protein analysis.

A SOMAscan version 3 pilot in 42 ARIC participants yielded median CV 5%, Spearman correlation 0.89, and intraclass correlation 0.96 in blinded split samples. Over six to eight weeks, only one protein showed significant change. The full study will use version 4 measurements of 4,931 proteins, with the six prespecified MHC class I-like proteins as its focus.

## 5. Research design and methods

### 5.1 Cohort, specimens, and assays

ARIC originally enrolled 15,792 Black and White adults aged 45–64 in 1987–1989 from Forsyth County, Jackson, suburban Minneapolis, and Washington County, Maryland. Interviews, clinical examinations, anthropometry, laboratory measurements, and blood collection were repeated at visits. The project will use already collected data and frozen plasma, with no new participant contact or collection. Cancer ascertainment through 2015 combines state-registry linkage and active follow-up. Genotyping comes from GWAS and deep whole-genome sequencing; approximately 12,000 participants have sequencing through TOPMed and Centers for Common Disease Genomics.

SOMAscan measurements are available or underway for Visit 2 (1990–1992), Visit 3 (1993–1995), and Visit 5 (2011–2013). Figure 2 gives 12,589 cancer-free participants and 360 subsequent CRC cases at Visit 2, 11,340 and 306 at Visit 3, and 6,538 and 56 at Visit 5. Visit 2 is the baseline for this proposal, not the original ARIC enrollment visit.

The aptamer assay measures protein-bound SOMAmers in relative fluorescence units. Quality control includes hybridization-control normalization, within-plate median-signal normalization, calibrators against global reference standards, 26 pooled blinded plasma controls in triplicate across batches, and approximately 5% split-specimen duplicates. The cited version 4 lifetime median CV is 6.2% across 220 runs and 660 replicate QC samples. Visit 3 and 5 assays have been completed; Visit 2 assays are described as underway.


### 5.2 Aim 1a: baseline proteins and subsequent cancer

Initial descriptive analyses will examine distributions, correlations, and trajectories. Participants with cancer before Visit 2 will be excluded from the baseline risk analysis. Protein values will be log-transformed. The principal exposure is a factor score representing the six correlated proteins, estimated using principal-axis factor analysis. We anticipate correlations of approximately 0.4–0.55 and retains the first factor with eigenvalue greater than one. Scores will combine concentrations weighted by factor loadings and will be modeled continuously and in quartiles.

Multivariable Cox regression will estimate CRC hazard ratios and 95% confidence intervals. Follow-up ends at cancer, death, or study end. Proportional-hazards assumptions will be tested; nonproportional hazards or generalized gamma regression are proposed alternatives. Baseline adjustment includes age, sex, race, center, and education, with other CRC risk factors included when also associated with protein levels. Candidates include BMI, activity, smoking and pack-years, diabetes, alcohol, aspirin, fiber and red-meat intake, and eGFR.

To address possible occult cancer influencing the biomarkers, sensitivity analyses will exclude diagnoses within two or five years of sampling. Interactions will be explored by sex, race, colon versus rectum, and follow-up length. Exploratory analyses examine individual proteins continuously and categorically, including a separate category for undetectable levels. We will undertake comparison of analyses including missing values with complete-data analyses and describes inverse-probability weighting if missingness for a protein exceeds 10%.

With 12,589 cancer-free participants and 360 CRC cases, our power calculation gives 80% power at two-sided α = 0.05 to detect HR 1.24 per SD of the continuous factor score. Its categorical power example uses tertiles and HR 1.58, whereas the analysis description specifies quartiles.


### 5.3 Aim 1b: change in proteins and cancer

The short-term analysis uses the Visit 3 minus Visit 2 change, excludes cancer through Visit 3, and begins cancer follow-up at Visit 3. The exploratory long-term analysis uses Visit 5 minus Visit 2 and begins follow-up at Visit 5. These timing rules ensure that the protein change precedes the outcome period.

The principal factor-score analysis will use marginal structural Cox models. Inverse-probability-of-treatment weights will address time-dependent confounding by BMI, smoking, and other covariates. Separate weights will represent the relevant short-term covariate changes. Individual protein changes and the effect modifiers used in Aim 1a will also be explored. With 11,340 cancer-free Visit 3 participants and 306 CRC cases, the stated 80% power thresholds are HR 1.26 per SD of factor-score change and HR 1.65 for the quartile comparison.

### 5.4 Aim 2: determinants of levels and trajectories

Baseline linear regressions will use log protein concentrations as outcomes. Candidate predictors include age, sex, race, BMI, smoking, physical activity, alcohol and aspirin, diabetes, SNPs, and microsatellite repeats. Candidate MICA variants include rs1051792, rs1051794, rs1131896, rs1063635, rs2516448, rs3763288, rs2395029, and rs3128982, and MICB variants including rs2516400, rs2855804, rs2855812, rs3132464, rs3132468, and rs3828903. MICA microsatellites include A4, A5, A5.1, A6, A7, A8, A9, and A10.

Characteristics associated with a biomarker in univariate analysis will enter multivariable models. Genetic variants will be jointly included when not in linkage disequilibrium. Comparing models with and without genetic variables will estimate the percentage of protein variation explained by genetics. Bonferroni correction across six proteins gives α = 0.05/6, approximately 0.008.

Random-intercept, random-linear-time mixed models will analyze short- and long-term changes. Baseline lifestyle variables and changes in BMI, smoking, diabetes, alcohol, and aspirin will be considered, with separate inverse-probability weights for Visit 2–3 and Visit 2–5 changes. The power analysis gives 80%-power detectable mean differences of 2.81 pg/mL at baseline, 3.00 for Visit 2–3 change, and 3.90 for Visit 2–5 change. 


### 5.5 Secondary aim: Mendelian randomization

Genetic variants that are strongly associated with the relevant proteins and associated with CRC will be examined as instruments. The team is identifying such variants in ARIC and replicating associations in CRC datasets available through dbGaP. Weighted genetic scores will use coefficients from the variant–protein associations.

The proposed instrumental-variable analysis compares standard probit with instrumental-variable probit models in Stata (`probit` and `ivprobit`). First-stage F-statistics will assess instrument strength, using the conventional value above 10 as the stated criterion. The analysis is intended to explore a causal role for circulating proteins; it is distinct from the primary observational risk analysis.


### 5.6 Follow-on validation

If associations are observed, the investigators plan later validation of individual protein measurements using standard methods and replication in an independent larger asymptomatic cohort, such as PLCO. Subsequent work would determine whether the proteins improve risk prediction or complement screening. 

## 6. Implementation, resources, and schedule

The team combines molecular cancer epidemiology, computational genetics, and biostatistics. Existing ARIC proteomic, genomic, risk-factor, and cancer data make the project principally an analytical study. SAS and Stata will support descriptive, survival, mixed-model, and instrumental-variable analyses.

Months 1–3 cover IRB arrangements and data extraction. Aim 1 analyses occupy months 4–12; Aim 2, months 13–18; and the secondary aim, months 19–24. Manuscript preparation overlaps months 13–24.

## 7. Ethics and data practices

The project involves analysis of existing de-identified data without participant contact or additional data collection. Investigators receive coded research IDs; direct identifiers and the identity linkage remain with the ARIC Coordinating Center. Privacy protections cover transmission, handling, and distribution. Descriptive and association results will be shared under NIH policy, while access to the study dataset follows ARIC leadership and publication-committee policies.

## 8. Scientific foundations

This work builds on the investigators' MICA and pancreatic-cancer work; studies of NKG2D-ligand shedding and immune recognition; ARIC cancer ascertainment and genomic resources; SOMAscan validation and normalization work; and methods for marginal structural models and Mendelian randomization.
