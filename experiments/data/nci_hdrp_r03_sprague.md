# Multi-center Evaluation of Digital Breast Tomosynthesis with Synthesized Two-dimensional Mammography for Breast Cancer Screening

**Principal investigator:** Brian L. Sprague.  
**Application mechanism:** R03.

## 1. Project overview

We will evaluate breast cancer screening with digital breast tomosynthesis and synthesized two-dimensional mammography (s2D/DBT), without a separate conventional digital mammogram. The comparator is combined conventional digital mammography and tomosynthesis (DM/DBT). A multicenter observational study will use the natural experiment created when seven radiology facilities uniformly changed from DM/DBT to s2D/DBT. Existing prospectively collected records from the University of Vermont and University of Pennsylvania PROSPR Research Centers will be linked to pathology and cancer registries.

The planned dataset contains 98,850 DM/DBT and 86,850 s2D/DBT screening examinations performed during 2011–2017 and interpreted by 22 radiologists. We will compare clinical performance, specific malignant and benign diagnoses, and radiologists' breast-density assessments. The goal is evidence relevant to patients choosing screening, providers considering the new modality, payers, and screening policy.

## 2. Background and rationale

DBT acquires multiple low-dose x-ray exposures over an arc and reconstructs thin breast images. By reducing masking from overlapping tissue, it can reduce recalls and improve invasive cancer detection when added to conventional DM. However, acquiring both DM and DBT can approximately double radiation exposure relative to DM alone. Synthesized 2D images reconstructed from the DBT acquisition replace the separate DM exposure and have been estimated to reduce dose by about 45% compared with combined DM/DBT.

Evidence for DBT has primarily concerned its combination with conventional DM. Evidence for replacing conventional views with s2D is more limited, with mixed results from reader studies and small single-institution clinical series. Some studies included symptomatic patients or selected cancer cases using DM, which complicates inference about screening performance. A clinical study with ascertainment of cancer outcomes for at least one year can address false negatives, sensitivity, and specificity in addition to recall and detection rates.

The change in image appearance may have several effects. Reconstructed architectural distortion can become more conspicuous, potentially aiding cancer detection. Normal dense structures may also be enhanced and resemble calcifications. Radiologists' responses to these appearances may alter detection of calcific benign lesions and ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS). Lower DCIS detection and maintained invasive detection could change the balance of benefit and harm.

Breast density is both a risk factor and a determinant of mammography performance. It is rated subjectively using four BI-RADS categories and influences density notification and consideration of supplemental imaging. If s2D changes perceived density, patients may receive different risk assessments or recommendations without an underlying biological change. The proposed study therefore evaluates density classification alongside diagnostic performance.

## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **Determine clinical screening performance with s2D/DBT.** We hypothesize lower recall and higher specificity than DM/DBT, with comparable overall cancer detection, sensitivity, and false-negative rates.
2. **Evaluate screen-detected malignant and benign lesions.** We hypothesize higher invasive cancer detection, lower DCIS detection, and lower benign biopsy rates with s2D/DBT.
3. **Determine the distribution of mammographic density assessments.** We hypothesize fewer examinations classified as heterogeneously or extremely dense with s2D/DBT than with DM/DBT.

All comparisons use the same facilities before and after their transition, rather than comparing unrelated sites selected for different technologies.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

The Vermont and Pennsylvania PROSPR centers have collected standardized breast screening and outcomes information since 2011 and have collaborated on screening performance and density assessment. Their existing common data elements and linkage infrastructure support pooled analysis. Vermont includes predominantly white rural populations; the Pennsylvania medical center serves an urban population with nearly half of screened patients African-American. This provides variation across practice settings and patient groups.

Single-site Pennsylvania preliminary data included 5,366 s2D/DBT and 15,571 DM/DBT screens. Recall was 7.1% versus 8.8% (P<0.001), with comparable overall cancer detection of 5.0 versus 5.5 per 1,000 (P=0.72). Recall for calcifications and asymmetries was reduced. DCIS detection was 0.9 versus 1.5 per 1,000, but this difference was not statistically significant (P=0.30). Dense classifications were assigned to 28.6% versus 32.5% of examinations. These preliminary observations motivate the proposed multicenter study.

Prior pooled Vermont, Pennsylvania, and Dartmouth work provided DM/DBT performance estimates for the power calculations: recall 8.7%, benign biopsies 14.1 per 1,000, cancer detection 5.9 per 1,000, invasive detection 4.2 per 1,000, DCIS detection 1.7 per 1,000, PPV1 6.4%, false-negative rate 0.6 per 1,000, sensitivity 90.9%, specificity 91.3%, and dense classifications 34.0%.

## 5. Research design and methods

### 5.1 Sites, eligibility, and exposure

Vermont's PROSPR center collects patient, radiology, pathology, and outcome data across the state's breast imaging facilities. Six facilities have adopted s2D/DBT uniformly, including three academic-center and three community-hospital facilities. Together they screen about 38,000 women annually, with 16 interpreting radiologists. At the Pennsylvania medical center, uniform s2D/DBT adoption occurred in 2015; its approximately 11,000 annual screens are interpreted by six radiologists.

Eligible facilities must have performed DM/DBT screening for at least one year before uniformly adopting s2D/DBT. Eligible examinations are screening examinations from 2011–2017. Women with a personal history of breast cancer will be excluded because their imaging protocols can include extra views of the prior cancer site. Transitional examinations that combined conventional DM, DBT, and s2D will also be excluded. The cohorts are therefore DM/DBT before adoption and s2D/DBT with no conventional DM after adoption.

All participating DBT facilities use Hologic Selenia Dimensions equipment, and all s2D facilities use Hologic C-View reconstruction software. Routine screens include bilateral craniocaudal and mediolateral-oblique images. This common equipment and uniform facility-level adoption reduce differences in technology and selective assignment of patient subgroups to s2D.

### 5.2 Data collection and outcome ascertainment

Patient questionnaires, radiology systems, and EHR records provide age, race, education, family cancer history, reproductive/menstrual history, prior cancer, symptoms, and date of the last mammogram. Radiologists prospectively record examination indication, views, BI-RADS assessment, breast density, and recommendations. Facility and radiologist identifiers permit examination of correlated observations.

Vermont pathology facilities provide standardized abstraction forms and reports for benign and malignant specimens. Pennsylvania obtains pathology electronically through Penn Medicine. Available characteristics include specimen type, behavior, histology, grade, size, ER/PR/HER2 status, and OncotypeDX recurrence scores. Linkage to the Vermont and Pennsylvania cancer registries supplies validated cancer diagnoses and stage, including cancers diagnosed outside the screening facility. The two centers' data align with BI-RADS, College of American Pathologists, and central cancer registry standards. An exam-level Pennsylvania dataset will be securely transferred to Vermont for pooling.

### 5.3 Aim 1: performance definitions and models

We will use the ACR BI-RADS fifth-edition performance definitions. A positive screening examination is assessment 0, 3, 4, or 5; assessments 1 and 2 are negative. Cancer outcomes are diagnoses within twelve months after the screen. The measures are:

| Measure | Definition |
|---|---|
| Recall | Positive examinations / all examinations |
| Cancer detection | True positives / all examinations |
| False-negative rate | False negatives / all examinations |
| Sensitivity | True positives / examinations associated with cancer |
| Specificity | True negatives / examinations without cancer |
| PPV1 | True positives / positive screening examinations |

Biopsy rates will also be compared. Generalized estimating equations will estimate population-average performance by modality, accounting for multilevel, non-nested clustering within women, radiologists, and facilities using the Miglioretti–Heagerty approach. Descriptive analyses will first compare cohort characteristics. Models will examine adjustment for age, race/ethnicity, breast density, family history, and screening interval, and test interactions between patient characteristics and modality.

With the planned examination counts, the study projects 80% power to detect absolute differences of 0.4 percentage points in recall and 0.8 cancers per 1,000 examinations in cancer detection. The power section anticipates 1,114 cancers and 174,546 non-cancer outcomes, yielding detectable differences of 5.6 percentage points in sensitivity and 0.4 percentage points in specificity at 80% power. Correlation adjustment could change GEE variances; the protocol expects limited inflation because both modalities are represented within the same facilities, radiologists, and most women.

### 5.4 Aim 2: lesion-specific detection

Linkage of positive screens to pathology and registry records will identify invasive cancer, DCIS, and benign lesions. Benign findings will be subdivided into non-proliferative changes, fibroepithelial proliferations, proliferative lesions without atypia, and atypical lesions. Exploratory analyses will examine invasive cancers by ER/PR/HER2 subtype. Descriptive statistics and logistic regression using methods similar to Aim 1 will compare modalities. At 80% power, the stated detectable absolute decreases are 0.8 per 1,000 for invasive cancer, 0.5 per 1,000 for DCIS, and 1.5 per 1,000 for benign lesions.

### 5.5 Aim 3: density classifications

We will compare the distribution of all four BI-RADS categories: almost entirely fatty, scattered fibroglandular density, heterogeneously dense, and extremely dense. Analyses will also combine the latter two as dense and the former two as non-dense. Chi-square tests and logistic regression will adjust for age, race/ethnicity, and body mass index. The projected detectable absolute difference in dense classifications is 0.6 percentage points at 80% power.

### 5.6 Design considerations

The study is observational and exploits a before/after natural experiment. Its main protections against confounding are a common set of facilities and radiologists, expected stability of the served populations, uniform adoption rather than patient-specific selection, and exam-level covariates for statistical adjustment.

The investigation focuses on s2D/DBT versus conventional DM/DBT. It therefore addresses a different comparison from trials principally contrasting DBT with DM alone. The proposed data provide a rapid evaluation of a specific implementation change in routine practice and include both academic and community settings.

## 6. Implementation and resources

The study uses existing screening registries and outcome linkage rather than acquiring new mammograms for research. It will assemble eligible exam-level records, pool common data elements, establish twelve-month outcomes, and complete the three coordinated analyses. The team combines radiology, pathology, epidemiology, and experience in multicenter PROSPR collaborations. Outputs will characterize screening performance, lesion patterns, and density assessment to inform implementation and further evaluation of synthesized imaging.

## 7. Ethics and data practices

Vermont's registry uses passive consent on its imaging questionnaire, with an opt-out checkbox; approximately 95% of eligible patients participate. Pennsylvania secured a waiver of informed consent for inclusion of women screened in its network. Exam-level data will be transferred securely to Vermont for pooled analysis. The proposed comparison uses clinical imaging already obtained in routine care and does not introduce additional research radiation exposure.
