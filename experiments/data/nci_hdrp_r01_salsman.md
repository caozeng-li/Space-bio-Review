# Using MOST to EMPOWER: Optimizing Emotional Regulation Intervention to Enhance Well-being Among Young Adult Cancer Survivors

**Principal investigators:** John Salsman and Judith Moskowitz.  
**Application mechanism:** R01.

## 1. Project overview

We will optimize EMPOWER—Enhancing Management of Psychological Outcomes With Emotion Regulation—for young adult cancer survivors after active treatment. The web-based intervention teaches skills for reducing distress and increasing psychological well-being. Rather than evaluate the whole package alone, we will use the Multiphase Optimization Strategy (MOST) to determine which of five components contribute meaningfully to outcomes and explore mechanisms and differences in response.

A full factorial experiment will assign 352 survivors to the 32 combinations of five components, each present or absent. Main effects compare all participants assigned a component with all participants not assigned it, rather than treating the study as 32 separate small comparisons with one control. Primary outcomes are positive affect, life satisfaction, depression, and anxiety. The current four-year project covers optimization and assembly of a refined intervention; a future multicenter trial would provide confirmatory evaluation of the resulting package.

## 2. Background and rationale

Young adults face cancer during education, employment, relationship formation, and family planning. They may have limited insurance, financial resources, and support while coping with an unexpected illness and its aftermath. We focus on supportive care after treatment because distress can persist even when active therapy ends and survivors have many years of life ahead.

Psychological well-being is not simply absence of depression. It includes positive affect, satisfaction, meaning, and valued functioning. The proposed approach combines reducing negative emotional states with increasing positive experiences. Broaden-and-Build Theory suggests that positive emotions can expand coping and social resources; Revised Stress and Coping Theory emphasizes their restorative role during stress. Coping self-efficacy and social support are therefore proposed mediating pathways.

Prior interventions have taught positive-event awareness, savoring, gratitude, mindfulness, reappraisal, personal strengths, achievable goals, and kindness in several health contexts. Although multicomponent interventions can help, a package-level result does not identify which skills are necessary or useful for particular survivors. MOST offers an efficient way to select components before a definitive trial and reduce unnecessary burden. The web format accommodates remote access, common devices, standardized delivery, and manipulation of component combinations.

## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **Optimize EMPOWER by identifying useful components.** Test: (a) noticing/capitalizing on positive events and gratitude; (b) mindfulness; (c) positive reappraisal; (d) personal strengths and achievable goals; and (e) acts of kindness. Primary outcomes are positive affect, life satisfaction, depression, and anxiety. Secondary outcomes include physical functioning, fatigue, satisfaction with social roles, and health behaviors.
2. **Identify mediators and moderators.** We hypothesize mediation through coping self-efficacy and social support and moderation by age and gender. These analyses will help distinguish how components work and for whom effects differ.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

The team's LILAC pilot in metastatic breast cancer randomized 39 women to in-person intervention, online intervention, or an attention-matched in-person control. Seventy-nine percent remained through one-month follow-up and home practice averaged 5.28 days per week. The trial was not powered for between-group efficacy; within-intervention changes supported further investigation and online delivery had similar trajectories and acceptability to in-person delivery.

The team then adapted content with young adult survivors and clinical stakeholders, revising language, examples, and images for post-treatment experiences and physical limitations. EMPOWER was piloted in two single-arm waves. At Northwestern, 18 of 185 potentially eligible survivors enrolled; 13 completed the lessons and assessments, giving 72% adherence and retention. Recommendation ratings were high. Changes in depression, positive affect, and life satisfaction were not statistically significant, and the pilot was not designed to establish efficacy. Participants who left had poorer baseline well-being on several measures.

The second wave at Wake Forest and community/online sources contacted 99 survivors and enrolled 23 (23.2%). Reported retention was 66.7% and adherence 58.3%, with 11 participants still on study. Positive-affect and depression changes were moderate but nonsignificant; life satisfaction improved with a reported effect size of 0.71 (P=0.006). These preliminary observations support feasibility and refinement while leaving component efficacy to the proposed experiment. Multiple recruitment channels and dedicated staff improved accrual relative to the first wave.

## 5. Research design and methods

### 5.1 MOST framework and factorial allocation

Preparation has established theory and candidate components. The current project implements optimization: testing components and using their effects to assemble a refined package. Confirmatory evaluation is a later phase.


The experiment has five binary factors, producing 2^5 = 32 combinations with 11 participants assigned to each. Each component is received by 176 participants and withheld from 176, balanced with respect to the other components. Interactions can identify combinations with synergistic or antagonistic effects. The study statistician will administer block randomization with block size 64, after baseline assessment.

### 5.2 Eligibility and recruitment

Eligible participants can read English, have a prior cancer diagnosis other than basal cell skin carcinoma, were aged 15–39 at diagnosis, are currently 18–39, and are within five years after active treatment. Hormonal therapy or maintenance chemotherapy is permitted. A home computer or smartphone with reliable internet access is required. Exclusions are recurrence, multiple primary cancers, palliative/hospice care, or significant psychiatric history.

The Wake Forest Baptist Comprehensive Cancer Center and Northwestern's Robert H. Lurie Comprehensive Cancer Center will identify candidates through registries and EHR review. Provider permission will precede contact. Recruitment uses letters, follow-up calls, local organizations, flyers, and electronic/community postings. Interested survivors complete a REDCap eligibility screener, consent, and baseline questionnaire. Recruitment planning anticipates approximately 2,850 potentially eligible survivors over three years, based on roughly 550 and 400 post-treatment survivors seen annually at the two sites. A conservative 13% recruitment assumption supports a target of 352 within 36 months; the analytic section also reports an anticipated 16.5% eligible-and-interested proportion derived from pilot experience. Community partnership with Cancer Services provides an additional route if accrual lags.

### 5.3 Components, control content, and delivery

The five scheduled weeks correspond to five components:

**Table 2. EMPOWER skills and content**

| Week | Skills | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Positive events; capitalization; gratitude | Recognize positive experiences and associated affect; savor, relive or share them; appreciate people, experiences and resources. |
| 2 | Mindfulness | Practice awareness and nonjudgment. |
| 3 | Positive reappraisal | Identify constructive meanings or a silver lining during stress, including positive outcomes and less negative interpretations. |
| 4 | Personal strengths; achievable goals | Identify strengths and recent uses; learn characteristics of attainable goals and set goals for the week. |
| 5 | Acts of kindness | Practice small helpful acts and notice their effects on positive emotion. |

A week comprises one to two days of instructional material followed by practice and reporting, with daily home practice taking about 20–30 minutes. Diary-style exercises show prior responses alongside new entries. Participants cannot skip ahead but can revisit previous material. Up to eight weeks are allowed for the five-week program to accommodate interruptions.

Absent components will be replaced with lessons and practice intended to be inert, including factual journaling and reading about nutrition or stress responses. This provides credible control material and maintains engagement. The protocol acknowledges that if this content itself improves well-being, estimated EMPOWER effects would be smaller. The Wake Forest Research Information Systems Unit will host the intervention.

### 5.4 Measures and assessment timing

All 32 conditions have baseline and follow-ups at one week, one month, three months, and six months after intervention. Participants receive $20 per assessment, up to $100. The primary endpoint for analysis is the one-month follow-up. Primary outcomes are also assessed weekly during intervention to help distinguish component-related changes.

Depression and anxiety use NIH PROMIS computer-adaptive tests (CATs); positive affect and life satisfaction use NIH Toolbox CATs. Secondary PROMIS domains are physical functioning, fatigue, and satisfaction with social roles/responsibilities. Health behaviors include diet, exercise, sedentary activity, alcohol, and smoking. Potential mediators use PROMIS Self-Efficacy for Managing Emotions and Emotional Support measures. Demographic and clinical characteristics are collected at baseline. The common battery is expected to take less than thirty minutes, often fifteen to twenty, using adaptive assessments to limit burden.

### 5.5 Aim 1 analysis, selection, and power

For each well-being outcome, the initial model adjusts follow-up score for baseline and includes the five component indicators:

`Y_followup = intercept + beta0 × Y_baseline + beta1 × X1 + ... + beta5 × X5 + error`.

Interaction terms will test combined effects. Separate longitudinal mixed-effects models will include all observations, fixed effects for components, interactions, baseline score and time, and person-level random effects with an autoregressive covariance structure for repeated measurements. Contrasts will estimate component effects. Baseline imbalances will be examined and adjusted if needed; stage and cancer type are not planned stratification variables. Effect sizes will use baseline standard deviations. Similar models will analyze physical/social outcomes and health behaviors.

The protocol expects an effect size around 0.50 and uses this as its criterion for retaining components. Alpha is set at 0.10 because optimization findings will subsequently undergo confirmatory testing. With 352 recruited and a stated 72% retention assumption, the power section specifies a final sample of 256 at one month. At 80% power, the detectable main effect is 0.31 (stated 95% interval 0.06–0.56), and the detectable interaction is 0.62 (0.37–0.87). The study may therefore miss smaller interactions. 

### 5.6 Aim 2 analysis

Mediation analyses will examine coping self-efficacy and social support for components judged effective and retained for subsequent evaluation. Individual indirect effects and total effects will be estimated using the cited MacKinnon approach. Moderation will examine interactions with age and gender using the Aiken–West approach. The objective is to identify plausible pathways and differential effects that inform the optimized intervention, alongside the randomized component comparisons.

### 5.7 Design considerations

The project selects post-treatment survivors because distress persists and recruitment is feasible across a large survivor population. It includes multiple cancer types because the cited young-adult distress evidence did not consistently associate distress with type or stage. PROMIS and NIH Toolbox measures offer common scales across this heterogeneity. Initial optimization at two comprehensive cancer centers uses established infrastructure and recruitment experience; later evaluation will seek community-practice participation through NCORP. The web intervention is intended to scale, but effectiveness of the final package remains a question for that future trial.

## 6. Implementation, resources, and schedule

Salsman contributes young-adult oncology, recruitment, and outcome-measurement expertise; Moskowitz contributes positive-emotion intervention expertise. Wagner brings MOST trial experience, and collaborators cover psychosocial oncology, medical oncology, quantitative methods, survivorship, and eHealth.

The four-year schedule begins with study setup, followed by optimization and participant follow-up, overlapping analysis in later years, manuscript preparation, and planning for confirmatory research. The timetable places setup in the first two quarters, optimization from later Year 1 into Year 4, data analysis from Year 3 into Year 4, and dissemination and follow-on grant preparation in the final period.

## 7. Ethics and data practices

Potential participants provide consent through the recruitment and REDCap workflow after screening. Unique study IDs and PINs control questionnaire access. REDCap supports secure survey and database collection; the institution's research information systems team hosts the intervention. Eligibility excludes significant psychiatric histories and palliative/hospice care . The online format provides remotely accessible supportive-care content while preserving participants' ongoing clinical care.
