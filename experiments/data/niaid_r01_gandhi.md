# Hair Extensions: Using Hair Levels to Interpret Adherence, Effectiveness and Pharmacokinetics with Real-World Oral PrEP, the Vaginal Ring, and Injectables

**Principal investigator:** Monica Gandhi, University of California San Francisco. **Mechanism:** R01. **Collaborating studies:** SEARCH, MTN-025/HOPE and ACTG A5359.

## 1. Project overview

This project will evaluate antiretroviral concentrations in hair as measures of cumulative exposure, adherence and pharmacokinetics. Hair measurements will be combined with plasma, self-reported use and, for dapivirine, residual drug in vaginal rings. Three linked aims use existing clinical-study infrastructure: oral tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine (TDF/FTC) PrEP in East Africa; open-label dapivirine ring use; and long-acting cabotegravir in HIV-positive people receiving treatment after prior adherence difficulties. The third aim evaluates exposure monitoring in a treatment population to inform possible future use in injectable prevention.

The overarching hypothesis is that a long-term measure paired with shorter-term measurements will better describe exposure patterns and predict outcomes than any single snapshot. The planned contribution is an integrated monitoring approach spanning delivery methods and clinically relevant subgroups.

## 2. Background and rationale

Oral PrEP trials demonstrated that effectiveness depends strongly on actual drug use, self-report can disagree with pharmacological evidence, and daily dosing is difficult for some participants. Open-label delivery in high-incidence African settings raises questions about adherence during periods of risk, interruptions, and differences among young women, mobile populations, fisherfolk and serodiscordant couples. Sustained-release approaches reduce daily dosing demands but still require exposure monitoring: rings can be removed, and injectable concentrations may fall near or beyond the scheduled dosing interval.

Plasma generally reflects recent dosing and can be affected by dosing immediately before a visit. Hair incorporates drug from systemic circulation over weeks to months. Occipital hair growth of approximately 1 cm per month permits segmental analysis as an approximate exposure timeline. Collection requires no phlebotomy, and storage/shipping can occur at room temperature. Intracellular metabolite measures are valuable for phosphorylated drugs such as TFV/FTC, but do not confer the same extended window for cabotegravir or dapivirine. Residual ring drug is another longer-term measure, but can vary with use conditions and handling.

Combining short- and long-term measures will help characterize adherence patterns. High plasma with low hair exposure may indicate recent use without sustained use; detectable hair and undetectable plasma may indicate intermittent earlier exposure. Consistently high plasma and hair dapivirine with lower residual ring levels would support regular use. We will examine these patterns in relation to participant characteristics and outcomes.

## 3. Objectives and specific aims

1. **SEARCH:** Evaluate hair and plasma TFV/FTC as adherence biomarkers and predictors of HIV seroconversion and renal toxicity; identify exposure patterns and associated participant/delivery factors.
2. **MTN-025/HOPE:** Evaluate hair dapivirine as a long-term ring-exposure measure, compare it with plasma, residual-ring drug and self-report, and estimate concentrations associated with lower HIV acquisition risk.
3. **ACTG A5359:** Evaluate hair cabotegravir for pharmacokinetic monitoring, including correlations with trough plasma levels, segmental changes between injections and factors associated with exposure variability.

## 4. Preliminary work and basis

Directly observed TDF dosing at two, four and seven doses per week produced a strong linear dose–hair TFV relationship, supporting approximate dosing interpretation. Previous work found correlations between hair TFV/FTC and dried-blood-spot metabolites, associations between higher hair exposure and renal-function decline during PrEP, and associations between hair antiretroviral levels and virologic outcomes in treatment. Hair and plasma comparisons in VOICE suggested intermittent exposure patterns that would not be apparent from plasma alone.

Hair collection has been acceptable in several populations when introduced with training and participant education. In the early SEARCH PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit. A separate SEARCH-community study collected samples from 837 participants, with approximately 95% acceptability. Representative chromatograms demonstrate measurement of dapivirine and cabotegravir spiked into hair.

## 5. Research design and methods

### Shared specimen and analytic procedures

Approximately 50–100 strands will be cut close to the occipital scalp from beneath the upper hair layer. The distal end will be marked to preserve orientation, and the sample enclosed in foil and a labeled bag with desiccant. Staff will receive demonstrations for different hairstyles; samples will be stored at room temperature before batch shipment.

For TFV/FTC analysis, the proximal 1–2 cm will be cut into 1–2 mm pieces, and approximately 5 mg processed. The established extraction uses methanol/water with the specified acid, hydrazine salt and internal standard, followed by overnight incubation and LC–MS/MS. The assays have relative error and coefficients of variation below 15% for quality-control samples. Validated ranges are 0.002–0.400 ng/mg for TFV and 0.02–4.0 ng/mg for FTC. Dapivirine plasma assays use validated LC–MS/MS with a 20 pg/mL lower quantification limit; residual ring drug is extracted and measured by chromatography. Cabotegravir plasma measurements are performed centrally through ACTG.

### Aim 1: Oral PrEP in SEARCH

SEARCH phase 2 re-randomizes 32 communities in Uganda and Kenya to targeted PrEP in addition to universal streamlined HIV treatment, or streamlined treatment alone. This ancillary study focuses on approximately 6,400 PrEP recipients in the 16 intervention communities, about 400 per community. Eligibility derives from a risk profile developed in phase 1, self-identified risk, or a serodiscordant partnership. PrEP can be delivered in clinics or outside clinics according to preference or randomization where no preference exists.

After baseline, visits occur at four weeks and quarterly through 144 weeks. HIV rapid testing and symptom/toxicity checks occur at each visit. Hair and plasma are collected after four weeks and every 12 weeks. Covariates include demographics, region, marital and polygamy status, occupation, education, alcohol use, circumcision, stigma, sexually transmitted infections, delivery setting, self-perceived current/future HIV risk, and three-day recalled adherence. Creatinine-based clearance and eGFR support renal outcomes.

**Sampling.** Approximately 83,200 hair samples could be collected from 6,400 participants across 13 visits. Instead of assaying every specimen, all visits from all seroconverters and a simple random sample of non-seroconverters will be analyzed, with three controls per case. The expected 100 cases and 300 controls yield approximately 5,200 person-visits. Up to 100 additional participants with substantial renal decline contribute approximately 1,300 further visits. Known sampling fractions and certainty sampling of outcome-enriched groups will be incorporated in weights, so the same selected data can support both effectiveness and renal analyses without treating the sample as a simple random cohort.

**HIV outcome analysis.** A discrete-time Cox approach implemented through pooled logistic regression will relate TFV/FTC hair levels to seroconversion, controlling for risk factors. Hair levels will be compared with plasma and self-report, alone and together. Continuous/log-transformed and categorical levels, including quartiles, will be assessed. Fitted models will estimate exposure levels associated with high protection. The simplified one-observation power calculation provides at least 80% power for an odds ratio of at least 2.1 comparing the lowest hair-exposure quartile with the upper three, approximately 2.6% versus 1.2% cumulative seroconversion. Repeated observations supply additional information.

**Renal analysis.** Certainty sampling includes people reaching estimated creatinine clearance at or below 60 mL/min or declining more than 15% at any point. Linear mixed models will estimate percentage change, adjusting for age, sex, baseline clearance, hypertension, diabetes, concomitant drugs and site. Logistic models will assess threshold decline, with analyses repeated for eGFR and a 70 mL/min threshold. Participants with >15% decline will be selected for the additional sampling, and the outcome models will assess decline of ≥15%. Hair concentrations, plasma and self-report will be compared as explanatory variables; the directly observed dosing study will inform interpretation of TFV hair levels.

**Adherence analyses.** Scatterplots and Spearman correlations will compare measures. Linear mixed models will relate hair levels to participant and delivery factors, followed by forward-selected multivariable models to assess independent associations. Undetectable TFV/FTC will be set to the detection limit, with the limit added to values before log transformation to reduce emphasis on very small differences near zero. Back-transformed coefficients will express fold effects. Longitudinal combinations of hair and plasma will characterize patterns such as sustained use, intermittent use and recent dosing before a visit.

### Aim 2: Dapivirine ring in HOPE

HOPE is an open-label extension for former ASPIRE participants who remain HIV-negative, are not pregnant and choose enrollment. Approximately 1,800 women are expected from Malawi, South Africa, Uganda and Zimbabwe. A self-inserted ring containing 25 mg dapivirine will be replaced monthly over 12 months. Visits for hair/plasma/used-ring collection occur at months 1, 2, 3, 6, 9 and 12 and at a post-product visit one month later. Self-report covers days with the ring out and overall use.

Approximately 12,600 hair specimens could be collected. All seroconverters and three randomly sampled controls per case will be assayed across all visits. Based on approximately 60 seroconversions, 240 women across seven visits yield 1,680 samples. The stated power calculation is at least 80% for a lowest-quartile versus upper-quartiles relative risk of 2.9, corresponding to about 6.6% versus 2.3% seroconversion.

Weighted pooled logistic models will associate hair dapivirine with HIV acquisition and compare hair, plasma, residual-ring drug and self-report individually and in combinations. Adjustment variables include age, region, marital status, attitudes toward ring efficacy, alcohol/drug use, anxiety/depression, sexual activity, condom use, vaginal practices and STIs. Models will estimate exposure thresholds associated with lower acquisition risk. Among seroconverters, logistic regression will explore hair exposure and NNRTI resistance.

Spearman correlations and mixed-effects models will compare exposure measures and participant factors. The study will analyze patterns of agreement and disagreement, including age-related patterns, rather than equating ring receipt with continuous use. Unassayed specimens remain available for future studies.

### Aim 3: Cabotegravir exposure during long-acting treatment

ACTG A5359 studies HIV-positive adults with a history of poor treatment adherence. Participants first receive oral induction and incentives tied to virologic-control milestones. Those achieving suppression are randomized to continued oral treatment or monthly long-acting cabotegravir plus rilpivirine. This proposal includes only the long-acting arm, anticipated to include at least 150 participants.

Following oral induction through week 20, four weeks of oral cabotegravir 30 mg and rilpivirine precede injections from weeks 24 through 76. Missed and delayed visits are recorded. HIV RNA, clinical measures, CD4 counts, safety labs and substance-use measures are collected every four to eight weeks. Hair and plasma sampling is described at weeks 20, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72 and 76.

Scatterplots, correlation coefficients and repeated-measures linear mixed models will evaluate prediction of trough plasma concentration by average and segment-specific hair levels. Sex, race/ethnicity, BMI, age, renal/hepatic function and alcohol/drug use will be examined as exposure correlates. The modeled protective reference is four times the protein-adjusted IC90, 0.66 µg/mL in plasma, with an estimated corresponding hair value of 66 ng/mg. We will analyze exposure continuously and using these candidate cutoffs.

Hair will be segmented into 0.25 cm portions, interpreted approximately as weekly average exposure based on 1 cm growth per four weeks. Longer 2–3 cm samples after missed visits will describe extended dosing gaps. Comparisons of late segments will assess faster decline, and trajectories will distinguish consistently low exposure from rapid decline after initially adequate exposure. With 150 participants, a single-time-point correlation of 0.8 would have an estimated 95% interval of 0.73–0.85; repeated samples will improve characterization.

Exploratory random-effects logistic models will relate hair and plasma exposure to virologic suppression below 50 copies/mL, testing individual weekly segments, averages and candidate thresholds. These analyses will inform pharmacokinetic monitoring during treatment and future prevention studies.

## 6. Implementation, resources and schedule

The project adds specimen analysis and monitoring questions to the three parent studies. Gandhi leads the hair-measurement program; Havlir and the SEARCH team support the East African cohort, Baeten and MTN collaborators the ring study, and Castillo-Mancilla the injectable study. Benet and Gerona provide pharmacokinetic and analytic-chemistry expertise, with Bacchetti providing statistical design and analysis. Laboratory selection after outcome ascertainment concentrates assays on informative cases while retaining population inference through sampling weights.

SEARCH follow-up spans approximately three years, HOPE ring use one year plus exit, and the A5359 regimen through week 76. Study coordinators will receive collection training and monthly data-quality review. In the final project year, laboratory investigators plan capacity-building work with African laboratories associated with SEARCH and MTN-025. The findings will support pharmacokinetic monitoring and development of tailored adherence interventions.

## 7. Ethics and data practices

The added procedure is small-volume hair collection, usually taking approximately 90 seconds. Risks include minor accidental scalp injury, confidentiality concerns and inconvenience; completely bald scalps cannot provide the planned specimen, and axillary/pubic hair will not substitute. SEARCH and HOPE have parent-study ethics approvals and monitoring boards ; A5359 arrangements are being established . Consent includes hair collection. SEARCH permits ages 15–17 only with parental co-consent; HOPE and A5359 involve adults.

Collection quantity, labeling and orientation will be checked with sites, with retraining when necessary. Coordinators/statisticians will review completeness and collection rates monthly, and adverse events or confidentiality breaches will be reported through study leadership. Trial-network laboratory data systems will support specimen quality. Blinded hair-concentration datasets will be made publicly available after planned analyses/publications; findings will be shared with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders.
