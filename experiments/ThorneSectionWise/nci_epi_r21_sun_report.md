**Overall Assessment**

This R21 proposal aims to develop a statistical framework that jointly models bulk-tumor gene expression and DNA methylation to estimate immune-cell composition, and then uses these estimated fractions, with their uncertainty, as latent variables in a penalized survival model for cancer prognosis. The project addresses a genuine methodological gap: how to recover the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) from routine multi-omic data without ignoring the errors inherent in the deconvolution step. The intellectual merit lies in the principled integration of two data modalities in a single likelihood and the explicit propagation of quantification uncertainty into downstream association analyses. The work is scoped for a two-year exploratory development project, with deliverables including open-source R packages, a catalog of immune profiles across TCGA cancer types, and a comparative evaluation of joint versus two-stage prognostic modeling.

The proposal is methodologically coherent, the question is well-motivated, and the investigative team possesses the necessary statistical and computational expertise. The preliminary work provides a targeted proof-of-concept for the methylation deconvolution core. However, the review process has identified several cross-cutting limitations that collectively constrain the attainable contribution within the stated plan. The most consequential unresolved issues are: (1) the absence of a pilot demonstration that adding methylation to expression substantively improves deconvolution accuracy on a true external benchmark—the proposal's central innovative claim thus remains a hypothesis; (2) untested parametric approximations (log-normal moment matching for expression mixtures, normal assumption for methylation beta-values) whose bias under realistic tumor-mixture conditions is not addressed by a diagnostic strategy; and (3) a survival-model evaluation design that may demonstrate improved prediction without isolating the specific contribution of uncertainty propagation, thereby leaving the key inferential advance unverified. Supporting limitations include reliance on pre-estimated tumor purity of unknown quality, underspecified validation datasets, and unresolved computational-scaling questions.

These are not fatal flaws for an exploratory project; the concept remains worthwhile, and the work is likely to produce useful methods and descriptive resources even under conservative assumptions about the multi-omic advantage. The proposal is therefore not judged unsupportable in principle. Rather, the current plan does not yet provide a route to robustly substantiating its own core claims within the project period. A supportable scope would need to front-load a definitive multi-omic validation experiment and include design elements that distinguish the sources of any predictive gain. Pending such revisions, the proposal cannot be recommended for support as submitted.

The ethical and safety profile is entirely unproblematic. The management and resource plan is plausible but underspecified in operational detail; those gaps accentuate the feasibility risks but are not independently decisive.

### Scientific Value: B (Confidence: B)

The proposed work targets a real and consequential knowledge gap: how to extract reliable immune-cell composition estimates from bulk tumor omics and use them for prognostic modeling without discarding estimation uncertainty. The attainable knowledge is clearly defined at the project’s modest development stage. Successful execution would yield (a) a validated likelihood-based estimation tool that integrates RNA expression and DNA methylation, (b) refined, tumor-context-adjusted cell-type-specific molecular profiles, (c) estimated immune-cell fractions across the TCGA pan-cancer atlas, and (d) an assessment of whether the joint modeling framework improves survival prediction. These outputs constitute a useful methodological resource and a descriptive catalog for the cancer-immunology community.

Two features elevate the scientific value beyond routine deconvolution optimization. The joint expression–methylation likelihood directly confronts the known difficulty that blood-derived reference profiles may misrepresent cell states in the tumor microenvironment. The survival model’s treatment of cell fractions as latent variables, rather than fixed plug-in estimates, is a principled attack on the errors-in-variables problem that pervades two-stage association studies. The project also recognizes that TCGA samples may underrepresent highly infiltrated tumors because of tissue-preparation requirements, which appropriately bounds the generalizability of the resulting descriptive findings without undermining the methodological contribution.

The limitations that temper the attainable value are primarily methodological and are discussed under Rigor. The absence of a pilot experiment showing a definitive multi-omic deconvolution advantage means the key addition—methylation—may contribute less discriminatory power than the narrative implies. The survival evaluation may yield a prediction tool whose performance gain over simpler alternatives is modest and whose origin (better point estimates versus genuine uncertainty quantification) remains ambiguous. The project would still deliver a method and a resource, but the magnitude of the practical improvement over existing single-modality, two-stage pipelines is uncertain.

### Innovation: B (Confidence: B)

The proposal distinguishes its contribution from prior work along three dimensions:

1. Joint expression–methylation mixture likelihood, in which blood-derived reference profiles serve as starting values but are then jointly re-estimated using the tumor samples themselves, with multiplicative cell-size/transcriptional-activity factors identifiable from cross-modality discrepancies.
2. A formal two-component mixture that separates informative features from noise features, intended to improve robustness when blood references and in-situ tumor profiles diverge.
3. A latent-variable survival model with a fused-lasso penalty ordered by hierarchical cell-type clustering to stabilize regression coefficients and borrow strength across transcriptionally or epigenetically similar immune populations.

These are incremental but coherent innovations, each building on well-established statistical frameworks (mixture deconvolution, penalized regression, EM algorithms). The novelty resides in their combination and their adaptation to the specific problem of immune deconvolution and prognosis. None of the individual elements is claimed to be a radical conceptual departure. The proposal’s explicit citation of Newman et al. (2015, CIBERSORT), Reinius et al. (2012), Accomando et al. (2014), and Lin et al. (2014) demonstrates a clear understanding of the relevant prior art.

The primary limitation on the innovation assessment is that the distinctive contribution—the added value of methylation beyond expression alone for cell-type resolution—remains a plausible but unsubstantiated hypothesis at this stage. The preliminary data show correlated but nonidentical fraction estimates from the two modalities, which is consistent with either a genuine multi-omic benefit or with differential noise structures. The proposal itself acknowledges that methylation may help separate closely related populations, but provides no targeted comparison of single- versus dual-modality deconvolution on a sample with known ground-truth proportions. Consequently, the innovation is well-conceived but not yet empirically anchored. If methylation contributes only marginal additional information in practice, the project would remain a solid expression-alone deconvolution method with a joint survival model, but its degree of advancement over existing tools would be more limited.

### Rigor: C (Confidence: B)

The core inference challenge—estimating latent cell fractions and their survival associations—is met with a design that is logically structured and proportionate to an exploratory development stage, but several methodological commitments introduce uncertainty that the current evaluation plan does not adequately resolve.

**Strengths.** The two-part validation strategy (simulated mixtures from measured reference profiles and experimental benchmarks with flow cytometry or known proportions) directly tests estimation accuracy. The preliminary known-mixture experiment (six samples, five cell types, 362 CpGs) demonstrates lower residual sum of squares and greater robustness to probe perturbation for the maximum-likelihood method compared to non-negative least squares, providing a well-targeted proof-of-concept for the mixture-model framework. The coordinate-ascent EM algorithm and the compositional-data transformation for the survival predictor are defensible, standard approaches. The use of BIC for penalty selection is conventional and appropriate.

**Decisive limitations.** Five material concerns affect the strength of the intended inferences.

1. *Unvalidated parametric approximations.* The proposal approximates a convolution of log-normal expression components with a single moment-matched log-normal, and treats methylation beta-values as normally distributed. The rationale is computational efficiency. However, the quality of these approximations under realistically heterogeneous tumor mixtures—where a small lymphocyte population coexists with a large, transcriptionally hyperactive tumor clone—is not assessed. If the log-normal approximation distorts the tails of the expression mixture, fraction estimates for low-abundance cell types could be systematically biased. No diagnostic framework or simulation experiment is described to bound this error. The proposal’s primary contribution rests on accurate quantification; an unchecked approximation injects uncontrolled uncertainty into that contribution.

2. *The core multi-omic claim is not tested by a decisive experiment.* The proposal asserts that joint expression–methylation modeling improves immune-cell resolution over expression alone. However, the evaluation plan does not include a comparison of single-modality versus joint-modality deconvolution on an external dataset where the true fractions are known (by flow cytometry or artificial mixing) and where both RNA and methylation have been measured on the same samples. The planned simulations will necessarily reflect the model’s own assumptions; the flow-cytometry benchmarks are not specified and may lack matched multi-omic data. Without this experiment, the central innovative claim cannot be rigorously substantiated within the project.

3. *The survival evaluation may not distinguish the specific benefit of uncertainty propagation.* The primary comparison metric is the cross-validated C-index for the joint model versus two-stage alternatives. A superior C-index could arise from (a) more accurate point estimates of the fractions produced by joint estimation, (b) the regularizing effect of the fused-lasso penalty, or (c) genuine propagation of deconvolution uncertainty into the survival likelihood. The proposal does not describe an analysis that isolates the contribution of (c), for example by comparing the full model against a variant that uses the same joint fraction estimates but treats posterior modes as fixed covariates. Therefore, the study may produce a better prognostic tool without establishing the inferential principle that motivates the entire Aim 2.

4. *Identifiability of cell-size factors under model misspecification.* The method infers multiplicative activity factors from systematic discrepancies between RNA- and methylation-based fraction estimates, claiming identifiability when the number of samples exceeds the number of cell types. This condition is necessary but not sufficient when discrepancies also arise from differential reference-profile quality, platform noise, or the parametric approximations noted above. No simulation experiment is proposed to test whether these factors are recoverable under realistic multi-source misspecification. If they are not, the re-estimated expression reference profiles—a key deliverable of the project—would be convolved mixtures of genuine biological signal and modeling artifact.

5. *Dependence on pre-estimated tumor purity of unverified quality.* The survival model includes log-transformed tumor purity as a covariate. The proposal does not discuss the source, accuracy, or cross-cohort consistency of these purity estimates. Biased or noisy purity measurements would propagate into both the latent-fraction estimation and the survival regression, compromising the very uncertainty quantification the method is designed to improve. No sensitivity analysis or quality-filtering strategy is proposed.

The project plan is adequate for developing and characterizing a new method through extensive simulations and within-TCGA cross-validation. It is insufficient, in its current form, to provide proportionate support for the inference that joint multi-omic deconvolution with uncertainty propagation substantively improves immune-cell quantification and prognostic modeling compared to best-practice single-modality, two-stage approaches.

### Feasibility: C (Confidence: B)

The feasibility of delivering the stated aims is reasonably supported by the investigator expertise, the reliance on existing public data, and the logical two-year schedule. However, several operational and methodological dependencies remain unresolved in the proposal text, and their collective weight reduces confidence that all planned objectives can be accomplished as described.

**Factors supporting feasibility.** The PI (Wei Sun) and collaborator (Michael Wu) bring directly relevant expertise in statistical genomics, expression and methylation analysis, and method development. The project uses exclusively existing, publicly available datasets (TCGA, ENCODE, published immune references), and the preliminary work demonstrates that the team already has access to and is actively analyzing some of these resources. Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center is a well-resourced institution where the required computational infrastructure would ordinarily exist. The two-year timeline, with Aim 1 (deconvolution) in year 1 and Aim 2 (survival modeling) in year 2, is structurally sensible; the sequential dependency between aims is explicitly acknowledged. The plan to release open-source R packages with C/C++ back-ends is a standard, achievable dissemination strategy for computational statistics.

**Factors constraining feasibility.**

1. *Unresolved validation-data dependencies.* The proposal states that method accuracy will be assessed against flow-cytometry-characterized samples and published measurements of immune cells isolated from the tumor microenvironment. These datasets are not named, their sample sizes are not specified, and it is not confirmed that they contain matched bulk expression and methylation from the same specimens. Such multi-omic gold-standard resources are rare. If suitable datasets cannot be identified, the most stringent empirical validation will be unavailable, shifting the evidential burden almost entirely onto simulations that share the model’s own assumptions.

2. *Undocumented reference methylation data availability.* The methylation reference is to be assembled from six published studies of sorted immune cells. The proposal describes normalization procedures but does not state whether these datasets are already archived locally or remain to be acquired and harmonized. Cross-study methylation normalization is a known source of batch effects that can be labor-intensive to resolve; this could compress the time available for core method development in year 1.

3. *Computational scaling unexamined.* The coordinate-ascent algorithm operates over thousands of genes and hundreds of thousands of CpGs across hundreds of tumor samples, iterating between fraction estimation and profile re-estimation. No estimate of per-iteration cost, memory footprint, or expected number of iterations is provided, nor is a fallback strategy (e.g., pre-filtering to a reduced marker set, stochastic-gradient methods) outlined. While capable computational statisticians can typically engineer a workable solution, the absence of any scaling analysis means that the practical run-time on a TCGA-scale dataset is an unknown risk. A first-year failure to achieve reliable convergence would directly block the planned pan-cancer fraction catalog and the survival modeling in Aim 2.

4. *Tumor purity input quality unaddressed.* As noted under Rigor, the model’s performance depends on externally supplied tumor-purity estimates whose accuracy and consistency are not discussed. This is an operational dependency because poor-quality purity inputs could necessitate iterative re-fitting and re-evaluation, adding unplanned work.

Feasibility is therefore contingent on early resolution of the validation-data search, the reference-data harmonization, and the computational performance of the full-scale algorithm. The project remains probably executable, but the delivery risk is material and not fully mitigated by the current plan.

### Ethics and Safety: A (Confidence: A)

The project is exclusively computational, using de-identified, publicly available omics and clinical data from well-governed repositories (TCGA, ENCODE, published reference datasets). No new participant recruitment, specimen collection, animal experimentation, or environmental release is proposed. The stated ethical-oversight framework—Exemption 4 status, data stored on secured institutional machines with access restricted to project personnel—is appropriate and proportionate for secondary analysis of de-identified human data. The re-identification risk is negligible. The proposal’s acknowledgment that TCGA may underrepresent highly infiltrated tumors is a methodological sampling-frame issue, not an ethical one. I see no material ethical or safety risks that would threaten the acceptability of the work.

### Management: C (Confidence: C)

The proposal identifies a capable investigator team and assigns broad roles: the PI for statistical genomics and software development, a collaborator for methylation and association methods, and clinical collaborators for colorectal- and lung-cancer applications. The two-year sequential schedule is logical and consistent with an R21 exploratory scope. The commitment to open-source software release is explicit.

However, the supplied text lacks details on project-management structure. There is no description of meeting frequency, division of specific coding or analysis tasks, data-management or version-control protocols, or contingency planning for the dependencies noted under Feasibility. For a small, co-located methods-development team, informal coordination may be adequate, and the absence of such detail may reflect the narrative format rather than a genuine gap. Nevertheless, the evidence available for evaluating management is thin. The risk that informal coordination leads to inefficiency or delay is probably low, but it cannot be excluded on the basis of the supplied text. This uncertainty limits confidence in the management judgment, rather than indicating a decisive failure.

### Recommendation: Current proposal insufficient for support (revise)

The project addresses a clearly defined, worthwhile methodological problem, and the proposed solution is conceptually sound. The investigative team has the necessary expertise, and the work is likely to produce useful computational tools and descriptive immune-atlas resources even under conservative assumptions about the added value of methylation. However, the current plan does not provide a route to rigorously substantiating its own primary claims—that joint expression–methylation modeling yields a meaningful improvement in immune-cell resolution and that the joint survival model’s advantage stems specifically from uncertainty propagation. These claims are central to the project’s scientific value and innovation as presented.

A supportable scope would require: (1) front-loading a definitive pilot experiment that compares single-modality and joint-modality deconvolution on an external sample set with known ground-truth composition and both omics measured; (2) incorporating a diagnostic framework to assess bias from the parametric approximations under realistic tumor-mixture conditions; and (3) designing the survival-model evaluation to isolate the contribution of uncertainty propagation, for example by benchmarking against a variant that uses posterior modes as fixed covariates. With these elements in place, the proposal would offer a proportionate test of its mechanistic hypotheses and would merit support as a well-specified development project. In its current form, these inferential gaps leave the evidence base insufficient to support the proposed work.
