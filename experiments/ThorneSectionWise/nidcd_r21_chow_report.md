# Comprehensive Review

**Proposal:** Neural correlates of persistent developmental stuttering and recovery in childhood using continuous-speech fMRI  
**Review date:** 2026-10-28  
**Reviewer:** Scientific project reviewer (synthesis)

---

## Overall Assessment

This proposal aims to characterize the neural correlates of persistent developmental stuttering and recovery in children aged 5–11 using continuous-speech fMRI. The work addresses a scientifically important question at the developmental stage when stuttering trajectories most often diverge. The project's primary strengths lie in its well-motivated research question, its access to a rigorously characterized longitudinal cohort that solves the most challenging recruitment problem in developmental stuttering research, and its methodological ambition to acquire interpretable fMRI data during naturalistic continuous speech in young children. The denoising strategy is supported by substantial preliminary evidence in resting-state pediatric data and adult speech tasks, and the analytical design that separates fluent-speech activity from stuttering-event activity is well-matched to the core inferences the proposal seeks to draw.

The principal limitation affecting the overall scientific value is the exclusively cross-sectional design, which the proposal itself acknowledges. This constrains the recovery-related inferences (Aim 2) to correlational claims about the recovered state and precludes attribution of observed neural differences to a recovery process or mechanism. A secondary concern is that the power analysis relies on adult effect sizes whose transferability to the target pediatric population is uncertain. The management plan is logically structured but provides insufficient detail on the size of the accessible participant pool within the existing cohort, leaving a material uncertainty about recruitment feasibility. Ethics and safety procedures are appropriate and grounded in the team's prior experience, though some practical details of the child assent and distress-signalling process are not specified in the supplied text. The innovation is genuine but incremental, consisting principally of applying established methods and paradigms to a new and clinically important population.

On balance, the proposal describes a worthwhile scientific direction with a supportable core scope already identifiable. The cross-sectional descriptive aims (Aim 1a, Aim 1b) are well-justified and supported by the design, while the mechanistic framing of Aim 2 exceeds what the design can warrant. Conditional support is appropriate: the project should proceed with the explicit understanding that Aim 2 yields characterizations of the recovered state rather than evidence about recovery processes.

## Evaluation by Criterion

### 1. Scientific Value

**Rating:** B (Good)  
**Confidence:** B (High)

The proposal addresses a question of genuine scientific and clinical importance: what are the neural correlates of persistent developmental stuttering and recovery during childhood continuous speech? Stuttering is fundamentally a disorder of connected speech, yet prior developmental neuroimaging has relied primarily on rest or simple speaking tasks with limited ecological validity. The project's immediate knowledge contribution—whole-brain functional maps of speech-motor and monitoring circuits in a developmental population, obtained without ionizing radiation—would constitute a substantive descriptive resource. The mixed block/event design that separates sustained speech activity from transient stuttering events is methodologically apt and would produce data that pure block designs or rest studies cannot yield. The explicit treatment of linguistic covariates (speech rate, utterance length, semantic diversity, syntactic complexity) strengthens the interpretability of group comparisons by addressing a known confound in naturalistic speech paradigms.

A further contribution lies in the methodological demonstration of a spatial ICA denoising pipeline for continuous-speech fMRI in young children. The preliminary data showing that sICA reduces the movement–BOLD relationship across over 45,000 volumes from 95 young children provide strong feasibility evidence specific to the target age range. If successful, this validated analysis approach would be adoptable by other developmental speech neuroimaging studies.

The contribution must be calibrated to the study's cross-sectional nature. The proposal states that Aim 2 characterizes "current group differences" and acknowledges that longitudinal work would be needed to track developmental trajectories. The design can establish whether recovered children show a functional organization closer to that of controls, and whether persistent children differ from both, but it cannot establish that observed neural differences preceded or caused recovery. A finding of greater right-frontal activity in recovered children, for example, would remain ambiguous with respect to causality—it could reflect a compensatory mechanism, a consequence of recovery, a pre-existing trait, or a maturational difference unrelated to stuttering. The knowledge contribution is therefore appropriately described as an initial characterization of the functional architecture of persistence and recovery, not as mechanistic evidence.

The proposal also tests whether adult functional findings from the same research group—including fluent-speech left perisylvian reduction and stuttering-event increases in putamen, SMA, and ventral premotor cortex—are already present in childhood. The preliminary observation that structural connectivity patterns in childhood do not simply mirror adult functional findings underscores the importance of this direct functional test in the pediatric population.

**Strengths:**
- Well-motivated question targeting the developmental window when stuttering trajectories diverge.
- Design that separates fluent-speech from stuttering-event activity provides data not available from rest or block-design studies.
- Methodological contribution in validating sICA denoising for developmental continuous-speech fMRI.
- Linguistic covariate measurement addresses a known confound in naturalistic speech paradigms.

**Limitations:**
- Cross-sectional design restricts recovery inferences to correlational claims about the recovered state.
- The study cannot distinguish compensatory processes from normalization or pre-existing traits.

### 2. Innovation

**Rating:** B (Good)  
**Confidence:** B (High)

The proposal makes several incremental but genuine advances beyond existing work. The use of a narrative storytelling task (*Frog, Where Are You?*) during fMRI in children as young as five, combined with an overlearned-speech control, represents a meaningful advance in ecological validity for developmental stuttering neuroimaging. The team's pilot data from an eight-year-old child showing perisylvian activation consistent with expectations provides direct, though single-case, demonstration of feasibility. The systematic application of sICA denoising to separate speech-correlated artifact from BOLD signal during continuous speech in children who stutter is not novel in the method itself (Xu et al., 2014), but its deployment in this specific population and task context would be a meaningful methodological demonstration that opens whole-brain access, including subcortical structures, without radiation exposure.

Identifying neural activity associated with individual stuttering moments within children (Aim 1b) is a technically demanding and rarely attempted measurement in pediatric populations. The adult precedent and the explicit power calculation for the within-subject contrast increase confidence that this aim is attainable. Such within-subject data could help disentangle state and trait neural signatures of stuttering, a distinction that block designs or rest studies cannot make.

The innovation is appropriately characterized as a novel measurement and population application rather than a conceptual breakthrough. The theoretical framework (left anterior/posterior monitoring circuits, right-frontal compensation, motor execution loop) is drawn from existing models (e.g., DIVA; Tourville & Guenther, 2011) and from the group's own prior adult work. The project builds closely on the team's adult continuous-speech fMRI study and their childhood white-matter diffusion study. The increment, while logical and well-justified, is relatively narrow. The proposal would have been strengthened by more explicit engagement with independent childhood stuttering neuroimaging work—for example, the cited Chang and Zhu (2013) resting-state study—to clarify what functional question remains unanswered by prior pediatric studies and why the continuous-speech approach is specifically necessary to resolve it.

A notable unexploited opportunity is the integration of the proposed functional data with existing structural data from the same longitudinal cohort. The team's prior finding that recovered children show no left inferior-frontal FA reduction but greater right inferior-frontal FA provides structural predictions for the functional hypotheses, yet no formal multimodal integration analysis is planned in the current aims.

**Strengths:**
- Ecologically valid continuous-speech task in young children, piloted in the target age range.
- Within-subject stuttering event analysis is technically demanding and rarely attempted in children.
- Application of validated denoising to a new population opens whole-brain access without radiation.

**Limitations:**
- The increment is narrow, relying heavily on the group's internal research trajectory.
- Limited engagement with independent childhood stuttering neuroimaging literature to contextualize novelty.
- Opportunity for multimodal integration with existing structural data from the same cohort is not exploited.

### 3. Rigor

**Rating:** B (Good)  
**Confidence:** B (High)

The proposal provides a well-specified analytical framework matched to the intended inferences. Participant criteria are concrete and reproducible: persistence requires serial SSI-4 scores above 10 in years 3 and 4; recovery requires a decline from intake score of 10 or above to below 10 at later assessments; controls must score below 6 with no family history or parental concern. The requirement that pCWS show greater than 4.4% stuttering during quiet speech assessment addresses the known problem of scanner-induced stuttering suppression, and the explicit minimum of ten in-scanner stuttering instances for Aim 1b's event analysis provides a clear inclusion threshold. The mixed block/event GLM design, with block regressors for sustained speech and event regressors for stuttering instances, directly supports the separation of fluent-speech from stuttering-event activity and prevents contamination of the fluent-speech estimates used in Aims 1a and 2. Blinding of analysts to diagnosis and permutation-based multiple-comparison correction reduce the risk of bias and inflated error rates.

Several limitations affect the strength of the supported inferences. The power analysis uses adult effect sizes (between-group difference 0.7, SD 0.65; within-stuttering difference 0.58, SD 0.74), and the proposal acknowledges that these "may not transfer directly" to children. If true effect sizes in 5–11-year-olds are smaller—plausible given greater variability in developing brains, different stuttering histories, and a different speech task—the study may be underpowered for the key comparisons, particularly the recovery contrasts in Aim 2. The recruitment buffer from 15 to 20 per group provides only modest protection against this uncertainty. Null results in Aim 2 could therefore reflect inadequate sensitivity rather than genuine absence of neural differences.

The cross-sectional nature of the design is acknowledged but the inferential language occasionally exceeds what the design can support. The proposal frames Aim 2 as identifying "activity characterizing recovery," yet a cross-sectional comparison can establish only correlates of the recovered state, not evidence that any observed neural features participated in a recovery process. This limitation does not prevent valuable descriptive findings, but it constrains the mechanistic conclusions the data can directly warrant.

Additional methodological concerns include the lack of experimental control over linguistic content in the storytelling task. Although the proposal plans to extract speech rate, utterance length, semantic diversity, and syntactic complexity measures and include principal components as covariates, residual linguistic differences could persist and confound between-group comparisons in left perisylvian regions that subserve broader language functions beyond speech motor control. The assumption of a 25% reduction in in-scanner stuttering frequency from the quiet-speech rate is stated but not empirically validated for this task and scanner environment in children. If actual suppression is larger, the number of stuttering events for Aim 1b could fall below the usable threshold more often than anticipated. The sICA denoising validation relies on resting-state pediatric data and adult continuous-speech comparisons; child-specific validation of denoising performance during continuous speech in the scanner is limited to a single eight-year-old participant.

**Strengths:**
- Reproducible, quantitative participant criteria.
- Mixed block/event GLM well-matched to the separation of fluent-speech and stuttering-event activity.
- Blinding and permutation-based multiple-comparison correction.

**Limitations:**
- Adult effect sizes may not transfer to children; buffer against this uncertainty is modest.
- Cross-sectional design constrains mechanistic inferences for Aim 2; proposal language occasionally exceeds this.
- Uncontrolled linguistic variation in the storytelling task may confound left perisylvian group comparisons.
- In-scanner stuttering frequency assumption not validated for this task and age group.
- Child-specific denoising validation for continuous speech is limited to a single pilot participant.

### 4. Feasibility

**Rating:** B (Good)  
**Confidence:** B (High)

The project's feasibility is strongly supported by several factors. The team's expertise directly matches the core demands: the PI contributes fMRI methods and sICA denoising expertise; Chang provides access to an existing longitudinal cohort, resolving the most critical recruitment challenge; Ratner adds specialist speech-analysis knowledge. The use of an established cohort with documented stuttering histories and serial SSI-4 scores is a substantial feasibility advantage that mitigates the high-probability failure point of de novo recruitment. The imaging equipment (GE MR750 3T scanner, optical microphone, MR-compatible camera, E-Prime synchronization) is stated as available. The technical pipeline has been tested on analogous data: over 45,000 resting-state volumes in children demonstrating sICA denoising performance, adult PET-fMRI convergence for continuous speech, and a successful pilot in an eight-year-old child showing interpretable perisylvian activation.

The three-year timeline partitions work into logical, overlapping phases: recruitment and scanning (months 1–18), analysis and presentations (months 12–24), and manuscript preparation and dissemination (months 24–36). The contingency plans—image scrubbing for volumes with abrupt motion exceeding 9 mm, rescheduling with extra training for task compliance failure, and exclusion of children with fewer than ten stuttering instances from Aim 1b only rather than from all analyses—are appropriate and well-specified.

Two material uncertainties affect the feasibility assessment. First, the schedule's success depends almost entirely on recruitment rate, yet the proposal does not state how many potentially eligible children are currently enrolled in the cohort, their ages relative to the required 5–11-year window, or their expected availability over the next 18 months. If the pool is deep, scanning 60 children in 18 months is reasonable. If the pool is only marginally sufficient, attrition or failure to meet the in-scanner stuttering threshold could compress the analysis and publication phases. Second, the proposal describes a combination of three domains of expertise (imaging, cohort access, speech analysis) as a single workflow but does not detail a formal coordination plan with regular meetings, data integration milestones, or a dedicated project manager. The arrangement is plausible given the focused scope, but the absence of explicit coordination mechanisms introduces an unquantified management risk.

**Strengths:**
- Team expertise directly matches core demands.
- Established longitudinal cohort solves the most critical recruitment challenge.
- Technical pipeline tested on relevant preliminary data, including a pediatric pilot.
- Well-specified contingency plans for motion, task compliance, and insufficient stuttering events.

**Limitations:**
- Size of the accessible participant pool within the cohort is not specified.
- Formal coordination plan across the three expertise domains is not detailed.

### 5. Ethics and Safety

**Rating:** B (Good)  
**Confidence:** B (High)

The proposal describes a set of procedures that are broadly appropriate for the population and methods. Parental informed consent and child verbal or written assent are standard, and the familiarization procedure before scanning supports genuine understanding. Standard MRI screening, hearing protection, breaks, and the option to stop address the main physical and psychological risks. The offer that parents may remain nearby or with a distressed child is a practical mitigation. The data protection plan—coded identifiers, locked or password-protected storage, controlled collaborator access, and de-identified data release under a formal data-use agreement within one year of project closeout—is proportionate to the sensitivity of the material. Voice recordings and facial video are re-identifiable in principle, and the restriction to de-identified data with controlled access is appropriate.

The exclusion rule that spares children who do not meet the in-scanner stuttering threshold from undergoing a scan that cannot address Aim 1b has an ethical dimension: it avoids a non-contributory scanning session. The scientific rationale—acquiring functional data without ionizing radiation during the developmental period when stuttering trajectories diverge—provides a plausible justification for conducting the study in this age group.

Several practical details that bear on participant welfare are not specified in the supplied text. The content of the child-assent explanation, particularly whether it covers the specific experience of fMRI (loud noise, confined space, requirement to remain still while speaking), is not described. No mechanism for signalling distress during scanning (e.g., squeeze-ball, periodic intercom query) is mentioned, though a stop-without-penalty rule is stated. The potential influence of the ongoing longitudinal-cohort relationship on a child's willingness to withdraw is not discussed. The terms of the data-use agreement and the plan for retention or destruction of facial video after speech-coding are not specified.

These unspecified details are not evidence of misconduct or neglect; they are points on which the provided narrative remains general. None creates a disproportionate risk given the low-intervention nature of the study and the team's documented prior experience with the same cohort and similar methods. The questions can be resolved in the detailed protocol submitted to the institutional review board.

**Strengths:**
- Appropriate consent and assent procedures with familiarization.
- Standard MRI safety measures and distress management.
- Proportionate data protection plan with de-identified sharing under data-use agreement.
- Exclusion rule avoids non-contributory scanning sessions.

**Limitations:**
- Content of child-assent explanation for fMRI-specific experience not specified.
- Distress-signalling mechanism during scanning not described.
- Management of ongoing cohort relationship to avoid undue influence not discussed.
- Video retention and data-use agreement terms not detailed.

### 6. Management

**Rating:** C (Moderate)  
**Confidence:** C (Moderate)

The management plan is logically structured but provides insufficient detail to support full confidence in its adequacy. The team composition matches the project's core demands: the PI contributes fMRI methods and sICA denoising expertise, Chang provides cohort access, and Ratner provides speech-analysis knowledge. The combination of these three domains is necessary for the project's success. However, the proposal describes this as a combination of expertise rather than providing a formal coordination plan with specified meeting schedules, data integration milestones, or a designated project manager. The arrangement is professionally plausible but the absence of explicit coordination mechanisms leaves the judgment resting on an inference of effective collaboration rather than on stated management procedures.

The three-year timeline is partitioned into logical phases with overlapping analysis and data collection. The schedule's feasibility depends primarily on recruitment rate, yet the proposal does not specify the number of potentially eligible children in the existing cohort, their ages relative to the required window, or their expected availability. This is the most significant unspecified management detail, as it affects whether the 18-month scanning window is realistic. The proposal also does not include a risk mitigation table or explicit recruitment contingencies beyond rescheduling individual children.

The stated deliverables—conference presentations and multiple manuscripts—are appropriate for a project of this scope, and the inclusion of dissemination to speech-language pathology communities acknowledges the translational intent. The timeline for data sharing (within one year of project closeout) is specified.

**Strengths:**
- Team expertise directly matches core project demands.
- Logical three-year timeline with overlapping phases.
- Appropriate dissemination plan including clinical community outreach.

**Limitations:**
- Formal coordination mechanisms across expertise domains not described.
- Size of the accessible participant pool within the cohort not specified.
- No explicit risk mitigation plan or recruitment contingencies beyond individual rescheduling.

## Recommendation

The proposal describes a worthwhile scientific direction with a supportable core scope already identifiable. The descriptive aims (Aim 1a and Aim 1b) are well-justified and supported by the design, the denoising approach, and the available preliminary data. The recovery-related aim (Aim 2) pursues an important question but the cross-sectional design and the mechanistic framing create a gap between what the study can deliver and what the proposal claims it will establish. The project can proceed productively with the explicit understanding that Aim 2 yields characterizations of the recovered state—neural correlates associated with recovery—rather than evidence about recovery processes, mechanisms, or causal direction.

The primary conditions affecting this recommendation concern the management plan: the size of the accessible participant pool should be confirmed as adequate for the 18-month scanning window, and a formal coordination plan across the three expertise domains should be developed. These conditions are resolvable and do not require redesign of the scientific aims.
