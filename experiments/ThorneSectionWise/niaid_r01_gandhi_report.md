# Comprehensive Review
**Proposal Title:** (inferred from text: Hair-Based Adherence-Exposure Monitoring Across Oral, Topical and Injectable Antiretroviral Delivery)
**Review Date:** 2026-10-28

## Overall Assessment

This proposal aims to develop and validate hair-based antiretroviral concentration monitoring as a versatile adherence-exposure biomarker platform across three distinct delivery modes—oral tenofovir/emtricitabine PrEP, dapivirine vaginal ring, and injectable cabotegravir—by embedding specimen collection and analysis within three established clinical studies. The core scientific contribution is the systematic, multi-cohort demonstration that combining a long-term cumulative measure (hair) with shorter-term measures (plasma, residual ring drug, self-report) can characterise adherence *patterns* that no single snapshot provides, and can estimate exposure levels associated with protection from HIV acquisition. 

The proposal has clear strengths: it addresses a question of genuine practical and scientific importance; it leverages existing, well-characterised cohorts with rich outcome and covariate data, achieving substantial efficiency; the case–control sampling design is statistically appropriate and resource-conscious; the preliminary work demonstrates high acceptability of hair collection in the target populations and established analytical validity for the tenofovir assay; and the management plan assigns clear roles to named experts with relevant track records. The integration across oral, topical and injectable delivery in a single programme is a novel and ambitious scope.

However, the proposal has material limitations that affect what can be confidently concluded and whether the plan, as submitted, is sufficient to realise its stated goals. The most consequential concern is an ethics and safety narrative that remains procedurally generic and fails to engage deeply with the cultural meaning of hair collection in the diverse study populations, or to specify proportionate safeguards for the uniquely sensitive, long-timeline behavioural data that segmental hair analysis will generate. This is not a minor documentation gap; it raises unanswered questions about the validity of informed consent and participant protection that bear directly on the feasibility and ethical soundness of the entire project. Additional cross-cutting concerns include: (1) the analytical assays for dapivirine and cabotegravir in hair—the linchpin of Aims 2 and 3—are described as “under development” without a clear validation pathway or contingency plan, creating a critical feasibility dependency; (2) the quantitative interpretation of segmented hair for weekly exposure reconstruction in Aim 3 rests on assumptions about hair growth rate that are neither validated nor subjected to sensitivity analysis, limiting the inferential strength of the project’s most innovative element; (3) the management plan relies heavily on parent-study clinical staff for specimen collection without specifying authority, dedicated funding, or a protocol for handling visit misalignment, which introduces a non-trivial operational risk to longitudinal data completeness; and (4) the exposure–outcome analyses, while appropriately descriptive and predictive in framing, lack explicit discussion of unmeasured confounding or sensitivity analyses that would bound the interpretation of “protective thresholds.”

The scientific value and innovation are good—the combined scope and the specific extensions to dapivirine and cabotegravir are genuine increments beyond prior work. The design is broadly capable of supporting the core descriptive and pattern-based inferences, even though the more ambitious quantitative threshold claims should be regarded as provisional. However, the unresolved ethical, assay-validation and management dependencies mean the current proposal is not yet sufficient for support as a complete package. The direction is worthwhile, and a supportable scope is identifiable—specifically, one that either provides the missing validation and ethical analysis, or appropriately scopes the claims to what can be delivered with the currently demonstrated tools. The recommendation therefore reflects the need for substantive revision to address these interconnected issues.

## Detailed Evaluation by Criterion

### 1. Scientific Value: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- The knowledge to be gained is directly relevant to a major global health challenge. Validating a practical, non-invasive exposure monitor that works across oral, topical and injectable antiretrovirals would fill a recognised gap in adherence science and could inform clinical monitoring in both trial and programmatic settings. The descriptive characterisation of exposure patterns (e.g., high plasma/low hair, detectable hair/undetectable plasma) through concurrent comparison of multiple measures provides a methodological contribution that extends beyond the specific drugs studied.
- The project is embedded in three outcome-rich cohorts (SEARCH, HOPE, ACTG A5359) with standardised visit schedules and plasma reference data. This supplies the necessary exposure variability and clinical endpoints at marginal additional cost, making the research questions answerable with high efficiency. The use of outcome-dependent sampling with population weights is a well-justified economising strategy.
- The aims are appropriately scoped for the stage of development: Aims 1 and 2 seek to validate hair as an adherence-exposure biomarker and to estimate approximate exposure thresholds associated with protection, not to deliver a fully calibrated clinical decision rule. Aim 3 is explicitly a pharmacokinetic monitoring study to inform possible future prevention use, framed as exploratory for virologic suppression outcomes. These are legitimate, valuable increments.

**Limitations**
- **Outcome-enriched sampling limits population-level calibration.** Because only seroconverters and sampled controls will have hair analysed, the precision and generalisability of absolute risk estimates depend on the sampling fractions and the correctness of the weighting model. The proposal candidly acknowledges this; the attainable contribution is “exposure levels associated with protection” in the sampled groups rather than a full population-representative calibration. This is a reasonable economising choice, but it bounds the knowledge contribution.
- **Reference thresholds are provisional.** The cabotegravir protective hair threshold of 66 ng/mg is derived from a modelled plasma reference (4× PA-IC₉₀); the proposal does not provide the empirical basis for that correspondence in human subjects. Until established in the proposed participants, the threshold analysis remains hypothesis-generating.
- **Interpretation of exposure patterns relies on pharmacokinetic assumptions.** Distinguishing behavioural patterns (e.g., “intermittent earlier exposure” vs. “recent dosing before visit”) from the combined measures depends on assumptions about the time-courses of hair incorporation, plasma clearance, and residual ring drug depletion that are only partially validated. The proposal appropriately focuses on *patterns* rather than claiming definitive behavioural reconstruction, but the inferential gap should be explicit.

These limitations do not negate the scientific value; they frame it. The knowledge gained will be strongest for relative ranking of exposure, demonstrating complementary information content of the multi-measure approach, and establishing the feasibility of hair monitoring for dapivirine and cabotegravir. Absolute threshold estimates should be regarded as provisional outputs requiring independent confirmation. Overall, the scientific value is good.

### 2. Innovation: B (Good) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- The systematic combination of three delivery modes and the multi-measure comparison framework is a genuine extension beyond prior work. While individual comparisons of hair with plasma or self-report exist, the proposal structures the investigation prospectively around pattern identification across oral, topical and injectable delivery in a single, harmonised programme. This operationalises a conceptual framework that has been discussed in the adherence literature but not tested at this breadth.
- The extension to dapivirine and cabotegravir is a non-routine methodological expansion. For dapivirine, establishing that hair concentrations correlate meaningfully with ring use and outcomes when systemic exposure is low and a residual ring drug measure is also available would be a genuine addition. For cabotegravir, the segmentation approach to approximate weekly exposure and characterise post-injection decline is not, to the reviewer’s knowledge, published for this drug class. This is a novel application that could inform future long-acting PrEP monitoring.
- The placement of hair monitoring within “real-world” delivery settings (SEARCH’s community-randomised PrEP programme, HOPE’s open-label extension) is a contextual innovation. It evaluates whether biomarker relationships observed under controlled trial conditions persist when delivery channels, participant characteristics and adherence support vary.

**Limitations**
- The core hypothesis—that a long-term measure paired with shorter-term measures better describes exposure than any single snapshot—is a logical extension of known pharmacokinetic principles and the prior VOICE hair-plasma comparisons cited in the preliminary work. The novelty resides more in the breadth and rigour of testing than in a new conceptual insight.
- The analytical methods (weighted logistic regression, linear mixed models, Spearman correlations) are standard. The innovation lies in the application and the sampling design, not in statistical methodology.

**Distinction from scientific value:** The originality is moderate—the core ideas have precedent—but the combined scope and the specific extensions to dapivirine and cabotegravir raise the innovation above a simple replication. If successful, the work would produce the first multi-drug, multi-cohort demonstration that hair can serve as a versatile platform for adherence-exposure monitoring, which is a practical capability that does not currently exist.

### 3. Rigor: C (Moderate) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- The outcome-dependent sampling design with population weights is statistically sound and well-suited to a biomarker-validation study with relatively rare events. The simplified power calculations, while conservative, suggest adequate power for the primary HIV-outcome contrasts.
- The multi-measure comparison framework is principled and well-motivated by pharmacokinetic properties. The specific expected patterns (high plasma/low hair, etc.) provide testable predictions that structure the descriptive analyses.
- The laboratory methods for the established tenofovir/emtricitabine assay are described in detail, with documented precision and accuracy. The handling of undetectable values (substitution at detection limit, log transformation) is a conventional approach.
- The visit schedules provide adequate frequency to capture temporal variation in exposure across the three delivery modes.

**Limitations**
- **Weighting and variance estimation are underspecified.** The proposal states that known sampling fractions will be incorporated as weights, but does not describe whether inverse-probability weights, non-response adjustments, normalisation, or robust variance estimation will be used. The effective sample size for weighted analyses can be substantially reduced by differential sampling fractions; this is not explicitly accounted for in the power calculations.
- **Unmeasured confounding is not addressed.** The pooled logistic models for seroconversion adjust for measured risk factors, but the observational design means participants with higher hair concentrations are likely to differ systematically from those with lower concentrations in ways that also affect HIV risk (health-seeking behaviour, partnership characteristics). The proposal does not discuss this limitation or propose sensitivity analyses (e.g., E-value analysis, negative-control outcomes). The descriptive and predictive contribution remains attainable; any inference that the association reflects a direct pharmacological protective effect, as opposed to partly marking a more adherent and lower-risk profile, would need explicit justification. The proposal does not claim a causal interpretation, but the language of “levels associated with high protection” invites a reading that the design alone cannot fully support.
- **Circularity risk in the renal analysis.** Participants are selected for renal sampling based on reaching an eGFR threshold or decline; the outcome analysis models decline using the same (or overlapping) criteria. The proposal does not show how the design avoids conditioning on the outcome, which is tractable but requires explicit specification.
- **Sensitivity to undetectable-value handling.** The substitution approach is conventional but can influence correlation and threshold estimates when the proportion of undetectable samples is high—a plausible scenario in low-adherence oral PrEP populations. Sensitivity analyses (multiple imputation, tobit models) are not described.
- **Segmental analysis assumptions are untested.** The 0.25 cm segmentation interpreted as weekly exposure relies on a 1 cm/month growth-rate assumption. Individual variability, hair-phase mixing, and segmentation recovery are not addressed. This limits the quantitative interpretation of the Aim 3 trajectory analyses to a qualitative level without additional validation.
- **Residual ring drug measurement is not specified.** The comparison of hair dapivirine with residual ring drug is a component of Aim 2’s inferential contribution, but the measurement properties, variability and handling conditions for residual ring drug are not described, leaving the feasibility of this specific comparison partially unevaluated.

**Overall:** The design is capable of supporting the core descriptive and pattern-based inferences. The more ambitious quantitative claims about protective thresholds and weekly exposure trajectories are limited by underspecified statistical procedures and untested assumptions. The rigour is moderate.

### 4. Feasibility: C (Moderate) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- The strategy of adding specimen collection and analysis to existing, funded parent studies de-risks participant access, follow-up and baseline data collection. Coordination with parent-study leadership is documented.
- The preliminary work provides concrete evidence of high hair-collection acceptability (95% in a community study; 112/113 participants at the one-month visit in SEARCH). The described collection method is operationally simple and has been field-demonstrated.
- The case–control sampling concentrates expensive assays on the most informative person-visits, linking resources efficiently to the primary questions.

**Limitations**
- **Critical assay dependency.** The entire project depends on validated, high-performing assays for hair dapivirine and cabotegravir. The proposal provides representative chromatograms from spiked samples, which is a necessary initial step but not a full method validation. The assays are described as “under development.” Failure to achieve adequate precision and accuracy at clinically relevant low concentrations for either drug would severely curtail Aims 2 and 3. A contingency plan or staged validation milestone is not described.
- **Reliance on parent-study operations.** Hair collection at the required visit frequency depends entirely on parent-study clinical staff, without a specified authority, funding line or protocol for ensuring compliance. If a parent study faces operational delays or reprioritisation, missed collection windows are irrecoverable and directly affect longitudinal data completeness.
- **Visit misalignment and segmental interpretation.** Aim 3’s weekly-exposure inference requires precisely timed collections relative to injection dates. The proposal acknowledges that missed and delayed visits are recorded but does not describe how a two- or four-week delay would be handled analytically. Without a predefined protocol, the timeline linking hair segments to injection events is compromised.
- **Laboratory throughput is not detailed.** The project requires processing approximately 5,200 person-visits for Aim 1, 1,680 for Aim 2, and longitudinal samples from 150 participants for Aim 3 across three distinct LC–MS/MS methods. The management plan does not address staffing, instrument time, or coordination of these pipelines. The PI’s established laboratory makes this surmountable, but the absence of specification is a gap.

**Overall:** The project is feasible in outline, but feasibility is contingent on two unresolved dependencies—assay validation for the novel matrices and the operational reliability of parent-study collection—that have not been adequately de-risked in the current plan.

### 5. Ethics and Safety: D (Weak) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- The added research procedure (small-volume hair collection) is minimally invasive, brief, and has a low baseline of physical risk, which is a foundational ethical strength.
- The proposal acknowledges specific, non-trivial risks including minor scalp injury, confidentiality concerns, and inconvenience.
- The project leverages the existing ethics and oversight infrastructure of the parent studies (ethics approvals, monitoring boards), providing a framework for continuing review and adverse-event reporting.
- The commitment to make blinded hair-concentration datasets publicly available is a positive step for transparency, provided de-identification is robust.

**Limitations**
- **Inadequate engagement with cultural and social meaning of hair.** The proposal’s handling of consent is minimal and undifferentiated: “Consent includes hair collection.” It fails to address how the meaning of hair—which can carry deep cultural, ethnic and personal significance—will be handled in the consent process across diverse populations in Uganda, Kenya, Malawi, South Africa and Zimbabwe. The reference to “different hairstyles” and collection from the “upper hair layer” or “occipital scalp” implies collection may be both visible and socially meaningful. No community engagement or formative work is described to ensure the process is acceptable and that consent is genuinely informed.
- **Insufficient safeguards for uniquely sensitive behavioural data.** Segmental hair analysis will reconstruct a multi-month timeline of drug exposure, creating a record of personal behaviour—potentially including lapses in PrEP use, ring removal, or missed injection visits—with far greater precision than self-report or plasma. This data could be severely stigmatizing if linked to an individual or their community. The data security plan is limited to relying on “Trial-network laboratory data systems,” without discussion of specific encryption, access controls, identifiability of the public dataset, or protocols for reporting incidental findings (e.g., evidence of diverted medication). The amplified privacy risks introduced by this new data type are not analysed or matched with proportionate safeguards.
- **Withdrawal and future-use consent are unspecified.** The procedure for handling samples from participants who subsequently withdraw consent is absent. The proposal notes that unassayed specimens will remain available for future studies, but the consent process for this future use is not described. These are standard ethical requirements for longitudinal specimen banking.
- **Vulnerable subgroups are not considered.** Adolescents (15–17 years) are grouped with adults, with no mention of assent procedures tailored to their comprehension of the long-term exposure timeline or its privacy implications. Participants in Aim 3, selected for a history of poor treatment adherence, may be economically or socially vulnerable and may perceive the added hair monitoring as coercive or judgmental surveillance. This is not discussed.

**Overall:** The proposal benefits from a low-risk collection procedure and existing oversight infrastructure, but the ethical analysis is procedurally generic and lacks the depth required for the proposed activity. The failure to engage with the cultural meaning of hair and to specify proportionate data safeguards are decisive limitations. The ethics and safety plan, as submitted, is weak.

### 6. Management: C (Moderate) — Confidence: B (High)

**Strengths**
- Clear roles are assigned to named experts: Gandhi (hair measurement programme lead), Benet (pharmacokinetics), Gerona (analytic chemistry), Bacchetti (statistics). This covers the necessary disciplinary expertise.
- The management plan includes specific coordination activities: monthly data-quality review of collection and specimen data, collection training with demonstrations for different hairstyles, and retraining when necessary. These are appropriate for maintaining specimen quality across international sites.
- The use of existing parent-study infrastructure reduces the management burden of independent site setup and participant recruitment.

**Limitations**
- **Unspecified authority over parent-study operations.** The hair-study team relies on parent-study clinical staff for timely and accurate specimen collection, but the proposal does not specify the mechanism or authority to ensure this occurs. If a parent study reprioritises or faces staff turnover, the hair add-on may be de-prioritised, and missed collection windows cannot be recovered.
- **No protocol for visit misalignment.** For Aim 3, the segmental interpretation requires collections precisely timed to injection visits. The proposal does not describe how delayed or missed visits will be handled analytically or operationally. A predefined protocol is essential for data interpretability.
- **Laboratory throughput planning is absent.** The volume of assays across three distinct LC–MS/MS methods and the coordination with specimen arrival from multiple international sites is not detailed. The capacity-building activity with African laboratories in the final year is framed as dissemination, not as core analytical capacity.
- **The capacity-building component has no evaluable plan.** Objectives, methods, timeline and evaluation criteria are not provided. This does not directly affect the primary scientific aims, but it is an unspecified dependency if subsequent local use of hair monitoring is an implicit long-term goal.

**Overall:** The management structure is logically organised around named expertise and includes sensible quality-control activities. However, the plan under-specifies critical operational dependencies—particularly the authority over parent-study collection and the handling of visit misalignment—that affect the likelihood of delivering the stated data. Management is moderate.

## Recommendation: Current proposal insufficient for support (revise)

This is a worthwhile scientific direction. The core idea—a multi-cohort, multi-measure validation of hair-based exposure monitoring across delivery modes—addresses an important need, and the project is efficiently embedded in rich existing cohorts. The innovation and scientific value are good, and the descriptive and pattern-based contributions are attainable with the broad design.

However, the current plan has substantive, interconnected problems that affect its main goals. The most decisive is the weak ethics and safety analysis: the proposal does not adequately engage with the cultural meaning of hair collection in the study populations or specify proportionate safeguards for the uniquely sensitive behavioural data the project will generate. This is not a marginal documentation issue; it raises unanswered questions about informed consent and participant protection that must be resolved before the project can be considered ethically sound.

Additional interconnected issues compound this concern:
- **Critical assay dependencies:** The dapivirine and cabotegravir hair assays are not yet validated. Aims 2 and 3 cannot proceed without them, and no contingency or staged validation plan is described.
- **Untested assumptions in the most innovative element:** The segmental analysis for weekly exposure reconstruction relies on hair growth-rate assumptions that are neither validated nor subjected to sensitivity analysis, limiting the quantitative contribution of Aim 3.
- **Operational dependencies without authority:** The project depends on parent-study staff for specimen collection but lacks a specified mechanism to ensure compliance or handle visit misalignment, creating a risk to longitudinal data completeness.

A supportable scope is identifiable and would require: (1) a substantive ethics analysis that addresses cultural context, community engagement, consent for the specific implications of long-timeline exposure data, data security protocols, and the handling of vulnerable subgroups; (2) completion and documentation of assay validation for dapivirine and cabotegravir in hair, or a staged plan where Aims 2 and 3 are contingent on achieving pre-specified validation milestones; (3) a sensitivity analysis plan for the segmental hair interpretation, or an appropriately scoped claim for the temporal resolution achievable without further calibration; and (4) a management protocol clarifying the authority, resourcing and fallback procedures for specimen collection within the parent studies. With these revisions, the project could address its current limitations and proceed on a sound footing. In its present form, however, the unresolved ethical, assay and operational dependencies prevent a recommendation of support.
