## Comprehensive Review

This review provides a consolidated assessment of the proposed research project, evaluating its scientific merit, methodological grounding, implementation feasibility, and ethical management as described in the submitted text. The proposal addresses the persistent and important question of whether submesoscale ocean dynamics (0.2–25 km) make quantitatively significant contributions to vertical exchange between the ocean surface boundary layer and the interior. This synthesis integrates separate analyses of the proposal’s contribution, design rigour, delivery feasibility, and ethical safeguards to form a holistic judgement.

### Overall Assessment

The proposal targets a genuine and well-motivated knowledge gap in physical oceanography: the lack of an observational benchmark capable of testing and discriminating among competing model representations of submesoscale vertical flux. The scientific rationale is clearly articulated, and the stated contributions—a multi-parameter four-dimensional dataset, process-level testing of model predictions, and an exploratory remote-sensing flux proxy—are valuable and properly staged. The conceptual framework linking frontal dynamics, seasonally varying stratification, and vertical exchange is current and appropriate for the scale of the investigation.

The project’s primary strength lies in the thoughtful integration of mature airborne sensors (DopplerScatt, PRISM, MASS, MOSES) with a nested in situ array of Lagrangian floats, Seagliders, Wave Gliders, drifters, and ship-based measurements. This multi-platform architecture is designed to overcome a central observational challenge: connecting wide-area surface current mapping to the three-dimensional velocity and tracer fields where vertical exchange occurs. The inclusion of a pilot campaign to test interoperability, pre-campaign observing-system simulation experiments, and a threshold-mission construct with a defined descope option are all evidence of a well-managed, staged approach suited to a complex field program.

These strengths are, however, circumscribed by three categories of limitation that emerge consistently across the detailed assessments.

1. **The methodological pathway to a validated remote-sensing flux proxy (Goal 4) remains underspecified.** The proposal correctly identifies this as the most exploratory objective, but the critical steps—assigning a vertical length scale *h* to a given surface divergence measurement, quantifying its spatial and temporal variability, and propagating the resulting uncertainty into flux covariance estimates—are not resolved in the submitted plan. The feasibility of obtaining a robust, generalisable *h* relationship within a 25-day intensive operations period is therefore difficult to assess. This is the most consequential limitation for the project's most ambitious aim.

2. **Multi-platform operational coordination carries unquantified delivery risk.** Achieving the required space-time coincidences (e.g., aircraft and in situ assets co-located within one hour over a moving, 25 km feature) with three aircraft, a research vessel, and multiple autonomous platforms is an operationally demanding objective. While the pilot campaign will test a subset of this integration, the full nested-sampling concept—critically involving deep-diving Seagliders and Lagrangian floats—will not be rehearsed until the first main intensive operations period. The proposal does not supply a concrete scheduling protocol or evidence of prior success with this specific combination of assets.

3. **The post-campaign schedule for data integration and synthesis leaves limited margin.** The project management plan assigns data delivery milestones that extend into the final year, leaving approximately nine months for integrated analysis and publication. The absence of a described common data-integration framework, combined with the acknowledged dependencies between instrument streams, creates a schedule-chain risk that is not addressed in the supplied risk register.

The environmental ethic of the project is positively indicated by the deliberate choice of biodegradable drifters, which manages a known source of marine debris. However, the proposal text does not contain a safety management plan for the integrated operation or documentation of environmental permitting and acoustic-impact assessment. These are informational gaps in the review evidence rather than demonstrated deficiencies, but they reduce confidence in the thoroughness of the project’s operational safety management.

The overall profile is that of a well-conceived field campaign with strongly defensible descriptive and comparative goals (Goals 1 and 3) whose principal translational aim (Goal 4) is not yet supported by a sufficiently detailed methodological specification. The delivery of the full set of baseline objectives depends on resolving the operational and schedule-chain dependencies identified above.

### Evaluation by Criterion

#### Scientific Value: B (Confidence: B)

The question is significant, and the attainable knowledge—a benchmark observational dataset and a rigorous confrontation of model predictions with direct field measurements—is consequential for physical oceanography and for downstream biogeochemical and climate-scale parameterisation efforts. The descriptive, conceptual, and methodological contributions are all substantive. The value of the most exploratory element (Goal 4) is conditional on validation of the proxy relationship, but the threshold-mission objectives are independently valuable. The seasonal comparison adds discriminating power for process attribution. The limitation is that the maximum attainable contribution (a validated remote-sensing flux capability) depends on methodological steps that are not fully specified; this is a scientific risk that affects the probability of delivering the highest-value outcome rather than the value of the attempt itself.

#### Innovation: B (Confidence: B)

The project’s originality is methodological and integrative rather than theoretical. It does not propose a new mechanism, instrument, or conceptual framework. Instead, it advances beyond prior multi-platform demonstrations (e.g., D’Asaro et al. 2018) by combining wide-area DopplerScatt current mapping, hyperspectral ocean colour, directional wave spectra, and a nested in situ array at the spatial and temporal resolution needed to test model predictions. The coordination of this particular sensor suite to address submesoscale vertical flux is novel, and the attempt to construct a remote-sensing proxy is the most original scientific element. The innovation is appropriately characterised as a well-designed incremental advance that meets a recognised community need.

#### Rigor: B (Confidence: B)

The nested observational strategy, science traceability matrix, dual-method vertical velocity estimation (Lagrangian floats and Wave Glider divergence), and the use of observing-system simulation experiments all indicate a methodologically informed design. The measurement specifications are, for the most part, matched to instrument performance claims with identified margins. The design supports the descriptive and process-characterisation goals (Goals 1 and 3) with proportional evidence. The main limitation concerns the rigour of the flux proxy route (Goal 4): the method for assigning and validating the vertical length scale, and the formal uncertainty analysis, are not yet specified to a level that allows a reviewer to judge whether the resulting covariance estimates will be distinguishable from noise under realistic conditions. The operational coordination required to achieve the space-time coincidence specifications is also described schematically rather than in terms of a validated protocol.

#### Feasibility: C (Confidence: C)

At the component level, the proposal documents credible platform and instrument maturity, generous aircraft accommodation margins, and a management structure with appropriate delegation. The staged plan with a pilot campaign, threshold and baseline missions, and identified backup windows is well-conceived. Feasibility of the full baseline aims is, however, materially uncertain in three respects. First, the full-system integration (aircraft, ship, Seagliders, Lagrangian floats) will not be tested until the first main IOP; an interoperability problem at that stage would erode mission days and reduce the number of feature realisations. Second, the proposal does not describe a specific real-time coordination mechanism for three aircraft and multiple autonomous platforms operating concurrently over a compact region with a one-hour coincidence requirement. Third, the schedule for integrated analysis is compressed, with little explicit float for delayed deliveries or reprocessing, and no described data-integration framework. These dependencies mean that the probability of delivering the full baseline mission as described is difficult to assess as high on the supplied text alone.

#### Ethics and Safety: C (Confidence: C)

The project design shows environmental awareness through the use of biodegradable drifters and reliance on established operational platforms and protocols. These are genuine strengths. The proposal text does not, however, contain a safety management plan for the uniquely complex integrated operations (three aircraft, a ship, and multiple autonomous vehicles working simultaneously offshore), nor does it document environmental permitting, acoustic impact assessment, or biological-sample handling protocols. These unspecified elements are not evidence of misconduct, but they prevent a confident judgement that all material risks have been proportionately managed. Given the non-sensitive nature of the physical and biogeochemical data, the data ethics obligations are inherently light and largely satisfied by the planned archive delivery.

#### Management: B (Confidence: B)

The management structure appropriately separates airborne, in situ, and analysis activities under a PI and Science Investigation Manager, with NASA Ames ESPO providing deployment support. The allocation of postdoctoral researchers, the phased sequence from modelling to integrated analysis, and the data-product delivery milestones with named leads all indicate a coherent organisational framework. The risk register identifies material operational risks and ties each to a mitigation. The principal management limitation is the schedule-chain risk posed by interdependent data streams and a tight final synthesis period; the risk register does not explicitly address cascading delays. Additionally, the absence of a specified data-integration protocol is a gap in the management documentation that could compromise the efficiency of the analysis phase.

### Recommendation

**Conditional support (conditional_support).**

A supportable scope is already identifiable: the threshold mission, focused on descriptive characterisation of submesoscale features and testing of process-level model predictions (Goals 1 and 3), is well-justified, methodologically proportional, and likely deliverable within the documented resources and timeline. The pilot campaign and observing-system simulation experiments provide a structured pathway for verifying platform interoperability and refining sampling protocols before the main intensive periods. This core contribution would, on its own, represent a worthwhile advance for the field.

Support for the full baseline mission, and particularly for Goal 4 (remote-sensing flux proxy), is conditional on the following premises being substantiated before the main intensive operations periods commence:

1. **A validated operational coordination protocol.** The project should demonstrate, through the pilot campaign or an equivalently documented rehearsal, that the three-aircraft, ship, and autonomous-platform system can achieve the stated one-hour space-time coincidence requirements over multiple feature surveys under realistic offshore conditions.

2. **A specified flux proxy analysis methodology.** The project should provide a detailed plan for constructing the vertical length scale *h* from the combined in situ and model datasets, including an analysis of *h*'s expected variability across frontal regimes and seasons, a formal propagation of related uncertainties into the flux covariance estimates, and a quantitative statement of the minimum detectable flux signal given the expected instrumental noise and sampling statistics.

3. **An integrated data-management and schedule-risk mitigation plan.** The project should produce a data-integration protocol for the diverse instrument streams and a revised synthesis schedule that includes explicit float for reprocessing, cross-calibration, and cascading delays, with a contingency scenario that identifies the minimum viable integrated analysis if one major instrument stream is significantly delayed.

If these conditions are met, the full baseline mission would become a supportable proposition with a proportionate evidential basis for its most ambitious objective. If they are not met, the project should be supported at the threshold-mission level, where the scientific value and methodological rigour are already well-established.
