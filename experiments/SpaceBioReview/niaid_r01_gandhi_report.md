# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project, “Hair Extensions,” aims to develop and validate an integrated approach for monitoring antiretroviral drug exposure in HIV prevention. It does so by measuring drug concentrations in hair—a cumulative adherence biomarker—across three distinct delivery modalities: oral tenofovir-based pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), a dapivirine vaginal ring, and long-acting injectable cabotegravir. The planned work leverages the existing infrastructure and participant cohorts of three large clinical studies in East and Southern Africa and the United States. The most valuable expected knowledge is the first systematic characterization of hair cabotegravir pharmacokinetics during long-acting injectable treatment and the validation of hair dapivirine as a complementary adherence measure for the vaginal ring, which together would provide a unified, long-term exposure monitoring framework to guide both future trials and clinical implementation.

The project is strongly supported by an experienced, multidisciplinary team and a highly efficient design that piggybacks on the large, well-funded parent trials SEARCH, HOPE, and ACTG A5359. The underlying hair-collection and LC–MS/MS analytical methods are well-established for oral tenofovir, and a validated assay exists for cabotegravir. The overarching concept of comparing short- and long-term exposure measures to reveal adherence patterns is scientifically sound. The main limitations affect the rigor of the inferences. For the dapivirine ring aim, the HIV event rate is only half of what was estimated for the power calculation, substantially limiting the precision of any protective threshold. More fundamentally, this aim’s open-label, single-arm design cannot fully disentangle the effect of drug exposure on HIV risk from confounding by user behavior. For the cabotegravir aim, the proposed hair-based protective threshold of 66 ng/mg is not empirically supported, though its treatment as a candidate cutoff mitigates this concern. The virologic suppression analysis is explicitly exploratory and underpowered. For the oral PrEP aim, the sampling design is valid, but the power calculation is overly simplified and the analytical plan for dual-outcome weighting is insufficiently specified.

Weighing this evidence, the project represents a high-value, well-coordinated scientific program with clear strengths in its integrating vision and excellent implementation platform. The limitations are primarily in the rigor of specific analytical plans and the realistic precision of the Aim 2 threshold estimates, not in the underlying scientific questions or the team’s capacity. These issues are refinable and do not fundamentally undermine the project’s ability to meet its primary descriptive and comparative objectives. The current plan is appropriate for its stage, and the collective knowledge gained will substantively advance the field’s capacity for exposure monitoring in HIV prevention. I therefore evaluate the plan positively, with the most impactful contributions expected from the novel cabotegravir and dapivirine ring work.

**Overall conclusion: Conditional support**

## Scientific value

**Scientific value: B (good); confidence B (high).**

The project addresses a critical barrier to HIV prevention: the difficulty of objectively measuring adherence across different antiretroviral delivery modalities in real-world settings where self-report alone is unreliable [1]. Its principal contribution is an integrated, long-term exposure monitoring framework that combines hair drug concentrations—a cumulative measure reflecting weeks to months of exposure—with plasma levels and product-specific metrics (residual ring drug, injection records) to characterize adherence patterns and their relationship to clinical outcomes [1]. The most valuable attainable knowledge differs substantially across the three aims and together forms a coherent program.

**Aim 3 (cabotegravir) fills a genuine gap with the first systematic hair pharmacokinetic dataset during long-acting injectable treatment.** Approximately 150 participants from ACTG A5359 will provide hair and plasma specimens at seven timepoints spanning the injection interval [1]. Segmental hair analysis with 0.25 cm resolution offers an approximate weekly exposure timeline that cannot be obtained from trough plasma alone, which reflects a single timepoint near the next scheduled injection [1] [2]. This capacity to distinguish sustained low exposure from rapid post-injection decline, and to detect patterns such as recent clinic attendance masking poor cumulative exposure, directly informs pharmacokinetic monitoring strategies for both treatment and future injectable PrEP. The planned characterization of exposure correlates—BMI, sex, and renal and hepatic function—addresses known pharmacokinetic variability, including the 28–30% lower trough concentrations observed in females and individuals with BMI ≥ 30 kg/m² [1] [3]. No published study has systematically evaluated hair cabotegravir during injectable use, and the only published paired hair-plasma data come from a single, non-steady-state case [4].

**Aim 2 (dapivirine ring) validates hair as an adherence measure for vaginally delivered antiretrovirals, a recognized priority.** Most studies assessing drug uptake in hair have been performed with orally administered drugs, and validation for vaginal delivery has been explicitly identified as a research need [5]. The Stalter et al. review designated drug levels in hair as a high-priority approach requiring further investigation for vaginally delivered compounds [5]. While residual ring drug levels already provide an objective adherence measure correlated with HIV protection—\>0.9 mg released was associated with 48% risk reduction, and \>4 mg released with 75–91% in exploratory analyses—hair dapivirine would offer a systemic exposure measure that potentially captures patterns that residual ring drug misses, such as intermittent ring removal with reinsertion [6] [1]. Establishing this measurement in a large ring-using population (1,456 women in HOPE) [7] would create a tool applicable to future ring-based multipurpose prevention technologies.

**Aim 1 (oral tenofovir PrEP) provides a valuable but more incremental extension.** Hair tenofovir levels are already validated as adherence biomarkers. A systematic review confirms that hair antiretroviral concentrations are among the strongest independent predictors of virologic outcomes and correlate with other pharmacokinetic measures (r 0.20–0.85 across plasma, PBMC, and DBS comparators) [8] [1]. This aim extends that evidence to a large, community-based East African cohort of approximately 6,400 PrEP recipients in the SEARCH study, relating hair levels to HIV seroconversion and renal toxicity in a real-world delivery setting that includes young women, mobile populations, fisherfolk, and serodiscordant couples—groups with distinct adherence challenges [1]. The planned comparison of hair, plasma, and self-reported use to identify exposure patterns such as “white coat” dosing (high plasma, low hair) provides practical clinical utility [1].

**The overarching concept of a unified monitoring framework** spanning oral, vaginal ring, and injectable delivery methods is itself a methodological contribution, enabling cross-modality comparisons that no single-product study can provide [1].

## Innovation

**Innovation: B (good); confidence B (high).**

The project’s principal original contribution is the application of a single, integrated hair-monitoring framework across three pharmacokinetically distinct antiretroviral delivery modalities—oral, vaginal ring, and long-acting injectable—within a coordinated research program [1]. While hair analysis for orally administered antiretrovirals is well-established, the systematic extension to vaginally delivered dapivirine and injectable cabotegravir, combined with explicit cross-modality comparisons of short-term (plasma) and long-term (hair, residual ring drug) exposure measures, represents a methodologically inventive approach that no prior study has undertaken. The innovation varies considerably by aim.

**Aim 3 (cabotegravir) is the most innovative component.** No published study has systematically characterized hair cabotegravir pharmacokinetics during long-acting injectable treatment. The only existing paired hair-plasma data derive from a single case [4]. The proposed segmental analysis at 0.25 cm resolution—yielding an approximate weekly exposure timeline spanning the injection interval—offers temporal detail unobtainable from trough plasma measurements, which reflect drug concentration at a single point near the next scheduled injection [1] [2]. The capacity to distinguish sustained low exposure from rapid post-injection decline, and to detect patterns where recent clinic attendance masks poor cumulative exposure, would be genuinely new for injectable antiretroviral monitoring. The exploratory characterization of exposure correlates including BMI, sex, and renal and hepatic function addresses known pharmacokinetic variability and will produce the first population-level dataset linking participant characteristics to hair-based drug exposure during injectable use [3] [1].

**Aim 2 (dapivirine ring) addresses a recognized methodological gap.** The HOPE platform provides an efficient means to generate these validation data in a large, well-characterized population across four African countries [1] [7]. The novel contribution is not merely measuring hair dapivirine—the laboratory has demonstrated detection of spiked dapivirine in hair [1]—but rather establishing whether vaginally delivered dapivirine reaches systemic circulation and incorporates into hair in proportion to ring use, and whether the resulting hair concentrations capture exposure information that differs meaningfully from the established residual ring drug measure [6]. This validation would position hair as a complementary adherence tool applicable beyond the current ring product.

**Aim 1 (oral PrEP) is a well-precedented application rather than a genuinely novel measurement.** Hair tenofovir concentrations have been validated against directly observed dosing, other pharmacokinetic measures (r range 0.20–0.85 across plasma, PBMC, and DBS), and clinical outcomes including virologic response and renal toxicity [1] [8]. The innovation resides in the setting and analytic approach: extending hair monitoring to a large, community-based East African cohort with diverse risk groups, applying a case-cohort sampling design with dual-outcome enrichment (HIV seroconversion and renal decline) to efficiently link hair exposure to multiple clinical endpoints [1]. This design enables population-level inference about the exposure-outcome relationship in a real-world PrEP delivery context that includes groups underrepresented in earlier validation work. The explicit comparison of hair, plasma, and self-reported use to identify longitudinal adherence patterns such as sustained use, intermittent exposure, and visit-related dosing is a practical extension rather than a conceptual departure [1].

**The project’s integrative contribution**—establishing a common exposure-monitoring framework that spans delivery methods and can guide both clinical trials and implementation—is a methodological advance that exceeds the sum of the individual aims. The inclusion of capacity-building activities with African laboratories in the final project year further extends the innovation toward sustainable implementation [1].

## Rigor

**Rigor: C (moderate); confidence B (high).**

The project employs a valid overall strategy—leveraging existing trial cohorts with case-cohort sampling to efficiently link hair drug concentrations to clinical outcomes—but several material limitations in design and analytical specification weaken the inferential support for the central claims, particularly in Aims 1 and 2.

**The shared analytical framework is methodologically sound in concept.** Hair collection follows a standardized protocol (50–100 occipital strands, orientation preserved, room-temperature storage), and the LC–MS/MS assays for tenofovir, emtricitabine, and cabotegravir are validated with relative errors and coefficients of variation below 15% for quality-control samples [1] [4]. For dapivirine, the laboratory has demonstrated detection in spiked hair but has not yet published data correlating hair dapivirine concentrations in actual ring users with ring use duration or residual drug levels [1]. This validation gap makes the comparative analysis of hair versus residual ring drug more fundamentally exploratory than the proposal acknowledges [1].

**Aim 1 (SEARCH oral PrEP): the case-cohort design is valid but the power calculation is simplified and key analytical details are unspecified.** The two-phase sampling strategy—certainty sampling of all seroconverters and renal-decline participants, with a random sample of non-seroconverters—and inverse-probability weighting (IPW) for population inference is a well-established and defensible approach [1]. The 3:1 control ratio is efficient. However, the proposal describes its power calculation as a “simplified one-observation” approach yielding 80% power for an odds ratio of 2.1 comparing the lowest hair-exposure quartile with the upper three, and states that “repeated observations supply additional information” [1]. This calculation does not account for two variance-inflating factors.

First, IPW estimation in two-phase designs introduces a design-based variance component: simulations show that weighted Cox regression standard errors in case-cohort samples were approximately 1.5–1.6 times larger than full-cohort standard errors, a design effect that erodes the precision gain from repeated observations [9]. Second, within-participant correlation in repeated hair measurements—expected to be substantial for a cumulative exposure measure—reduces the effective sample size below the person-visit count. The target OR of 2.1 is large enough that the primary comparison likely retains adequate power despite these factors, but the precision for estimating the exposure-response relationship across the full continuous range of hair levels is less well characterized.

Two further analytical gaps affect Aim 1. The proposal does not specify whether sampling weights are assigned at the participant level or the visit level [1]. A participant selected as a control contributes all their longitudinal visits; if weights vary by visit, the variance estimation must accommodate this additional complexity. The renal sampling enriches for participants crossing a creatinine clearance or decline threshold [1], but the linear mixed model for percentage change in renal function uses a continuous outcome related to the selection criterion. The proposal does not clarify how the weights for the renal analysis are specified for visits other than the selection visit. Third, the pooled logistic regression for HIV outcomes does not specify a variance estimation approach for within-participant clustering—such as robust sandwich variance estimators—which is essential given the repeated-measures data structure [1]. The combined requirement for survey-weighted estimation and clustered standard errors creates a doubly complex variance estimation problem that requires explicit attention.

**Aim 2 (HOPE dapivirine ring) is the weakest aim with respect to its capacity to deliver the stated inferences.** The most consequential limitation concerns precision. The proposal assumes approximately 60 seroconversions for the power calculation, yielding 80% power for a lowest-quartile relative risk of 2.9 [1]. The published HOPE results report 35 HIV infections among 1,456 enrolled women, with an incidence of 2.7 per 100 person-years—roughly 58% of the assumed events [7]. With only 35 events and 105 controls (3:1), threshold estimation from a dose-response curve will produce wide confidence intervals, and the ability to distinguish clinically meaningful exposure cutoffs is substantially reduced. The proposal provides no precision analysis for the threshold estimates themselves, only a power calculation for a dichotomized quartile comparison.

Second, the open-label, single-arm design precludes separating the effect of drug exposure on HIV risk from confounding by participant behavior. Women who use the ring more consistently and thus have higher hair dapivirine levels may differ systematically from less-adherent women in HIV risk factors—risk perception, sexual behavior, partnership characteristics—that are imperfectly captured by the measured covariates (age, region, marital status, sexual activity, condom use, STIs) [1]. The prior analysis of residual dapivirine and HIV protection benefited from the randomized ASPIRE placebo group, providing a partial check on confounding that the HOPE design lacks [6]. The proposal does not discuss sensitivity analyses for unmeasured confounding, such as E-values or bias analysis.

Third, the incremental value of hair dapivirine beyond the established residual ring drug measure is uncertain. Residual dapivirine levels have already been shown to correlate with HIV protection: \>0.9 mg released was associated with 48% risk reduction, and \>4 mg with 75–91% in exploratory analyses [6]. Plasma dapivirine rises to near-steady-state approximately eight hours post-insertion and does not reflect long-term ring use, so plasma adds limited information for adherence characterization [6]. The proposal’s analytic plan to examine “patterns of agreement and disagreement” [1] between hair, plasma, and residual ring levels could reveal whether hair captures exposure information that differs from residual ring drug—for instance, if ring removal and reinsertion patterns affect residual drug differently from systemic exposure reflected in hair—but the proposal does not articulate a specific pharmacokinetic rationale for expecting such differences.

**Aim 3 (cabotegravir): well-motivated PK objectives but unsupported threshold specification.** The core pharmacokinetic characterization—hair-plasma correlation, segmental trajectories, and exposure correlates—is adequately powered with approximately 150 participants and seven timepoints yielding roughly 1,050 paired observations, providing a 95% confidence interval of 0.73–0.85 for an expected correlation of 0.8 [1]. The 0.25 cm segmental analysis is ambitious, however. Hair growth rate varies across individuals (0.6–1.4 cm/month) and within individuals over time, making segment position an imperfect proxy for calendar time, and 0.25 cm segments from a typical 50–100 strand collection may yield insufficient mass for reliable quantification across diverse hair types.

More critically, the proposed hair cabotegravir threshold of 66 ng/mg—derived as the hair counterpart to the plasma protective reference of 4× PA-IC₉₀ (0.66 µg/mL)—lacks empirical justification [1]. The plasma threshold is correctly derived from the protein-adjusted IC₉₀ of 0.166 µg/mL. However, the hair value of 66 ng/mg implies a hair-to-plasma ratio of approximately 0.01, for which the proposal provides no empirical basis. The only published hair-plasma paired data—a single case—shows hair 0.190 ng/mg with simultaneous plasma 3.37 µg/mL, yielding a ratio of roughly 0.000056, two orders of magnitude below the assumed value [4]. That case is non-steady-state and not directly comparable, but it illustrates that the actual hair-to-plasma relationship for cabotegravir is unknown. The proposal appropriately uses the 66 ng/mg value as a “candidate cutoff” alongside continuous analyses, which mitigates this concern [1].

The virologic suppression analysis in Aim 3 is correctly labeled “exploratory” [1], and its limitations arise from the parent trial design rather than from any defect in the ancillary study plan. All participants entering the long-acting arm have already achieved virologic suppression through oral induction with adherence support and economic incentives; only 5 of approximately 152 long-acting participants experienced virologic failure as the first event through week 48 [10]. With so few events, logistic models relating hair exposure to suppression will have minimal power and wide confidence intervals. The realistic contribution is descriptive: characterizing the hair concentration distribution in those who maintain suppression versus the rare failures, generating hypotheses for future prospective studies.

**In summary,** the case-cohort design is valid, the laboratory methods are established for oral and injectable agents, and the analytical plans appropriately compare multiple exposure measures across all aims. The principal rigor limitations are the imprecise Aim 2 threshold estimation due to the lower-than-expected event rate, the single-arm confounding in Aim 2’s exposure-outcome analysis, the unsupported cabotegravir hair threshold, and the under-specified variance estimation for Aim 1’s two-phase, repeated-measures analytical framework.

## Feasibility

**Feasibility: B (good); confidence B (high).**

The project is designed as an ancillary study to three large, well-resourced clinical trials, which provides a strong implementation platform. The core hair-collection and analytical methods are established for oral tenofovir and cabotegravir, and the case-cohort sampling strategy is an efficient use of assay resources. Several practical uncertainties, particularly for hair dapivirine validation and the proposed segmental resolution for cabotegravir, are manageable within the study’s exploratory framework.

**The parasitic relationship to the parent studies substantially reduces logistical risk.** SEARCH already follows approximately 6,400 PrEP recipients across 32 communities in Uganda and Kenya with quarterly visits through 144 weeks, collecting hair, plasma, and clinical data at scheduled intervals [1]. HOPE enrolled 1,456 women across Malawi, South Africa, Uganda, and Zimbabwe, with hair and plasma collection at months 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 alongside used-ring returns [1] [7]. ACTG A5359 provides 150 participants with hair and plasma sampling at seven timepoints from week 20 through week 76 [1]. The proposal does not need to establish new clinical sites, recruit participants, or fund the core trial operations. This design is highly efficient.

**The laboratory methods are well-established for the most technically demanding assays.** The UCSF Hair Analytical Laboratory has validated LC–MS/MS methods for tenofovir (range 0.002–0.400 ng/mg) and emtricitabine (0.02–4.0 ng/mg) with relative errors and coefficients of variation below 15% for quality-control samples [1]. A validated cabotegravir hair assay exists with a lower limit of quantitation of 0.00625 ng/mg [4]. The preliminary demonstration of dapivirine detection in spiked hair [1] confirms analytical capability, though this falls short of demonstrating that vaginally delivered dapivirine incorporates into hair in detectable and dose-proportional concentrations—a validation that the study itself is designed to produce. This is a recognized research priority [5] and the risk is inherent to the research question rather than to the feasibility of the methods.

**The case-cohort sampling strategy concentrates assay resources on informative specimens.** In Aim 1, assaying all visits from approximately 100 seroconverters and 300 randomly sampled controls, plus up to 100 renal-decline participants, yields roughly 6,500 person-visits for analysis out of a potential 83,200—a greater than 90% reduction in assay burden while retaining population inference through inverse-probability weighting [1]. Aim 2 similarly assays all seroconverters and three controls per case, yielding approximately 1,680 samples from 240 women [1]. This sampling approach requires the statistical capacity to implement weighted analyses correctly—a dependency the team addresses through biostatistician Bacchetti’s involvement [1].

**Two practical uncertainties warrant acknowledgement.** First, the 0.25 cm segmental resolution proposed for cabotegravir hair analysis is ambitious [1]. Hair growth rate varies across individuals (0.6–1.4 cm/month) and within individuals over time, making segment position an imperfect proxy for calendar time. More practically, 0.25 cm segments from a standard 50–100 strand collection may yield insufficient mass for reliable quantification, particularly for participants with fine or short hair. The protocol should anticipate the need to pool longer segments (0.5–1.0 cm) when sample mass is limiting; this would reduce temporal resolution but preserve the core pharmacokinetic objectives. Second, the HOPE parent study’s HIV incidence of 2.7 per 100 person-years—lower than the 4.4 per 100 person-years expected from ASPIRE placebo-group data [7]—means the available event count for Aim 2 is approximately 35 rather than 60, which affects the precision of threshold estimates rather than the feasibility of the comparative validation. The study remains fully capable of characterizing the correlation between hair dapivirine and residual ring drug levels across the full cohort.

**The resource plan leverages existing trial infrastructure.** Staff training includes demonstrations for different hairstyles, and the standardized specimen protocol—orientation marking, foil wrapping, desiccant packaging, and room-temperature storage before batch shipment—is practical for resource-limited field settings [1]. The inclusion of capacity-building activities with African laboratories in the final project year [1] is a forward-looking extension that depends on the prior aims being completed successfully and does not threaten the core deliverables.

## Ethics and safety

**Ethics and safety: B (good); confidence B (high).**

The project adds a single, low-risk procedure—small-volume occipital hair collection—to three parent studies that already operate under established ethics approvals and data and safety monitoring frameworks. The risks are proportionately managed, and the mitigation measures described are appropriate for the study populations and settings.

**Physical and privacy risks are minor and addressed.** The collection of approximately 50–100 hair strands cut close to the occipital scalp from beneath the upper hair layer typically takes approximately 90 seconds [1]. Accidental minor scalp injury is acknowledged, and the exclusion of individuals with completely bald scalps—for whom axillary or pubic hair is explicitly not substituted—appropriately avoids attempting specimen collection where the planned anatomical site is unavailable [1]. The procedure is materially less invasive than phlebotomy, requires no cold chain for storage or shipping, and has demonstrated high acceptability in the relevant populations: in the SEARCH PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit, and a separate SEARCH-community study achieved approximately 95% acceptability among 837 participants [1]. Confidentiality risks are addressed through standardized labeling, orientation tracking, and trial-network laboratory data systems [1]. Monthly data-quality review by coordinators and statisticians will identify breaches, which will be reported through study leadership [1].

**The ethical governance framework relies appropriately on the parent studies.** SEARCH and HOPE have existing ethics approvals and monitoring boards, and ACTG A5359 arrangements are being established [1]. Consent for the added hair collection is included in the parent-study consent processes. The SEARCH study permits enrollment of adolescents aged 15–17 years only with parental co-consent, while HOPE and A5359 involve adults, which appropriately addresses the additional protections needed for minors [1].

**Two provisions would benefit from additional specification.** First, staff training includes demonstrations for different hairstyles [1], which is important given the diversity of hair textures, styles (braids, weaves, shaved patterns), and cultural practices across the study populations in East and Southern Africa. The proposal could strengthen this by describing whether training materials were developed with community input and whether ongoing feedback mechanisms exist to identify collection difficulties that may differentially affect participants with particular hair characteristics. Second, the proposal does not specify a protocol for managing incidental findings—for example, if segmental hair analysis revealing drug exposure patterns suggests non-adherence in a treatment context (Aim 3), or if hair concentrations indicate substantially higher or lower exposure than clinically expected. The parent-study protocols likely govern the clinical management of participants, but the proposal does not address whether hair-derived exposure information will be fed back to participants or their clinicians during the study. These are refinements rather than ethical deficiencies; the core risks are low and the existing oversight structures provide appropriate protection.

## Management

**Management: B (good); confidence B (high).**

The management structure draws on established leadership from each parent study with clearly demarcated responsibilities and complementary expertise spanning the clinical, pharmacokinetic, and statistical dimensions of the project. The coordination plan is pragmatic and leverages existing trial infrastructure; the principal gap is the under-specification of the analytical workflow for the two-phase, repeated-measures statistical analyses, though this is addressable within the team’s demonstrated capacity.

**The leadership team is appropriately constituted.** Gandhi leads the hair-measurement program and brings the core analytical expertise from the UCSF Hair Analytical Laboratory [1] [4]. The parent-study leads—Havlir for SEARCH, Baeten for MTN-025/HOPE, and Castillo-Mancilla for ACTG A5359—provide the clinical and operational authority to integrate hair collection into ongoing trial visit schedules and to access participant-level data [1]. Pharmacokinetic and analytical chemistry expertise is supplied by Benet and Gerona, and statistical design and analysis by Bacchetti [1]. This distribution covers the critical competencies: clinical trial operations in East and Southern Africa and the United States, bioanalytical method development, population pharmacokinetics, and complex survey-weighted statistical inference.

**The operational coordination plan is efficient and appropriate for an ancillary study.** Hair collection training will be provided to study coordinators at each site, with demonstrations for different hairstyles [1]. Monthly data-quality review by coordinators and statisticians will monitor collection completeness and identify sites or staff requiring retraining [1]. Specimen management follows a standardized protocol that is practical for resource-limited field settings [1]. The use of existing trial-network laboratory data systems for specimen tracking and quality assurance avoids duplicative infrastructure [1].

**The schedule is tied to the parent studies’ follow-up periods.** SEARCH spans approximately three years, HOPE ring use extends for 12 months plus an exit visit, and the A5359 regimen continues through week 76 [1]. The case-cohort sampling strategy—assaying only the selected subsamples after outcome ascertainment—concentrates the laboratory workload in the later project years, which is a logical sequencing [1]. The final-year capacity-building activities with African laboratories associated with SEARCH and MTN-025 are a forward-looking extension [1].

**The most material gap is the absence of a specified workflow for the statistical analyses** that must accommodate both the survey-weighted estimation from the two-phase sampling design and the clustered variance estimation for repeated measures. The pooled logistic regression for Aim 1 requires robust variance estimators for within-participant clustering, and the inverse-probability weights from the dual-outcome enrichment require careful specification of participant-level versus visit-level weights [1]. Bacchetti’s involvement as the statistical lead is noted, but the proposal does not describe how the analytical workflow will be implemented—for example, which software will be used, how the sampling weights will be constructed and documented, and whether a statistical analysis plan will be finalized before outcome ascertainment. These are operational details rather than fundamental capacity limitations; the team includes the necessary statistical expertise, and the methods are implementable with standard software. Explicit specification would strengthen the management plan.

**The dissemination plan is outlined in broad terms.** Blinded hair-concentration datasets will be made publicly available after planned analyses and publications, and findings will be shared with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders [1]. No specific repositories, timelines, or data documentation standards are specified, which is a minor gap in an otherwise well-coordinated operational plan.
