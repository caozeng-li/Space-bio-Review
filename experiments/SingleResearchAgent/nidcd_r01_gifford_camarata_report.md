# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project will conduct the first randomized, double-blind, waitlist-controlled trial of image-guided cochlear implant programming (IGCIP) in 72 children, aged 6–12, with prelingual deafness. IGCIP uses CT imaging to identify and deactivate electrodes likely providing redundant or extracochlear stimulation, aiming to improve the quality of the auditory signal delivered to the auditory nerve [1]. The primary value lies not only in testing IGCIP’s efficacy for improving speech recognition, but more importantly in investigating how individual variation in auditory function change relates to downstream development of phonological awareness, speech production, language, and literacy over a 24-month follow-up. The study’s sophisticated path-analytic framework, designed to elucidate the mechanisms by which a peripheral intervention may or may not cascade into higher-level skills, addresses a critical gap in understanding why many children with cochlear implants continue to show persistent language and reading difficulties despite early implantation [1].

The proposal is supported by extensive preliminary data from the same team demonstrating feasibility and substantial inter-individual variability in response to IGCIP, as well as by external evidence motivating the investigation of non-spectral auditory pathways for language development. The study design is a major strength, incorporating a deferred-treatment control, baseline map optimization, a two-month stabilization period, and blinded outcome assessors to isolate the intervention effect from maturation and practice. The team led by Rene Gifford and Stephen Camarata is exceptionally qualified, with complementary expertise in auditory perception and child language development, and the management plan is well-organized with a project manager, trained staff, monthly team meetings, and a five-year implementation schedule with staggered cohorts [1].

The decisive limitation is that the sample size of 60 analyzed participants (30 per group), while adequate for detecting a moderate-to-large treatment effect (Cohen’s d equivalent 0.67–0.74) [1], provides limited statistical power for the complex cross-lagged and path-analytic models that represent the proposal’s most novel scientific contribution. Detectable path correlations are approximately .35 and differences between path coefficients approximately .4–.5 [1], which are moderate-to-large effects. The resulting confidence intervals are likely to be wide enough to accommodate substantively different interpretations of the developmental relationships among auditory, linguistic, and literacy domains. The proposal acknowledges this limitation and responds appropriately by shifting emphasis to effect-size estimation and confidence intervals rather than null-hypothesis testing [1].

On balance, the proposal’s considerable strengths—a clinically and theoretically important question, a well-matched team, and a carefully controlled design for its primary aim—outweigh this central limitation. The main risk is not that the study will fail, but that its most ambitious and distinctive scientific contribution, the mechanistic pathway analysis, will be underpowered. This is a known and accepted risk. The resulting data will be highly valuable regardless of the precision of the path models, providing the first randomized evidence for pediatric IGCIP and a uniquely rich longitudinal dataset on auditory and linguistic development in children with cochlear implants.

**Recommendation: Support.** The randomized trial design, safety measures, and analysis plan are sufficient to achieve its primary and secondary aims. The study should be supported as proposed.

**Overall conclusion: Support**

## Scientific value

**Rating: B (good). Confidence: A (very high).**

The project addresses a clinically significant and undertreated problem with a design that yields valuable knowledge at multiple levels. The attainable contributions are structured in layers that function independently of one another, so that even where the most ambitious analyses remain preliminary, the study produces useful scientific and clinical evidence.

The persistent communication difficulties experienced by children with cochlear implants are well-documented. Many children implanted early still show language and reading outcomes below age expectations. The proposal documents this gap directly with group-level standard scores that remain beneath the normative range even for children implanted before 24 months [1]. The quality of the electrical signal delivered to the auditory nerve is a plausible, modifiable constraint on subsequent development. Children with prelingual deafness often demonstrate poorer spectral resolution than adult implant users, and spectral resolution does not consistently predict their speech recognition [1]. These observations motivate the investigation of alternative auditory dimensions—temporal-envelope and spectrotemporal information—as contributors to developmental outcomes. The proposal’s central premise, that anatomically informed programming could alter the electrode–neural interface and thereby provide a controlled source of auditory change for testing these relationships [1], is well-grounded in both the signal-processing rationale and the preliminary evidence.

**A randomized test of IGCIP efficacy.** The first layer of attainable knowledge is the randomized comparison of immediate versus deferred IGCIP on spectral, temporal, and spectrotemporal resolution and on speech recognition, particularly in noise \[证据17, Aim 1\]. The existing pediatric evidence consists of uncontrolled, unblinded case series from this team. Noble et al. (2016) reported significant improvement in 21 pediatric ears after approximately four weeks [2]. The preliminary data on 41 children showed that in the experimental ear, only 16% improved significantly on quiet speech tests and 28% improved on sentence-in-noise tests, while most showed no change—79% in quiet and 69% in noise [1]. These data demonstrate feasibility and substantial variability but cannot separate IGCIP effects from maturation, practice, or expectation. The waitlist-controlled design directly addresses this gap by providing 12 months of untreated growth data in the deferred arm [1]. Establishing whether IGCIP produces a reliable average benefit, and characterizing the magnitude and variability of that benefit under controlled conditions, would be a genuine clinical contribution—providing the level of evidence that current practice lacks.

**Individual variation as a scientific resource.** The second and more distinctive layer exploits individual variation in auditory change to investigate developmental pathways, whether or not an average treatment effect emerges [1]. The team’s published cross-sectional work demonstrates that children with cochlear implants can achieve typical-range reading comprehension despite significantly below-average phonological processing [3], suggesting that alternative developmental routes exist—for example, through oral language comprehension rather than through phonological decoding. The proposal’s path-analytic framework, connecting auditory resolution changes to speech recognition, phonological awareness, language, and reading over 24 months [1], would test these alternative routes directly. This mechanistic knowledge would be valuable regardless of IGCIP’s average efficacy, because it addresses the fundamental question of which auditory dimensions support which developmental outcomes in this population. The correlations between sentence-recognition changes and receptive-language gains observed in 17 preliminary participants—for example, pooled BabyBio sentence-recognition change correlating .48 with vocabulary change and .57 with grammatical morpheme change [1]—provide empirical grounds for expecting the longitudinal design to reveal relationships that cross-sectional studies cannot.

**Detection of subclinical change.** A further contribution is the capacity to detect functionally important changes that standardized clinical instruments miss. The preliminary case data show children whose standard articulation scores did not change after IGCIP, yet acoustic analysis revealed increased high-frequency speech energy and improved vowel precision [1]. Another child showed improved consonant precision and duration without recognition benefit [1]. By including automated acoustic analyses—harmonic-to-noise ratio, formant analysis, vowel space, spectral energy, speaking rate—alongside norm-referenced tests [1], the study can capture production changes that precede or bypass conventional clinical thresholds.

**Precision constraints on the most ambitious analyses.** A limitation that affects the scientific value is the precision available for the pathway analyses. With 60 analyzed participants, the study is powered for the treatment contrast but yields wide confidence intervals for the cross-lagged and path coefficients [1]. The pathway component is therefore better characterized as generating well-constrained hypotheses and effect-size estimates for future work, rather than as definitively resolving the mechanisms. The proposal acknowledges this by emphasizing estimation over null-hypothesis testing [1], and this framing is appropriate. Even imprecise pathway estimates from a randomized longitudinal design would advance beyond the existing cross-sectional and uncontrolled evidence. The dataset itself—comprehensive auditory, linguistic, and literacy measures repeated over 24 months in a well-characterized sample—has independent descriptive and hypothesis-generating value that does not depend on the precision of any single coefficient.

## Innovation

**Rating: B (good). Confidence: A (very high).**

The project advances beyond the existing evidence base through three interconnected innovations. Together they shift the study from a standard efficacy test toward a mechanistic investigation of how a peripheral intervention might cascade into higher-level developmental skills. Each advance is well-motivated by the limitations of prior work.

**First randomized evidence for pediatric IGCIP.** The most visible innovation is methodological. The published evidence for IGCIP in children consists entirely of uncontrolled, unblinded case series from this same team. Noble et al. (2016) reported statistically significant improvement for word and sentence recognition in quiet and noise in 21 pediatric ears at approximately four weeks [2]. The proposal’s own preliminary series of 41 children confirmed feasibility and substantial individual variability but could not distinguish intervention effects from maturation or practice [1]. The double-blind, randomized waitlist-controlled design—with permuted-block allocation (blocks of four), a two-month baseline stabilization period after clinical map optimization, and blinded outcome assessors [1]—provides the control condition that the field currently lacks. The deferred-treatment arm generates 12 months of untreated growth data that directly addresses the maturation confound [1]. The within-child comparisons available in bilateral users, where only one ear receives IGCIP while the unmodified ear and the bilateral condition serve as concurrent controls [1], add analytical sensitivity absent from the existing literature. The baselines are carefully prepared: conventional clinical maps are optimized to achieve CI-aided thresholds of 20–25 dB HL from 250–6,000 Hz, with electrically evoked stapedial reflex thresholds or behavioral measures confirming upper stimulation levels, and contralateral hearing aids verified by real-ear measurement [1]. This ensures that the comparator represents current best practice.

**Variable auditory change as a scientific resource.** A more subtle conceptual innovation is the treatment of individual variation as the primary scientific resource rather than a nuisance. The preliminary data show that most children did not demonstrate significant speech-recognition improvement after IGCIP in the experimental ear—79% were unchanged in quiet, 69% unchanged in noise—while 16% and 28% improved, respectively [1]. A conventional trial might focus exclusively on whether the average improvement is statistically significant. This proposal instead builds its developmental aims around individual differences: the path-analytic framework tests whether changes in auditory resolution predict changes in phonological awareness, language, and reading, regardless of whether the group-level treatment contrast is significant \[证据17, Aims 2–3\] [1]. Nonresponders remain informative, and outcome follow-up continues where possible [1]. This reframing is scientifically productive because the question of which auditory dimensions support which developmental outcomes persists whether or not IGCIP produces a mean benefit, and the randomized design provides the controlled variation in auditory change needed to address it.

**Longitudinal pathway modeling within a controlled intervention.** The third innovation is the integration of developmental pathway analysis into a randomized intervention study. Prior cross-sectional work has established that children with cochlear implants can achieve typical-range reading comprehension despite significantly below-average phonological processing [3], and that neither spectral nor temporal resolution significantly correlates with speech recognition in this population after correction for multiple comparisons [4]. These findings motivate the investigation of alternative developmental routes—through temporal or spectrotemporal processing, or through lexical restructuring—but cross-sectional designs cannot distinguish developmental precedence from correlated outcomes. The proposal’s cross-lagged and path-analytic models, with repeated measures of auditory resolution, speech recognition, phonological awareness, language, and reading at specified intervals over 24 months [1], directly test whether earlier auditory changes predict later linguistic and literacy changes. The conceptual framework allows both direct auditory-to-language pathways and speech-recognition-mediated pathways [1], testing the proposal’s own theoretical commitment rather than assuming a single causal chain. This goes substantially beyond both the existing IGCIP efficacy literature and the broader pediatric cochlear implant outcome literature, neither of which has combined a controlled peripheral intervention with longitudinal developmental pathway modeling.

The innovation is appropriately tempered by the recognition, discussed under Rigor, that the 60-subject sample limits the precision of the pathway estimates. The resulting models will generate effect-size estimates and hypotheses for future work rather than definitive mechanistic conclusions. This does not diminish the originality of the approach; it means that the most innovative component will produce preliminary rather than conclusive evidence, which is appropriate for the first study of its kind.

## Rigor

**Rating: B (good). Confidence: A (very high).**

The design provides strong internal validity for the primary treatment contrast, and the measurement approach is thorough and well-justified. The central limitation is that the sample size, while adequate for the group-level efficacy comparison, constrains the precision of the developmental pathway analyses. This limitation is inherent to the study’s pioneering stage and is partially mitigated by the proposal’s explicit shift toward effect-size estimation.

**Internal validity for the treatment contrast.** Several design features collectively strengthen causal inference. The permuted-block randomization with blocks of four, generated by the statistician with allocation access restricted to the PIs and statistician, coupled with blinding of participants and outcome assessors [1], minimizes selection and expectation biases. The baseline map optimization and two-month stabilization period [1] ensure that the pre-intervention condition represents current best practice rather than a suboptimal comparator. Equipment and listening checks at every study visit reduce the risk that hardware malfunction is misinterpreted as a treatment effect. The deferred-treatment arm generates 12 months of untreated within-child growth [1], providing a direct estimate of maturation and practice effects. The group-by-time contrast specified in the analysis plan—testing whether immediate treatment changes trajectories relative to waiting, with a hypothesized near-zero waitlist slope and a positive immediate-treatment slope [1]—is the appropriate test for this design. Intent-to-treat principles, type-I error control at .05 or below, and general linear mixed or multilevel models for group differences in change [1] provide an appropriate analytical framework.

**Comprehensive measurement aligned with inferential goals.** The measurement battery is well-matched to the proposal’s conceptual motivation. Auditory resolution tasks span spectral modulation detection at 0.5 and 1.0 cycles per octave, temporal modulation detection at 4, 32, and 128 Hz (sampling the low-rate plateau and higher-rate decline of the modulation-transfer function), and spectrotemporal combined conditions [1]. This range directly addresses the proposal’s aim to compare spectral and temporal contributions rather than assuming one dimension dominates [1]. The use of a three-interval, two-alternative forced-choice format with two-down/one-up tracking targeting 70.7% correct, supported by time-locked cartoon images and touchscreen responses [1], is well-suited to maintaining children’s engagement during nonlexical psychophysical tasks.

Speech recognition is assessed with multiple instruments: CNC monosyllables, nonword repetition, BabyBio sentences, and BKB-SIN in quiet and noise, plus an adaptive HINT task with semidiffuse noise [1]. Nonword repetition reduces lexical support and may be more sensitive to altered auditory detail. Multiple lists permit repeated testing without item-list repetition across the seven-visit schedule.

The developmental battery includes multiple standardized tests per construct—ROWPVT-4, PPVT-4, TACL-4 subscales, and CELF-4 for receptive language; EOWPVT-4, SPELT-3, and CELF-4 for expressive language; CTOPP-2 and TAPS-3 plus a developmentally ordered experimental battery for phonological awareness; WRMT-III and GORT-5 for reading [1]. This multiplicity reduces dependence on any single instrument and permits the study to distinguish vocabulary from grammar and decoding from comprehension. The inclusion of nonverbal cognition (Leiter-3) and working memory (Woodcock-Johnson IV Numbers Reversed plus experimental serial recall and visuospatial tasks) [1] supports the planned covariate adjustment. Subclinical acoustic measures—harmonic-to-noise ratio, fundamental frequency distribution, articulation precision, formants and vowel space, long-term spectrum, speaking rate—supplement standardized articulation scores and can detect production changes before they appear in norm-referenced instruments [1], as the preliminary case data already illustrate [1].

**Limitation: precision of pathway analyses.** The 60-subject analyzed sample (30 per group) is powered for the group-by-time treatment contrast—80% power at two-sided alpha .05 for effects of 0.32 adjusted for baseline (Cohen’s d equivalent 0.67) or 0.35 unadjusted (d equivalent 0.74) [1]. For the path-analytic and cross-lagged models, N = 60 permits detection of a path correlation near .35 and differences between path coefficients of approximately .4–.5 [1]. These are moderate-to-large effects. Published developmental path models routinely employ samples several times larger to achieve stable parameter estimates, and small-sample structural equation modeling is known to produce wide confidence intervals and inflated fit indices. With only three measurement occasions for the language and literacy battery (baseline, 12 months, and 24 months) [1], the temporal resolution for cross-lagged models testing whether earlier auditory changes predict later linguistic changes is coarse.

The proposal’s response to this constraint is appropriate: it emphasizes effect-size estimation and bootstrapped confidence intervals over null-hypothesis testing for the path analyses [1]. This aligns with contemporary reporting standards and acknowledges that the pathway component will generate well-constrained hypotheses for larger investigations rather than definitive mechanistic conclusions. The analytical plan’s provision that pathway analyses proceed even without a significant average treatment effect [1] is an important safeguard that protects the study’s most distinctive contribution against the risk that the treatment contrast is smaller than the powered effect size.

**Missing data and sample integrity.** The handling of missing data is appropriately conservative for a clinical-trial context. The expectation that most missing assessments will be nonrandom, the decision not to impute for those assessments, and the use of bootstrapping for analyses with incomplete repeated assessments [1] are defensible choices. A sensitivity analysis evaluating the plausible impact of nonrandom missingness on the pathway estimates would further strengthen the plan. The withdrawal rule for participants whose IGCIP plan is identical to their clinical map [1] is logically necessary—no programming intervention then exists to test—but the frequency of this occurrence is not estimated from preliminary data, introducing some uncertainty about the final analyzed sample size that could modestly affect the already-limited pathway precision.

## Feasibility

**Rating: B (good). Confidence: B (high).**

The study is realistically achievable in its core components given the team’s demonstrated experience, institutional access, and the carefully staged five-year plan. The principal remaining uncertainty concerns recruitment throughput, which has a sound basis but lacks a detailed pipeline quantification; this affects confidence rather than the fundamental judgement.

**Demonstrated operational experience.** The team has already performed IGCIP on 41 pediatric CI users aged 4.4–17.9 years using the same image-processing pipeline, measurement battery, and clinical programming procedures proposed here [1]. The imaging workflow—atlas-based registration of preoperative CT to a micro-CT-derived cochlear atlas, postoperative electrode localization, and distance-versus-frequency analysis to identify electrodes whose stimulation regions overlap those of neighboring contacts [1]—has been established and refined through this prior work. The intervention is delivered through standard clinical programming tools [1]; no novel device or surgical procedure is required. The measurement battery, including the child-adapted psychophysical tasks, has been administered successfully in the preliminary series. That 31 of 41 children chose to retain the IGCIP map [1] provides practical evidence that the intervention is acceptable to families and does not generate unmanageable attrition from the treatment arm. Of 23 children followed for one year, 16 retained the map and showed continued group-level improvement [1], further supporting feasibility of longitudinal retention.

**Recruitment capacity.** Recruitment draws on Vanderbilt’s large pediatric cochlear implant program [1]. The five-year enrollment window with staggered cohorts targets approximately 12–14 children during summer and 5–10 during winter breaks [1], translating to roughly 17–24 children per year. For a program of this size, attracting 72 children meeting the specific criteria—prelingual deafness, implantation before age three, current age 6–12, nonverbal IQ ≥ 85, bilateral moderate-to-profound loss, and exclusion of major temporal-bone abnormalities such as common cavity or ossification [1]—is plausible. The age window of six years means that children becoming age-eligible during the project can supplement the existing clinical population. The proposal does not, however, provide a formal recruitment feasibility analysis documenting the number of potentially eligible children currently followed or the typical annual inflow of new age-eligible patients. This is a minor gap rather than a decisive weakness; the team’s extensive clinical involvement and the program’s size make the target credible, but the absence of a quantified pipeline introduces some uncertainty about the achievable pace of enrollment, which could compress the follow-up schedule for later cohorts.

**Sample size contingencies.** The withdrawal rule for children whose IGCIP plan turns out identical to their clinical map [1] is logically necessary but introduces an unquantified attrition risk. In the preliminary data, the fraction of children whose deactivation analysis yields no change is not separately reported, although most children did show some alteration and the majority retained the IGCIP map. If this fraction is non-trivial, the enrolled count of 72 would need to absorb these withdrawals to maintain the target of 60 analyzed participants. The five-year schedule with built-in recruitment across multiple years provides some buffer.

**Implementation schedule.** The five-year plan in [1] is detailed and well-paced. Year 1 focuses on hiring the project manager and PhD student, training, assessment acquisition, trial registration, and initial recruitment; the first cohort begins their two-year visit sequence. Years 2–3 overlap follow-up of earlier cohorts with recruitment and early visits for new entrants, making efficient use of staff time. Years 4–5 complete longitudinal assessments and transition to integrated cross-aim analyses and dissemination. The assignment of a project manager and trained research staff to coordinate visits, combined with monthly team meetings \[證據61\]\[證據64\], provides the operational infrastructure needed to sustain a complex seven-visit protocol across five years. The specification that a no-cost extension may be requested to complete dissemination \[證據63\] is a realistic acknowledgment of the time required for the cross-aim pathway analyses and manuscript preparation after the final 24-month visit.

The interaction between recruitment uncertainty and the already-limited sample size for pathway analyses, discussed under Rigor, modestly tempers confidence. If enrollment proceeds more slowly than planned, or if withdrawals reduce the analyzed sample below 60, the precision of the pathway estimates would deteriorate further. The core trial comparison and the descriptive value of the longitudinal dataset remain attainable even if the analyzed sample falls somewhat short of the target.

## Ethics and safety

**Rating: A (excellent). Confidence: A (very high).**

The ethical framework, risk management, and participant protections are carefully designed and proportionate to the study’s procedures. The proposal addresses the principal ethical challenges—the vulnerability of the pediatric population, the potential for performance decline after reprogramming, the use of ionizing radiation, and the obligation to provide access to a potentially beneficial treatment—through specific, well-specified safeguards that reflect the team’s clinical experience with this intervention.

**Deferred-treatment design.** All participants receive IGCIP: the immediate arm after baseline and the deferred arm at the 12-month visit [1]. This addresses the concern that a placebo or no-treatment control would withhold a potentially beneficial intervention from children who might need it, while still generating the untreated growth comparison that the scientific question demands. The 24-month follow-up for both arms means the study contributes longitudinal data on treated development regardless of initial randomization.

**Safety monitoring for performance decline.** The most direct potential harm of reprogramming is reduced speech recognition, and the safety provisions are specifically calibrated to this risk. The preliminary data show that 5% of children worsened in quiet speech tests and 3% worsened in sentence-in-noise tests after IGCIP, while no child showed a significant decrement in the bilateral aided condition [1]. At baseline and 12 months, staff establish smartphone-based word-recognition scores; caregivers repeat testing at home one month later [1]. A decline exceeding the 95% test-retest interval triggers a pre-specified protocol: the option to restore the prior clinical map immediately, or to continue for one additional month until the next scheduled visit [1]. The one-month home-monitoring interval minimizes the duration of any undetected performance decline. The explicit provision for unmasking a participant whose performance warrants map restoration [1] appropriately prioritizes clinical welfare over blinding integrity for that participant, while preserving the option to continue two-year outcome observation.

**Radiation exposure.** The use of imaging radiation is managed proportionately. The proposal prioritizes existing clinically obtained CT scans; outside-implanted children may receive low-dose imaging through a linked approved protocol subject to institutional radiation oversight [1]. The requirement that preoperative CT be available and that postoperative imaging be either available or obtained through the approved imaging protocol after consent [1] ensures no child receives unnecessary scans solely for study purposes.

**Baseline optimization as ethical safeguard.** The baseline optimization procedure serves an ethical as well as methodological function. By verifying that conventional clinical maps achieve CI-aided thresholds of 20–25 dB HL from 250–6,000 Hz, with electrically evoked stapedial reflex thresholds or behavioral measures confirming upper stimulation levels, and by verifying contralateral hearing aid fittings through real-ear measurement [1], the protocol ensures children enter the study with optimized current management. Children in the deferred arm are not left with suboptimal clinical programming during their waiting period, and any performance decline after IGCIP cannot be attributed to correctable deficiencies in the baseline map.

**Consent, data protection, and dissemination.** Parental consent and child assent under institutional procedures, coded data with a separate identity master list retained by the PIs, secured paper and electronic records, access limited to research personnel, and a retention period of at least six years after study completion \[證據66\]\[證據68\] meet standard expectations. The plan to provide families with reports of their children’s assessments \[證據68\] extends the benefit of participation beyond the intervention itself and supports retention. Adverse events will be documented and reported, with independent monitoring organized if the IRB requires it \[證據67\]. The deferred arm’s access to IGCIP at 12 months and the safety-net provision for map restoration together ensure that the risk–benefit ratio is acceptable for a pediatric population that already faces significant communication challenges and for whom the intervention has shown preliminary evidence of benefit without serious adverse outcomes in the team’s prior work.

## Management

**Rating: A (excellent). Confidence: A (very high).**

The leadership, team composition, division of responsibilities, and operational infrastructure are exceptionally well-matched to the project’s interdisciplinary demands, and the five-year implementation plan is realistic and appropriately detailed. The management arrangements directly reflect the team’s collaborative track record on this exact line of work.

**Leadership and team composition.** The principal investigators provide complementary expertise spanning the full scope of the proposal. Gifford leads auditory perception, psychophysical measurement, and CI programming and will oversee recruitment and the auditory aims; Camarata leads speech, language, and literacy development and will oversee the corresponding assessment and analysis [1]. This division maps onto the two major scientific domains the study must integrate, and the preliminary data already demonstrate productive collaboration between these domains, including joint analyses of sentence-recognition changes and language gains in the preliminary series [1].

The broader team fills the remaining critical functions without redundancy or gap. Noble and Dawant contribute image-analysis and engineering expertise for the IGCIP processing pipeline [1]. Labadie provides otologic input on electrode placement and clinical imaging. The named consultants cover phonological processing and speech production—areas where the measurement battery extends beyond the PIs’ primary research programs [1]. A biostatistician is integrated into the team, responsible for generating the allocation schedule and supporting the planned mixed-model, cross-lagged, and path analyses \[證據34\]\[證據61\]. Every technical component—imaging, programming, auditory assessment, language assessment, and statistical modeling—has a named expert, and the group has a history of direct collaborative publication on pediatric IGCIP, including Noble et al. (2016) and the preliminary work described throughout the proposal [1] [2].

**Operational coordination.** A project manager and trained research staff will coordinate visits and assessments \[證據61\], providing the personnel infrastructure for a protocol with seven formal visits per participant over 24 months, supplemented by home-based word-recognition checks [1]. Monthly team meetings are specified throughout the five-year period \[證據64\], which is appropriate for a longitudinal study where cohort overlap, data quality, and protocol fidelity require continuous oversight.

**Implementation schedule.** The five-year plan in \[證據63\] is well-paced. Year 1 concentrates on hiring and training the project manager and PhD student, acquiring assessments, registering the trial, and recruiting the first cohort of approximately 17–20 children across summer and winter. Year 2 continues recruitment while completing 6- and 12-month visits for earlier cohorts. Year 3 recruits additional children and completes visits through 24 months for year-1 entrants. Year 4 completes most longitudinal visits and begins submitting auditory-perception analyses. Year 5 completes remaining visits, unblinds the data, and conducts integrated cross-aim analyses. The plan specifies manuscript preparation and dissemination in the final year, with a no-cost extension acknowledged as a possibility \[證據63\].

**Data management and reproducibility.** REDCap and a centralized repository will store coded data including imaging and behavioral measurements, with a separate identity master list retained by the PIs and access limited to research personnel \[證據68\]. The commitment to depositing de-identified data, methodological detail, and raw data in supplements or shared repositories meets contemporary expectations for data sharing in federally funded research.

At the level of evidence provided in the proposal narrative, the management arrangements are coherent, comprehensive, and well-supported by the team’s track record. The proposal documents specific roles, relevant preliminary work, institutional access, and a demonstrated ability to recruit and retain participants in IGCIP studies. The one aspect that prevents fully unqualified confidence—the absence of detailed CVs, formal letters of support, or itemized budget information in the reviewed materials—limits independent verification but does not undermine the judgement, because the narrative itself provides substantial evidence of capacity.
