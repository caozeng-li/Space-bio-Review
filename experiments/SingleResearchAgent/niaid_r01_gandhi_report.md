# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project aims to validate and apply antiretroviral concentrations in scalp hair as a long-term, cumulative measure of drug exposure and adherence, and to integrate it with shorter-term metrics across three established clinical platforms. The planned work spans three linked aims: evaluating hair tenofovir levels to predict HIV seroconversion and renal toxicity among oral PrEP users in East Africa; evaluating hair dapivirine as an adherence measure for a vaginal ring; and evaluating segmental hair cabotegravir for pharmacokinetic monitoring in a long-acting injectable treatment setting. The most valuable expected knowledge is a validated, integrated exposure-monitoring framework that moves beyond single snapshots to characterize patterns of drug use, providing a practical tool for interpreting outcomes in both prevention and treatment programs.

The proposal is supported by a strong foundation of preliminary work, including a validated dose-response relationship between tenofovir dosing and hair concentrations and high acceptability of hair collection in the target populations. The exceptional feasibility, stemming from embedding within well-resourced parent studies, and the outstanding investigator team are major strengths. The fundamental limitation is that a directly observed ring-use calibration study, analogous to the one performed for oral PrEP, is absent for dapivirine, which limits the quantitative interpretation of hair levels for this delivery method. The analysis of virologic outcomes for cabotegravir is constrained by a modest sample size, a limitation that is appropriately managed by framing the analyses as exploratory. These limitations do not negate the project's core value but constrain the specificity of conclusions for two of the three aims.

The project represents a substantial and well-justified scientific contribution that balances strong feasibility and high potential for immediate translational impact against well-defined and appropriately managed limitations. Aim 1, the core of the project, is robust and will deliver results with high confidence. The exploratory nature of Aims 2 and 3 is a responsible strategy for generating foundational data in new contexts, but their structural reliance on associational evidence limits their contribution. The project's clear strengths in addressing a practical, high-priority need, its exceptional feasibility, and the rigor of its principal aim outweigh the recognized, inherent limitations of its more exploratory components.

**Overall conclusion: Support**

## Scientific value

**The project addresses a pressing and well-defined need for objective, cumulative exposure monitoring in HIV prevention and treatment, promising an integrated framework with immediate practical value.**

The central gap is well established: the effectiveness of oral PrEP, vaginal rings, and long-acting injectables depends critically on actual drug exposure, yet the commonly used adherence measures each capture only a partial or potentially misleading picture [1]. Self-report is unreliable; plasma levels can be inflated by a single dose taken immediately before a clinic visit; and residual ring drug may vary with handling conditions [1] [2]. The project's core hypothesis, that pairing a long-term cumulative measure (hair) with shorter-term measures will better characterize exposure patterns and predict clinical outcomes than any single snapshot [1], is both logically sound and strongly supported by prior evidence.

### Aim 1: oral PrEP in SEARCH

The scientific value of Aim 1 is the most mature and substantial. The relationship between tenofovir dosing frequency and hair concentration has been rigorously established through a directly observed dosing study, which demonstrated a strong log-linear relationship with a 76% increase in hair tenofovir level per two-fold dose increase [3]. Median hair tenofovir levels were 0.012, 0.023, and 0.038 ng/mg for 2, 4, and 7 doses per week respectively, with minimal overlap between the lowest and highest dosing conditions [3]. This calibration permits interpretation of hair levels as approximate doses per week, not merely as a binary exposure indicator, which is essential for adherence pattern analyses that distinguish sustained from intermittent use [1].

The Abaasa et al. (2018) study demonstrated that hair tenofovir was the single best pharmacokinetic measure to predict electronically monitored adherence (area under the receiver operating curve 0.85), and that combining hair with plasma improved discrimination to above 0.9 [2]. This directly validates the proposal's core premise about the incremental value of combining measures. A systematic review encompassing 31 studies across 11 antiretroviral drugs confirmed that hair antiretroviral concentration is a valid adherence biomarker and one of the strongest independent predictors of virologic outcomes, with tenofovir hair levels also significantly associated with renal toxicity in large studies [4].

The project extends this foundation in two practically important directions. First, it will relate graded hair tenofovir exposure to renal function decline. Prior work demonstrated that six months of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate/emtricitabine PrEP was associated with measurable declines in estimated glomerular filtration rate and changes in tubular health biomarkers [5], supporting the biological plausibility of a dose-response relationship between cumulative tenofovir exposure and renal outcomes. Second, the SEARCH platform provides a large, diverse population of approximately 6,400 PrEP recipients across 32 communities in Uganda and Kenya [1], enabling the characterization of exposure patterns among clinically relevant subgroups including young women, mobile populations, fisherfolk, and serodiscordant couples [1].

### Aim 2: dapivirine ring in HOPE

Aim 2 extends hair-based monitoring to a delivery method where adherence assessment remains a recognized gap. The 2016 landscape analysis by Stalter et al. identified drug levels in hair as one of four high-priority approaches for vaginal ring adherence measurement, noting it as promising but requiring further development [6]. The project directly responds to this call. By comparing hair dapivirine with plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report in an open-label extension of the ASPIRE trial enrolling approximately 1,800 women across four African countries [1], the project can characterize patterns of ring use—including removal and reinsertion—that would be invisible to any single measure alone [1]. Even without a formal dose-response calibration for the ring (see Rigor), the associational evidence linking hair dapivirine levels to HIV acquisition risk will provide valuable descriptive benchmarks for the field. Age-stratified analyses are particularly relevant given that the phase III trials (ASPIRE and RING) showed substantially lower effectiveness among younger women (18–21 years), where adherence was likely poorer [1].

### Aim 3: cabotegravir in ACTG A5359

Aim 3 addresses the emerging challenge of exposure monitoring for long-acting injectables, where plasma concentrations may fall near or beyond the scheduled dosing interval and missed visits create gaps in the exposure record [1]. The segmental hair analysis at 0.25 cm resolution, interpreted as approximately weekly average exposure [1], offers a retrospective exposure timeline that plasma cannot provide. The Gandhi group has already demonstrated the feasibility of hair cabotegravir measurement in a clinical case, confirming exposure before and after diagnosis with segmental concentrations of 0.190 and 0.950 ng/mg [7]. Scaling this from case-level to systematic cohort-level evaluation with 150 participants and repeated measures represents a meaningful contribution to the toolkit for pharmacokinetic monitoring in populations with prior adherence difficulties [1].

Across all three aims, the project generates knowledge that is directly relevant to real-world programs. The findings will help interpret adherence and exposure patterns, identify participants who may benefit from additional support, and inform the design of tailored adherence interventions [1].

## Innovation

**The project's originality lies in systematically extending a validated hair-based adherence monitoring approach to dapivirine vaginal rings and long-acting injectable cabotegravir, and in constructing an integrated multi-measure framework that spans the major HIV prevention and treatment options.**

For oral tenofovir-based PrEP (Aim 1), the Gandhi group has already published extensively on the dose-response relationship and the application of hair monitoring in the SEARCH cohort [3] [8]. The incremental value of combining hair with plasma has been demonstrated [2]. Aim 1 therefore consolidates and deepens an existing line of investigation in a larger, more diverse population with the addition of renal outcomes, rather than opening a fundamentally new direction. The renal toxicity analysis, relating graded hair tenofovir exposure to declines in estimated glomerular filtration rate, extends prior work [5] but is methodologically incremental within the group's established paradigm.

### Novelty by delivery modality

**The extension to dapivirine (Aim 2) is more novel.** The 2016 Stalter et al. review identified hair drug levels as a promising but undeveloped approach for ring adherence measurement [6]. No directly observed ring-use study has established the quantitative relationship between ring use patterns and hair dapivirine concentrations. The preliminary work demonstrates only that dapivirine can be measured in spiked hair samples via the laboratory's liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry platform [1]. The project is therefore genuinely establishing first-generation evidence for hair dapivirine as an exposure biomarker in a ring context, moving from assay feasibility to population-level characterization. The comparison of hair dapivirine with residual ring drug and plasma represents a novel multi-metric approach specific to ring delivery, where the relationships among these measures are not yet characterized.

For cabotegravir (Aim 3), the innovation lies in scaling from case-level demonstration to systematic cohort evaluation and in refining the temporal resolution of hair analysis. The Gandhi group has published a validated hair cabotegravir assay with a linear dynamic range of 0.00625–3.20 ng/mg [7] and demonstrated segmental analysis in a clinical case [7]. The proposal extends this to 150 participants with repeated measures over 76 weeks and introduces finer segmentation at 0.25 cm resolution to approximate weekly average exposure [1]. This segmental approach for a long-acting injectable, where plasma concentrations are relatively stable between injections, is a distinctive methodological contribution: hair segmentation can retrospectively reconstruct an exposure timeline across missed visits and dosing gaps that plasma sampling cannot provide. The exploratory analyses relating segmental hair levels to virologic suppression represent a first systematic attempt to evaluate hair cabotegravir as a pharmacokinetic monitoring tool in a treatment population with prior adherence difficulties \[45\].

### Integrative design

The integrated design—applying a common specimen collection and analytical platform across oral, ring, and injectable products within a single program—is itself a modest conceptual innovation. It enables cross-modality comparisons of how hair incorporates drug from systemic circulation following different routes and schedules of administration, and it produces a unified framework for exposure monitoring that can inform programs offering multiple prevention options.

## Rigor

**The project's inferential strength is uneven across aims. Aim 1 is built on a rigorous dose-response calibration and appropriate sampling design. Aims 2 and 3 face structural limitations that constrain their quantitative conclusions but are appropriately positioned as exploratory.**

### Shared analytical foundation

The shared specimen collection and analytical platform is methodologically sound. Hair collection of approximately 50–100 strands from the occipital scalp, with distal-end marking for orientation and storage at room temperature in foil with desiccant [1], has demonstrated approximately 95% acceptability in SEARCH community settings [1]. The validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry assays for tenofovir and emtricitabine in hair have coefficients of variation below 15% for quality control samples and limits of quantification of 0.002 ng/mg and 0.02 ng/mg respectively [1] [3] [9]. The cabotegravir assay has a validated range of 0.00625–3.20 ng/mg [7]. The dapivirine assay, supported by spiked chromatograms in the preliminary work [1], should be feasible using the same extraction and analytical platform, though full validation parameters are not yet reported.

### Aim 1: strong design with rigorous calibration

The foundation for Aim 1 is the directly observed dosing study by Liu et al. (2014), which established a strong log-linear relationship between tenofovir dosing frequency and scalp hair concentration, with a 76% increase in hair level per two-fold dose increase (95% CI 60–93%) and minimal inter-individual variability in the dose effect [3]. This calibration permits interpretation of hair levels as approximate doses per week, which is essential for the adherence pattern analyses [1].

The outcome-enriched sampling strategy is statistically efficient and well-justified. Rather than assaying all approximately 83,200 potential hair specimens, the design selects all seroconverters and three randomly sampled controls per case, yielding approximately 5,200 person-visits for the HIV outcome analysis, with an additional approximately 1,300 visits from participants with substantial renal decline [1]. The use of known sampling fractions and certainty sampling of outcome-enriched groups, incorporated through sampling weights, permits valid population-level inference without treating the sample as a simple random cohort. The discrete-time Cox approach implemented through pooled logistic regression, with adjustment for risk factors and assessment of both continuous and categorical hair exposure levels, is appropriate for relating time-varying exposure to HIV seroconversion [1]. The power calculation, indicating at least 80% power for an odds ratio of 2.1 comparing the lowest hair exposure quartile with the upper three [1], is conservative and acknowledges the ancillary nature of the study.

The renal analysis employs certainty sampling of participants reaching clinically meaningful thresholds—estimated creatinine clearance at or below 60 mL/min or a decline exceeding 15%—and uses linear mixed models with adjustment for age, sex, baseline clearance, hypertension, diabetes, and concomitant drugs [1]. This approach appropriately accounts for the longitudinal data structure and the selected nature of the sample.

### Aim 2: structural limitation from absent calibration

Aim 2 faces a more fundamental inferential challenge. The interpretation of hair dapivirine as a quantitative ring-exposure measure depends on establishing that dapivirine enters systemic circulation from vaginal ring delivery and incorporates into hair in a dose-dependent manner that reflects patterns of ring use, including partial removal and reinsertion. No directly observed ring-use study analogous to the STRAND trial has established this relationship. The preliminary work demonstrates that dapivirine can be measured in spiked hair samples [1], which addresses assay feasibility but not the dose-response calibration. Without this calibration, hair dapivirine levels can indicate exposure but cannot distinguish continuous ring use from intermittent use with removal and reinsertion, nor can they estimate the proportion of time the ring was in place. The proposal's stated goal of estimating "concentrations associated with lower HIV acquisition risk" [1] will therefore yield associational rather than calibrated estimates.

This limitation is inherent to the current stage of the science for this delivery method and does not negate the value of generating first-generation descriptive benchmarks, but it does constrain the specificity of conclusions. The weighted pooled logistic models relating hair dapivirine to HIV acquisition, with adjustment for age, region, marital status, and behavioral covariates [1], are appropriate for the associational evidence the design can support. The planned comparison of hair, plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report, individually and in combinations, will characterize patterns of agreement and disagreement among measures, which is valuable even without a calibration standard [1].

### Aim 3: exploratory analyses with modest power

Aim 3 benefits from the validated hair cabotegravir assay [7] and prior case-level demonstration [7]. The 0.25 cm segmentation, interpreted as approximately weekly average exposure based on an assumed growth rate of 1 cm per four weeks [1], enables retrospective reconstruction of exposure timelines across injection intervals and missed visits. The correlation analyses relating hair cabotegravir to trough plasma concentrations, with 150 participants providing a 95% confidence interval of approximately 0.73–0.85 for a single-time-point correlation of 0.8 [1], are adequately powered for descriptive purposes.

However, the exploratory random-effects logistic models relating hair and plasma exposure to virologic suppression below 50 copies/mL are explicitly framed as exploratory and will have limited power to detect moderate effect sizes or to distinguish the independent contributions of hair versus plasma exposure [1]. The proposal appropriately treats these as hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

### Analytical considerations

The adherence analyses across aims employ a mix of descriptive and model-based approaches. Scatterplots and Spearman correlations compare measures, while linear mixed models relate hair levels to participant and delivery factors [1]. The handling of undetectable values—setting them to the detection limit and adding the limit to values before log transformation—is a pragmatic approach to reduce emphasis on very small differences near zero, though alternative methods such as multiple imputation or tobit models could be considered. The classification of longitudinal hair-plasma patterns into categories such as sustained use, intermittent use, and recent dosing before a visit [1] is clinically interpretable, though the criteria for these classifications should be pre-specified to avoid post hoc categorization bias.

## Feasibility

**The project is exceptionally feasible because it is embedded within three well-resourced, ongoing parent studies that already provide the participant populations, clinical infrastructure, staffing, and regulatory approvals needed for specimen collection.** This ancillary design reduces the incremental cost, logistical complexity, and start-up time to a fraction of what a standalone study would require.

### Reliance on established platforms

Aim 1 uses the SEARCH phase 2 trial, which is already re-randomizing 32 communities in Uganda and Kenya and providing oral PrEP to approximately 6,400 participants [1]. The parent study already conducts quarterly visits with HIV testing, symptom and toxicity checks, and creatinine monitoring; the ancillary study adds only hair collection and additional plasma sampling to this schedule [1]. Aim 2 is embedded within MTN-025/HOPE, an open-label extension that will enroll approximately 1,800 women across Malawi, South Africa, Uganda, and Zimbabwe with monthly ring replacement and regular clinic visits [1]. Aim 3 leverages ACTG A5359, which already follows HIV-positive adults through oral induction, randomization, and long-acting injectable treatment through week 76 with existing HIV RNA monitoring and safety labs [1]. In each case, the parent study bears the substantial costs of participant recruitment, retention, clinical care, and outcome ascertainment.

### Practical specimen collection

The hair collection procedure is practical and demonstrably acceptable. Collection of approximately 50–100 strands from the occipital scalp requires roughly 90 seconds, causes minimal discomfort, and does not require phlebotomy [1]. In the early SEARCH PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit, and a separate study collected samples from 837 participants with approximately 95% acceptability [1]. Specimen handling is well-suited to East African settings: samples are enclosed in foil with desiccant, stored at room temperature, and shipped in batches—no cold chain is required [1]. Staff will receive demonstrations for different hairstyles [1], and the proposal describes monthly data-quality review with retraining when necessary [1].

### Efficient laboratory strategy

Rather than assaying all collected specimens, the design selects specimens after outcome ascertainment, concentrating assays on seroconverters, participants with renal decline, and a random sample of controls [1]. This outcome-enriched sampling, combined with sampling weights to recover population inference, maximizes the information yield per assay dollar [1]. The laboratory has validated assays for tenofovir, emtricitabine, and cabotegravir in hair [1] [3] [9] [7] and has demonstrated the ability to measure dapivirine spiked into hair [1]. The analytical instrumentation and extraction protocols are shared across analytes, further reducing development time.

### Investigator team

The investigator team is exceptionally well-qualified. Gandhi has led the hair antiretroviral measurement program for over a decade [3] [2] [8] [7]. Havlir directs the SEARCH platform; Baeten is a lead investigator in the MTN network; Castillo-Mancilla brings long-acting antiretroviral expertise; Benet and Gerona provide pharmacokinetic and analytical chemistry depth; and Bacchetti provides statistical leadership [1]. This team combines the specific technical expertise with the clinical trial leadership needed to coordinate across three independent platforms.

### Dependencies

The principal dependency risk is the timing of parent study completion and outcome ascertainment, since the laboratory selection strategy requires knowing which participants seroconverted or experienced renal decline before selecting specimens for assay. This is inherent to the ancillary design and is appropriately managed by the staged analysis plan, but the project's timeline is partially outside the investigators' direct control.

## Ethics and safety

**The added research procedure is minimally invasive and poses low risk to participants, with proportionate safeguards for safety, confidentiality, and community engagement.**

The sole research-specific intervention is small-volume occipital hair collection, typically requiring approximately 90 seconds and involving cutting approximately 50–100 strands close to the scalp [1]. This procedure is substantially less invasive than phlebotomy and has demonstrated high acceptability [1]. The physical risks are limited to minor accidental scalp injury, which the proposal acknowledges and manages through staff training demonstrations and monthly data-quality review with retraining when necessary [1].

### Exclusion criteria and consent

The proposal appropriately identifies and addresses the key ethical boundary: completely bald scalps cannot provide the planned specimen, and the proposal explicitly excludes substitution with axillary or pubic hair [1]. This is scientifically appropriate given differences in drug incorporation and growth patterns across body sites, and ethically sound because it avoids pressuring participants to provide alternative specimens from more sensitive areas.

Each parent study has its own ethics approvals and monitoring boards. SEARCH and HOPE approvals are in place, while A5359 arrangements are being established [1]. Consent processes include hair collection, and SEARCH permits ages 15–17 only with parental co-consent [1]. The proposal describes standard procedures for reporting adverse events and confidentiality breaches through study leadership [1], and trial-network laboratory data systems will support specimen quality tracking [1]. These arrangements are proportionate to the minimal risk level.

### Data confidentiality and community obligations

The combination of hair specimens with the rich covariate data collected in the parent studies—including HIV status, sexual behavior, substance use, and mental health measures [1]—creates a detailed and potentially sensitive dataset. The proposal addresses this through standard confidentiality protections and a commitment to making blinded hair-concentration datasets publicly available after planned analyses [1]. The plan to share findings with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders [1] reflects appropriate recognition that research conducted in East and Southern African populations carries obligations of reciprocal benefit and transparent communication.

### Documentation gaps

Two considerations merit mention as areas where documentation is limited, though neither constitutes a material ethical concern. First, the proposal does not describe a specific protocol for managing incidental scalp injury beyond the general adverse event reporting framework; given the very low probability and the existing clinical infrastructure, this is a minor documentation gap. Second, while acceptability data are strong [1], the proposal does not discuss cultural sensitivities around hair collection that may vary across the multiple countries and communities involved. The described training demonstrations for different hairstyles [1] suggest awareness of practical variation, but cultural considerations could be addressed more explicitly.

### Capacity building

The capacity-building component—laboratory work with African partner laboratories in the final project year [1]—represents a positive ethical feature, as it invests in local scientific infrastructure rather than extracting specimens for analysis elsewhere without building local capability.

## Management

**The management structure is well-suited to the multi-study ancillary design, with clear leadership assignment across aims, established investigator expertise, and specified coordination mechanisms.**

### Leadership and expertise

The leadership assignments align directly with the substance of each aim. Gandhi leads the overall program and the hair-measurement laboratory; Havlir and the SEARCH team support Aim 1; Baeten and MTN collaborators support Aim 2; and Castillo-Mancilla provides long-acting antiretroviral expertise for Aim 3 [1]. This allocation respects the existing governance of each parent study while centralizing the shared analytical core. Benet and Gerona provide pharmacokinetic and analytical-chemistry depth; Bacchetti provides statistical leadership, which is critical given the non-standard sampling designs requiring specialized expertise [1].

### Operational procedures

The proposal specifies concrete implementation procedures. Study coordinators will receive collection training, and data quality will be reviewed monthly [1]. Staff will receive demonstrations for different hairstyles [1]. These measures address the practical challenge of maintaining standardized specimen collection across multiple clinical sites in five countries, where staff turnover and varying participant hairstyles could introduce variability. The laboratory selection strategy requires coordination between clinical teams that ascertain outcomes and the laboratory that performs assays [1]. Trial-network laboratory data systems will support specimen quality tracking [1], providing infrastructure for managing specimen identity and chain of custody.

### Staggered timelines

The project spans three parent studies with different timelines: SEARCH follow-up extends approximately three years, HOPE ring use covers one year plus exit, and the A5359 regimen continues through week 76 [1]. This staggered schedule allows the laboratory to process specimens in waves rather than facing simultaneous peak demand, which is a practical advantage. The capacity-building component—laboratory work with African partner laboratories in the final project year [1]—is appropriately scheduled late in the project, when the analytical methods will be well-established.

### Coordination across platforms

The principal management consideration is coordination across three separate clinical networks, each with its own leadership, regulatory frameworks, and operational timelines. The proposal does not describe a formal cross-study coordination mechanism—such as a steering committee or dedicated project manager—beyond the implicit coordination provided by Gandhi's overall leadership and the shared laboratory core. While the experienced investigator team and the ancillary nature of the work mitigate this risk substantially, a lightweight coordination structure would strengthen the management plan. The documented track record of collaboration among these investigators—evidenced by their co-authorship on foundational publications [3] [2] [8]—provides reassurance that informal coordination mechanisms are already functional.
