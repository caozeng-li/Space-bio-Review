# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project investigates how the inflammatory cytokine IL6 disrupts the epigenetic machinery that maintains regulatory T cell (Treg) suppressive function in inflammatory bowel disease. It tests the specific hypothesis that IL6-activated JAK2 phosphorylates the histone methyltransferase EZH2 within its SET domain, impairing its activity and thereby weakening the FOXP3-EZH2-mediated repression of inflammatory gene networks [1]. The work is organized into three linked aims: mapping the FOXP3-EZH2 protein interaction domains and their functional requirement in vivo (Aim 1); identifying IL6-dependent EZH2 phosphorylation sites—particularly Y641—and connecting them to altered enzymatic activity and target-gene transcription (Aim 2); and testing whether interrupting IL6 signaling in Tregs, either through receptor deletion or through EZH2 constructs resistant to phosphorylation, preserves their suppressive capacity in an established experimental colitis model (Aim 3) [1].

The proposal rests on a strong foundation of preliminary evidence. Conditional loss of the EZH2 SET domain under control of the FOXP3 promoter produces a severe inflammatory phenotype: approximately 80% of FOXP3ΔEZH2 animals died by 35 days in a study involving 107 mice, and survivors examined at eight weeks were highly susceptible to a normally subclinical 1% DSS challenge, assessed with blinded histology in 16 mice [1]. Mutant Tregs retained surface markers but produced less IL10, more IFNγ and IL17, and suppressed responder-cell proliferation poorly [1]. Proximity ligation places EZH2 and FOXP3 in close nuclear proximity in wild-type Tregs [1]. IL6 reduces EZH2-associated H3K27 methyltransferase activity in a JAK2-dependent manner, and a Y641E substitution shows reduced methyltransferase activity in an EZH2-deficient cell system [1]. Human CD4-cell expression profiles from active Crohn's disease resections and healthy ileal biopsies—involving 12 age- and sex-matched subjects—show altered IL6-related networks and EZH2 targets, with 379 of 457 differentially expressed EZH2 target genes upregulated in disease [1].

The experimental design incorporates careful controls, including receptor-specific IL6Rα deletion, inducible Cre models to separate mature Treg function from developmental effects, and CRISPR/Cas9-generated EZH2-deficient HAP-1 cells for clean mutant comparison [1]. The PI's laboratory demonstrates proficiency with the key techniques, and institutional infrastructure—flow cytometry, cell sorting, confocal imaging, sequencing and proteomics cores, and mouse genetics facilities—is well-matched to the proposed work [1].

The central limitation that materially affects the judgement is the biochemical ambiguity of the Y641F construct. Published structural and enzymatic studies identify Y641F not as a simple loss-of-function control but as a gain-of-function lymphoma-associated mutant with altered substrate preference for converting H3K27me1/me2 to H3K27me3, complicating the interpretation of comparisons between Y641E and Y641F as clean tests of phosphorylation status [2] [3]. This ambiguity, together with the absence of an in vitro JAK2-EZH2 kinase assay to establish direct phosphorylation, tempers confidence in the specific mechanistic inferences drawn from Aims 2 and 3, though the broader research program retains substantial scientific value.

The most valuable expected knowledge is a mechanistic account of how a specific inflammatory pathway directly inactivates a core epigenetic enzyme in Tregs, and proof-of-concept evidence that a cell-intrinsic intervention can sustain Treg function during active intestinal inflammation, thereby informing future inflammation-resistant Treg cell therapies. The proposal's sound scientific foundations, disease-relevant question, and logical experimental framework support a judgement that the planned work is likely to deliver meaningful mechanistic knowledge despite a biochemical limitation that affects the interpretation of the central Y641F experiments. The core value proposition—establishing how IL6 modifies EZH2 and testing whether protecting EZH2 preserves Treg function—remains intact and important. The Y641F ambiguity and the absence of a direct kinase assay introduce uncertainty into the precise molecular conclusions of Aims 2 and 3, but do not negate the project's capacity to generate useful advance; the alternative pathways and additional phosphorylation sites provide credible routes to mechanistic insight if the primary Y641 hypothesis requires revision. Support is warranted for the full scope of the three aims, with the specific condition that the interpretation of Y641F-based experiments must explicitly account for the mutant's known gain-of-function substrate preference, and the team should incorporate a direct in vitro kinase assay with purified JAK2 and EZH2 substrate to strengthen the causal evidence for direct phosphorylation. The supportable scope is otherwise the complete proposed work; these conditions are resolvable within the existing experimental plan.

**Overall recommendation:** Conditional support.

## Scientific value

**B. The question addresses an important mechanistic gap between inflammatory signaling and epigenetic control of Treg function, and the knowledge attainable from each aim is clearly defined and worthwhile.**

The proposal asks how inflammatory signals—specifically IL6—disrupt the epigenetic machinery that maintains Treg suppressive function, and whether interrupting that disruption can preserve Treg activity during active intestinal inflammation [1]. The question sits at a genuine point of uncertainty. Prior work established that EZH2 is required for Treg identity maintenance after activation [4], and that FOXP3 recruits EZH2 to deposit H3K27me3 at target genes upon inflammatory stimulation [5]. However, neither study addressed how inflammatory cytokines directly modulate EZH2 enzymatic activity, nor tested whether protecting EZH2 from such modulation could sustain Treg function in an inflamed environment. The proposal's central hypothesis—that IL6-activated JAK2 phosphorylates EZH2 within its SET domain, impairing methyltransferase activity and weakening FOXP3-EZH2-mediated repression of inflammatory gene networks—is mechanistically specific, testable, and fills a gap that neither the EZH2-Treg literature nor the EZH2-phosphorylation literature has addressed in primary Tregs.

**Knowledge attainable from each aim.** Aim 1 will produce the first detailed domain-level map of the FOXP3-EZH2 protein interaction, distinguishing direct from indirect association through co-immunoprecipitation of FLAG-EZH2 and HIS-FOXP3 deletion constructs covering the FOXP3 zinc-finger and forkhead domains and EZH2 WD/SET-associated regions, proximity ligation, and depletion of candidate bridging partners such as EED or SUZ12 [1]. It will test whether this interaction is required for repression of the target genes RORA and IRF4—identified from overlapping FOXP3/EZH2 chromatin targets upregulated in Crohn's disease CD4-positive cells [1]—and for Treg suppressive function both in vitro and in a T-cell transfer colitis prevention model [1]. The inducible EZH2-deletion system, in which tamoxifen is administered after Treg transfer, permits separation of mature Treg function from developmental effects [1], strengthening the inference that observed phenotypes reflect ongoing Treg-intrinsic requirements.

Aim 2 addresses the signaling mechanism: it will identify IL6-dependent phosphorylation sites in the EZH2 SET domain using mass spectrometry and a developed phospho-Y641 antibody, test whether JAK2 activity is necessary and sufficient for these modifications through AZD1480 inhibition, siRNA, dominant-negative and constitutively active constructs, and connect phosphorylation status to methyltransferase activity and target-gene transcription using the EZH2-deficient HAP-1 reconstitution system and genomically integrated luciferase reporters in Jurkat cells [1]. Six candidate phosphorylation sites—including Y641—have been identified through motif prediction and mass spectrometry [1]. The preliminary evidence that IL6 reduces EZH2-associated H3K27 methyltransferase activity in a JAK2-dependent manner, and that a Y641E substitution shows reduced activity, provides a credible starting point [1].

Aim 3 translates these mechanistic findings into a disease-relevant setting by testing whether receptor-deficient or EZH2-modified Tregs can treat established colitis—a treatment model in which RAG-null recipients with four-week established colitis receive titrated doses of 100,000–300,000 modified Tregs [1]. This treatment setting is more translationally informative than the prevention models predominantly used in the field. The parallel approach of FOXP3-directed IL6Rα deletion and ex vivo TALEN editing of IL6Rα provides both a well-validated genetic system and a methodological exploration relevant to future cell therapy development [1].

**Value of alternative outcomes.** Negative results in any aim would still usefully constrain the mechanistic model. The proposal outlines explicit alternatives: indirect FOXP3-EZH2 association through EED or SUZ12, EZH2-associated long noncoding RNAs directing target specificity, EZH1 compensation for H3K27 methylation, and effector-cell resistance to suppression rather than intrinsic Treg defects [1]. These contingency paths demonstrate that the research program is structured to yield informative results regardless of whether the primary hypotheses are confirmed.

**Clinical motivation.** The proposal explicitly recognizes that IL6 also supports epithelial homeostasis, motivating the search for cell-intrinsic interventions that preserve Treg suppressive function while leaving IL6 responses in other tissues intact [1]. This selective-modification rationale strengthens the translational significance of the mechanistic aims.

## Innovation

**B. The proposal meaningfully extends prior work through specific, well-defined increments—a systematic domain-level mapping of the FOXP3-EZH2 interaction, a novel IL6-JAK2-EZH2 phosphorylation hypothesis targeting SET-domain residues not previously examined in primary Tregs, and a creative ex vivo editing approach—without overclaiming its originality.**

**Domain-level mapping of FOXP3-EZH2 interaction.** Although FOXP3 and EZH2 have been shown to associate functionally—Arvey et al. demonstrated that FOXP3 recruits EZH2 to deposit H3K27me3 at target genes upon inflammatory stimulation, and DuPage et al. established that EZH2 is required for Treg identity maintenance after activation [4] [5]—the physical interaction between the two proteins has never been systematically mapped at the domain level. Aim 1 addresses this gap directly. The proposal deploys FLAG-EZH2 and HIS-FOXP3 deletion constructs covering the FOXP3 zinc-finger and forkhead domains and the EZH2 WD/SET-associated regions, combined with co-immunoprecipitation and proximity ligation in both T cell lines and primary cells [1]. The explicit contingency plan—testing whether the interaction is indirect, mediated through Polycomb complex members EED or SUZ12, followed by functional knockdown—demonstrates that the experimental strategy is designed to produce a definitive answer rather than simply confirming expected association [1].

**The IL6-JAK2-EZH2 phosphorylation hypothesis.** This represents a mechanistically distinct departure from the established paradigm. Cha et al. showed that Akt phosphorylates EZH2 at serine 21 in the N-terminal region, suppressing methyltransferase activity by impeding EZH2 binding to histone H3 [6]. Kim et al. reported EZH2 phosphorylation linked to STAT3 activation in glioblastoma stem-like cells. Neither examined IL6-JAK2 signaling, nor investigated phosphorylation within the SET domain—the catalytic core responsible for substrate binding and methyl transfer. The proposal's focus on SET-domain residues, particularly Y641, which the structural model predicts participates in orienting the substrate lysine, represents a novel mechanistic hypothesis [1]. The systematic evaluation of six candidate phosphorylation sites, using phosphomimetic (S→D, Y→E) and phosphorylation-resistant (S→A, Y→F) substitutions in the EZH2-deficient HAP-1 system, provides a rigorous framework for attributing functional consequences to specific modification events [1]. The preliminary evidence that a JAK2 inhibitor blocks IL6-induced reduction of EZH2 methyltransferase activity more consistently than AKT or MAPK inhibitors further distinguishes this pathway from the Akt-S21 axis [1].

**Methodological innovation in ex vivo editing.** The TALEN editing of IL6Rα in primary wild-type Tregs (Aim 3) introduces a methodological exploration relevant to translational development [1]. Rather than relying solely on germline Foxp3-Cre-driven receptor deletion—a powerful genetic tool but not a therapeutic strategy—the proposal tests whether transient transfection, fluorescent selection, and expansion can produce receptor-deficient Tregs suitable for adoptive transfer. This explores a practical avenue toward cell-intrinsic Treg modification that avoids systemic IL6 blockade.

These increments are appropriately calibrated to the project's stage. The proposal does not claim to discover a new paradigm but resolves specific mechanistic questions left open by prior work and tests whether those insights can be harnessed for a functional outcome—preserving Treg suppression during active colitis—that the field has not yet demonstrated.

## Rigor

**C. The experimental design incorporates multiple rigorous controls—including an inducible deletion system, receptor-specific negative controls, and clean reconstitution backgrounds—but the central mechanistic inference is weakened by the biochemical ambiguity of the Y641F construct and the absence of a direct phosphorylation assay.**

**Design strengths.** Several features deserve particular recognition. The EZH2-deficient HAP-1 cell system, generated by CRISPR/Cas9, allows mutant EZH2 constructs to be compared without interference from endogenous wild-type enzyme [1]. The FOXP3-directed IL6Rα deletion provides a receptor-specific negative control that cleanly separates Treg-intrinsic IL6 signaling from paracrine effects [1]. IL6-receptor-deficient responder cells were used in preliminary experiments to exclude responder-cell IL6 signaling as the explanation for reduced Treg function [1]. The inducible EZH2-deletion model permits assessment of mature Treg function without confounding developmental defects [1]. The use of genomically integrated RORA and IRF4 luciferase reporters in Jurkat cells avoids the artefactual chromatin environment of episomal reporters [1]. Reconstitution of EZH2-deficient Tregs via a CAR-transgenic background addresses the low nucleofection efficiency (\~20%) in primary cells by enabling efficient adenoviral delivery (\>75% transduction) [1]. These features collectively reflect careful experimental design appropriate to the mechanistic questions.

**The Y641F problem.** The most significant limitation concerns the Y641 residue, which occupies a central position in the proposal's mechanistic logic. The proposal treats Y641E as a phosphomimetic with reduced activity and Y641F as a phosphorylation-resistant control predicted to maintain activity during IL6 exposure [1]. Published structural and enzymatic studies, however, establish that Y641 mutations in the EZH2 SET domain—including Y641F—are gain-of-function alterations found in B-cell lymphomas that alter substrate preference rather than simply abolishing or preserving wild-type activity.

Sneeringer et al. demonstrated that wild-type EZH2 shows greatest catalytic efficiency for the H3K27me0→me1 reaction, whereas Y641 mutant enzymes display very limited ability to perform the first methylation step but enhanced catalytic efficiency for converting H3K27me1→me2 and H3K27me2→me3 [2]. McCabe et al. confirmed this altered substrate preference and further showed that A677G—a mutation that enlarges the lysine tunnel—produces yet another distinct specificity pattern, highlighting the functional interplay between Y641 and neighboring residues in the substrate-binding pocket [3]. The proposal's own structural model predicts that Y641 participates in orienting the substrate lysine [1].

This body of work has direct implications for interpreting the Y641F rescue experiments in Aims 2 and 3. If Y641F maintains or even enhances certain aspects of methyltransferase activity for reasons related to its altered substrate preference—independent of whether it blocks phosphorylation—then differences observed between Y641E and Y641F in suppression assays or target-gene repression cannot be cleanly attributed to phosphorylation status alone. The proposal acknowledges the oncogenic associations of Y641F and mentions investigating long-term function and toxicity [1], but does not address how the known gain-of-function biochemistry will be distinguished from the proposed role as a phosphorylation-resistant control.

**Absence of a direct kinase assay.** The proposal uses AZD1480, JAK2 siRNA, dominant-negative JAK2, and constitutively active JAK2 to establish that JAK2 activity is necessary and sufficient for the observed phosphorylation events [1]. These experiments provide strong correlational evidence. However, an in vitro kinase assay with purified JAK2 kinase and recombinant EZH2 substrate—or the relevant SET domain—would distinguish direct phosphorylation from JAK2 activating an intermediate kinase, and would substantially strengthen the causal chain from IL6→JAK2→EZH2 phosphorylation. The mass spectrometry and phospho-specific antibody approaches are well-suited to identifying the modified residues [1], but without a direct biochemical demonstration, the specific claim that JAK2 directly phosphorylates EZH2 remains inferential.

**Statistical considerations and additional concerns.** The power calculation for the transfer-colitis experiments—four recipients per treatment group, repeated three times, providing 90% power to detect a 1.25 SD difference under one-way ANOVA—is adequately powered for large effects [1]. However, Aim 3 involves multiple concurrently tested Treg conditions: wild-type, IL6Rα-deficient, Y641E-reconstituted, Y641F-reconstituted, and TALEN-edited cells, potentially at titrated doses of 100,000–300,000 cells [1]. If all conditions are compared within the same experimental framework, effective power for the full set of comparisons may be lower than the nominal calculation suggests. The block design can address this, but the proposal does not specify whether the power calculation accounts for the complete set of planned comparisons.

The proposal also considers that IL6-dependent modification might change EZH2 substrates or protein partners rather than simply abolishing activity, and outlines complex isolation and partner/substrate analysis to examine this possibility [1]. The additional exploration of other inflammatory inputs—including TNF-family receptors, IL1 pathways, and protective IL10 signaling—is presented as contingent on the primary results. A further consideration is the IL6 concentration used for stimulation (10 ng/ml) [1]; the relationship between this experimental dose and concentrations reached in the inflamed intestinal microenvironment is not discussed, which may affect how confidently the in vitro phosphorylation findings can be extrapolated to the in vivo colitis setting.

## Feasibility

**B. The proposed work can be carried out with the stated methods, access, and resources; the PI has demonstrated proficiency in all essential technical systems, institutional infrastructure is well-matched, and the principal uncertainty—ex vivo TALEN editing of primary Tregs—has a credible parallel genetic approach that maintains overall feasibility.**

**Team capabilities and institutional resources.** The PI's laboratory has established expertise in the core techniques. The preliminary data demonstrate proficiency with conditional mouse models—both the FOXP3ΔEZH2 system and the inducible deletion model [1]. Adoptive transfer colitis is a working system: preliminary experiments show that conditional EZH2 deletion in transferred Tregs impairs protection of RAG-null recipients [1]. The biochemical toolkit—co-immunoprecipitation, proximity ligation in primary Tregs, chromatin immunoprecipitation, and adenoviral reconstitution—is documented in preliminary figures [1]. The mass spectrometry workflow and the phospho-Y641 antibody have been developed and applied to IL6-treated samples [1]. The laboratory's earlier work on KLF-dependent regulation of FOXP3 and TGFβ pathways, including KLF10 interactions with Sin3-HDAC and PCAF, provides transcriptional, chromatin, and experimental-colitis expertise [1]. These are operational methods producing the preliminary data, not aspirational capabilities.

Institutional resources are documented and well-matched [1]: flow cytometry and cell sorting, confocal imaging, sequencing and gene-expression cores, proteomics facilities, mouse genetics infrastructure, biochemical instrumentation, and tissue-culture facilities. The five-year timeline and estimated animal usage—approximately 2,139 mice over five years, with approximately 150 wild-type, 100 IL6R-mutant, and 100 EZH2-deficient donor animals per year for recurring biochemical work—are proportionate to the three aims [1]. The sequencing of aims—with domain mapping and mechanistic identification (Aims 1 and 2) providing reagents and interpretations for the in vivo rescue and treatment experiments (Aims 2 and 3)—is logical and creates internal dependencies that are appropriately ordered.

**TALEN editing uncertainty and fallback.** The principal feasibility uncertainty lies in the ex vivo TALEN editing of IL6Rα in primary wild-type Tregs [1]. The proposal describes transient transfection, fluorescent marker-based selection, verification of receptor loss by flow cytometry, and expansion using established Treg culture methods, to be performed with the institutional genetics core. However, the efficiency of biallelic editing in primary Tregs, the post-selection yield after transfection and expansion, and the functional consequences on Treg phenotype are not documented in preliminary data. Primary Tregs present challenges for efficient nucleic acid delivery—the proposal reports approximately 20% nucleofection efficiency, motivating the adenoviral approach with \>75% transduction [1]. The TALEN approach will need sufficient editing efficiency and cell recovery to permit functional testing in the colitis treatment model.

This uncertainty is mitigated by the well-validated parallel genetic approach. FOXP3-directed IL6Rα deletion provides receptor-deficient Tregs through a system already in hand, and these cells are explicitly planned for the same treatment experiments [1]. If TALEN editing proves inefficient or yields cells with altered function, the receptor-deleted Tregs provide a robust alternative that tests the same biological question. The TALEN experiments explore a translational methodology while the genetic model secures the core scientific aim. This redundancy strengthens the feasibility plan.

## Ethics and safety

**B. Animal welfare provisions are explicitly described and proportionate to the experimental procedures; the proposal acknowledges the specific safety concern raised by the Y641F construct and commits to investigating it; and human tissue use is limited to existing samples.**

**Animal welfare.** The animal experiments encompass approximately 2,139 mice over five years for donor cell preparation, adoptive transfer colitis models, and breeding of conditional and receptor-deficient lines [1]. All animal work is subject to Mayo Clinic IACUC review within an accredited centralized facility with veterinary oversight [1]. Monitoring provisions are concrete: study animals will be weighed and examined daily, with predefined humane endpoints set at loss of more than 20% of starting body weight or a moribund appearance. Analgesic support is provided after adoptive transfer, and approved euthanasia procedures are followed. These arrangements are standard for murine colitis models, where weight loss and clinical scoring are established disease severity indicators, and the 20% threshold is a conservative endpoint. The tamoxifen administration in the inducible deletion model is a short-term intervention (five days in the reported preliminary experiment) [1]. The DSS challenge experiment used blinded histological assessment, indicating awareness of observer bias in outcome scoring [1]. The daily monitoring frequency, combined with predefined objective endpoints, provides a proportionate framework for managing the anticipated morbidity.

**Human tissue and data.** The proposal draws on human CD4-positive cell RNA sequencing data from active Crohn's disease resections and healthy ileal biopsies, involving 12 age- and sex-matched subjects [1]. These data form part of the preliminary scientific basis and provide human disease context for target gene selection. No new human subjects research is proposed; the cell-treatment studies are conducted entirely in mice [1].

**Safety of modified constructs.** The proposal explicitly acknowledges that "Y641F has been associated with lymphoproliferative conditions in other settings" and commits to investigating "long-term function and toxicity" in rescue experiments using mice lacking functional Tregs [1]. This is appropriate given the published literature establishing Y641 mutations as gain-of-function oncogenic drivers through altered substrate preference [2] [3]. The proposal's recognition of this risk and commitment to studying adverse effects constitute a responsible approach.

**Operational safety and data sharing.** The institutional infrastructure—centralized animal facility, proteomics core, and genome-editing core—operates under standard biosafety and occupational health frameworks. No unusual environmental release, dual-use, or biosafety-level escalation is described. The resource-sharing plan makes generated cell and mouse lines available under institutional and NIH guidelines and provides for deposition of published results in PubMed Central [1]. Overall, the ethical and safety arrangements are clearly articulated, proportionate to the risks, and include specific attention to the most pertinent safety concern.

## Management

**B. The five-year project integrates the PI's established expertise with targeted collaborations in a logically sequenced plan; institutional resources are well-matched, and the experimental framework is coherently organized, though specific contingency timelines would strengthen the management plan.**

**Division of labour and scientific leadership.** The PI brings established expertise in intestinal immunology and Treg biology, as demonstrated by the published and preliminary work on KLF-dependent FOXP3 regulation, conditional EZH2 deletion models, and adoptive transfer colitis [1]. The three aims are sequenced so that the biochemical and mechanistic outputs of Aims 1 and 2—domain maps, phosphosite identification, and mutant EZH2 constructs—directly supply reagents and interpretive frameworks for the in vivo rescue and treatment experiments in Aims 2 and 3 [1]. This internal dependency is logically ordered, with the early aims producing defined molecular tools before the more resource-intensive colitis treatment experiments are undertaken.

**Collaborations and infrastructure.** Targeted collaborations fill specific technical gaps without fragmenting intellectual ownership. Structural modeling informing the Y641 hypothesis is a defined input to Aim 2 [1]. The mass spectrometry workflow and phospho-Y641 antibody are already in hand [1]. The TALEN editing in Aim 3 is a collaboration with the institutional genetics core, presented as a methodological exploration rather than an essential dependency [1]. Proteomics, sequencing, flow cytometry, cell sorting, confocal imaging, and mouse genetics are provided through institutional cores whose availability is documented [1]. This structure concentrates the PI's laboratory effort on the immunology, biochemistry, and in vivo modeling that represent its core competence.

**Resource planning and timeline.** The animal estimate and experimental-block design are explicitly specified: the total of approximately 2,139 mice over five years, with approximately 150 wild-type, 100 IL6R-mutant, and 100 EZH2-deficient donor animals per year, is proportionate to the described experiments [1]. Transfer-colitis experiments are organized as repeated blocks (four recipients per treatment group, three repetitions), providing both statistical motivation and logistical manageability. In vitro assays—co-immunoprecipitation, suppression assays, ChIP, and reporter experiments—are planned in triplicate. The five-year timeline accommodates the sequential logic: domain mapping and phosphosite identification occupy the earlier period, with treatment-model experiments building on those results. The resource-sharing plan commits to making generated cell and mouse lines available under institutional and NIH guidelines [1].

**Areas for management refinement.** The plan would benefit from two clarifications. First, the TALEN collaboration's timeline and decision points are not specified; given that editing efficiency in primary Tregs is not documented in preliminary data, a defined checkpoint for evaluating efficiency and cell yield before proceeding to in vivo treatment experiments would strengthen the management framework. Second, contingency timelines for the alternative pathways—particularly the EED/SUZ12 depletion experiments in Aim 1 and the EZH1 compensation studies in Aim 3—would clarify how the project adapts if primary hypotheses require revision. These are refinements rather than structural deficiencies; the proposal's clear articulation of alternative experimental paths already demonstrates awareness of the contingencies.
