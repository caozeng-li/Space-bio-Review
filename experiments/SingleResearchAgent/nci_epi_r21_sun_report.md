# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project develops and evaluates likelihood‑based statistical methods that jointly use gene expression and DNA methylation to estimate immune‑cell composition from bulk tumor samples, and that subsequently integrate those latent cell‑fraction estimates into survival prediction. The primary deliverables are open‑source R packages and TCGA‑based analyses across multiple cancer types, with an initial focus on colon and lung cancer [1]. The investigators — Wei Sun (Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center), who contributes statistical genomics and software expertise, and Michael Wu, who contributes methylation and association‑method expertise, supported by clinical collaborators in colorectal and lung cancer — apply under the R21 exploratory mechanism \[证据2, 47\].

The most valuable expected knowledge is a practical computational framework that can be adopted by epidemiologists and translational researchers to characterize tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) composition in large cohorts where bulk omics are already available, and a demonstration of how multi‑modal deconvolution can improve prognostic modeling. The longer‑term goal of creating TIME biomarkers for tumor subtyping or immunotherapy response prediction is honestly framed as aspirational rather than delivered within the project period [1].

The proposal is supported by a carefully elaborated statistical framework — log‑normal expression model, normal methylation model, coordinate‑ascent estimation, two‑component feature selection — and by identification of appropriate public reference datasets comprising over 800 expression samples covering 28 cell types and methylation data from six Illumina 450k studies [1]. Preliminary analyses provide encouraging feasibility signals: TCGA colon‑cancer analyses show associations between hypermutation status and composition estimates from separate modalities (Wilcoxon p‑values 0.046 for B‑cells and 0.005 for monocytes) [1]; a small methylation‑mixture experiment with six samples and five cell types gave a residual sum of squares of 0.029 for maximum‑likelihood estimation versus 0.043 for non‑negative least squares [1]. These preliminary results justify method development but are modest in scale and do not yet demonstrate that the full joint likelihood will outperform single‑modality approaches in realistic settings.

The proposal identifies a genuine methodological gap — joint deconvolution of bulk tumor expression and methylation with propagation of uncertainty to prognostic modeling — and proposes a statistically coherent and well‑specified plan to address it. Strengths include the carefully developed likelihood framework, the planned adaptive refinement of reference profiles, and the innovation of a joint survival model with compositional constraints and fused‑lasso regularization. Material limitations are the modest scale of the preliminary validation experiments, the still incomplete prognostic evaluation strategy (additional survival datasets are mentioned but not named, and the use of BIC for penalty tuning may be conservative), and the tight sequential timeline with strong dependency between aims. These limitations do not undermine the scientific value but temper confidence that the full two‑aim program will be completed and validated within the proposed period. On balance, the combination of a worthwhile scientific question, a credible methods plan, and appropriately managed risks makes the project supportable with the acknowledgement that the evaluation framework will require further detail during execution.

**Overall conclusion: Support**

## Scientific value

**A worthwhile methodological contribution that addresses a genuine gap with practical utility for epidemiological and translational research.**

The proposal tackles a well‑recognised limitation in current tumor immune microenvironment research. Bulk‑tissue deconvolution methods — which estimate immune‑cell composition from gene expression or DNA methylation data — are widely used in large cohort studies where single‑cell approaches remain impractical [2]. Contemporary practice, however, relies on single‑modality estimation (CIBERSORT, CIBERSORTx for expression; MethylCIBERSORT, EpiDISH for methylation [3]) and almost universally treats the resulting cell‑fraction estimates as observed values without error in downstream analyses such as survival regression. This two‑stage approach can bias association estimates and inflate apparent precision. A recent authoritative review explicitly identifies “integration of transcriptomic deconvolution with epigenetic data” and methods that “identify synergistic signals” from multiple data types as important future directions, noting that “approaches that integrate additional data types (such as DNA‑seq or epigenetic data) with RNA‑seq data to identify synergistic signals are likely an important avenue for further deconvolution method development” [4].

The project directly addresses this gap by developing a likelihood framework that jointly models gene expression and DNA methylation to estimate immune‑cell composition, and subsequently integrates those latent fraction estimates into survival prediction. The attainable knowledge is primarily methodological rather than a biological discovery: validated statistical methods, open‑source R packages, and benchmark analyses demonstrating how multi‑modal deconvolution with uncertainty propagation can improve prognostic modeling in TCGA and similar cohorts. This scoping is appropriate for an exploratory R21 mechanism and does not over‑promise clinical translation within the project period.

**Practical design features extend the value beyond the methodological literature.** The joint framework is designed to accept samples with only one omics modality once trained, accommodating the many existing cohorts that have RNA‑seq or methylation arrays but not both [1]. The coordinate‑ascent estimation allows tumor samples to refine blood‑derived reference profiles, addressing the concern that peripheral‑blood immune cells may differ transcriptionally from their tumor‑infiltrating counterparts [1]. The explicit modeling of multiplicative factors relating RNA‑derived to methylation‑derived fractions — capturing differences in cell size or transcriptional activity — provides a formal framework for a discrepancy that is widely acknowledged but rarely modeled [1]. All methods will be released as open‑source R packages with computationally intensive components in C or C++, ensuring accessibility for the epidemiological and translational research communities [1].

The contribution is incremental: individually, the statistical devices — log‑normal approximations, coordinate ascent, compositional regression, fused‑lasso regularization — are established. Their integration into a coherent deconvolution‑plus‑survival pipeline that combines two omics modalities and propagates estimation uncertainty is, however, a distinct advance over current two‑stage, single‑modality practice. If successful, the project would produce tools that enable more reliable TIME characterization in the large archival cohorts where bulk omics remain the predominant data type, and would demonstrate principles — joint modeling, uncertainty propagation, adaptive reference refinement — that can inform subsequent method development.

## Innovation

**A coherent integration of three distinct methodological advances that together fill a recognised gap, though each element individually represents an incremental refinement rather than a transformative departure.**

The proposal makes its primary contribution by combining several statistical strategies into a unified framework that no current method provides. The joint expression–methylation likelihood is the most distinctive element. Existing deconvolution tools operate on a single molecular modality — CIBERSORT and CIBERSORTx use expression [2], while MethylCIBERSORT and EpiDISH use methylation [3] — and treat the two data types as separate, parallel analyses. The proposal instead models expression and methylation simultaneously in a single likelihood [1], exploiting the complementary properties of the two modalities: methylation beta‑values are independent of transcriptional activity and cell size, while expression captures functional states. This allows the method to address a known interpretive problem, namely that RNA proportions need not equal cell‑number proportions because cell size and transcriptional output vary across cell types [1]. The explicit parameterization of multiplicative cell‑size or transcriptional‑activity factors that reconcile RNA‑derived and DNA‑derived fraction estimates is a concrete, testable formalization of a discrepancy that the field acknowledges but typically sets aside [1].

The second contribution is the adaptive refinement of reference profiles. Standard reference‑based deconvolution assumes that purified‑cell profiles measured from peripheral blood or sorted populations adequately represent the same cell types in the tumor microenvironment. The proposal relaxes this assumption through a coordinate‑ascent algorithm that initializes parameters from purified‑cell data, then alternates between estimating cell fractions and re‑estimating cell‑type‑specific molecular profiles using the tumor samples themselves [1]. This allows the large number of tumor samples to adjust blood‑derived references toward their tumor‑context counterparts. The approach is practical because it does not require new experimental reference data for every tissue context, yet it addresses the concern that tumor‑infiltrating immune cells may differ transcriptionally and epigenetically from circulating counterparts.

The third contribution lies in the downstream survival model. The conventional two‑stage approach — estimate fractions, then plug them into a Cox or parametric survival regression — ignores deconvolution uncertainty and can produce biased coefficient estimates and overconfident standard errors. The proposal instead treats immune‑cell fractions as latent variables shared by the omics and survival components of a joint likelihood [1], so that survival estimation properly accounts for uncertainty in composition. The use of a compositional‑data parameterization with log fractions and a sum‑to‑zero constraint respects the mathematical structure of proportions [1], and the fused‑lasso penalty that shrinks adjacent, molecularly similar cell types toward common survival effects is a sensible regularization strategy that addresses the practical difficulty of distinguishing closely related immune subsets in bulk data [1].

These innovations are appropriately scoped for an exploratory R21 project. None individually constitutes a fundamental theoretical breakthrough: the log‑normal and normal distributional assumptions, coordinate‑ascent estimation, compositional regression, and fused‑lasso penalties are established statistical devices. A recent review identifies multi‑modal integration as an important future direction [4], confirming that the gap is recognised but not yet filled by available tools. The proposal’s contribution is therefore to assemble these elements into a working pipeline that answers a specific, unmet need — joint expression–methylation deconvolution with uncertainty propagation to survival outcomes — and to implement it in accessible open‑source software.

## Rigor

**The statistical framework is carefully specified and the validation strategy is appropriate for an exploratory methods project, though the preliminary evidence remains modest and the prognostic evaluation design is only partially elaborated.**

**Aim 1 likelihood construction.** The likelihood combines four information sources — tumor expression, tumor methylation, purified‑cell expression, and purified‑cell methylation — so that both tumor and reference data inform the latent cell fractions and cell‑type‑specific parameters simultaneously \[证据26–27\]. The distributional choices are pragmatic: log‑normal for expression, normal for methylation beta‑values at each CpG [1]. The investigators have conducted preliminary checks supporting their adequacy. CpG‑level distributions within cell groups were reasonably approximated by normal distributions, simulated mixtures of five log‑normal cell‑type distributions yielded more accurate fraction estimates with a log‑normal generalized linear model than with normal linear regression, and a small methylation‑mixture experiment with six samples and five cell types gave a residual sum of squares of 0.029 for maximum‑likelihood estimation versus 0.043 for non‑negative least squares \[证据13–14\]. These results are encouraging for the chosen approximations but come from limited settings — six samples, five broad cell‑type categories, a single mixture experiment — and therefore support feasibility without yet demonstrating that the full joint likelihood will outperform single‑modality methods in realistic, higher‑resolution deconvolution tasks.

The coordinate‑ascent estimation procedure is clearly described. Reference‑cell parameters are initialized from purified‑cell data and tumor‑cell parameters from high‑purity samples, after which the algorithm alternates between estimating cell fractions conditional on molecular profiles and re‑estimating those profiles conditional on fractions until convergence [1]. This structure allows the many tumor samples to refine blood‑derived references. The identifiability condition for the multiplicative cell‑size factors — that the number of tumor samples exceeds the number of immune cell types — is stated explicitly [1] and is likely to be satisfied for TCGA‑scale datasets. However, the biological interpretation of these factors, and the extent to which they will separate genuine cell‑size or transcriptional‑activity differences from other sources of modality discrepancy (reference‑panel composition, platform effects, differential probe coverage), will require careful empirical investigation that the proposal acknowledges but does not fully specify.

**Feature selection and reference harmonization.** The two‑component feature‑selection model, which distinguishes informative genes or CpGs from noise features that do not follow the deconvolution model, is a sensible robustness measure [1]. The preliminary experiment with perturbed probes (10 % of probes perturbed, yielding RSS of 0.055 for the mixture‑based method versus 0.155 for a naive approach) provides an initial proof of concept [1], but the validation is drawn from a single small experiment and requires extension to the scale and complexity of the full method. The reference‑data harmonization strategy — cell‑type‑specific quantile normalization, with preliminary principal‑component analyses supporting removal of major study effects while retaining cellular differences — is appropriately described [1].

**Validation strategy.** Validation plans are multi‑pronged and appropriate: simulated bulk tissues from weighted mixtures of measured cell‑specific profiles, comparison against existing expression‑ and methylation‑based deconvolution methods, and benchmarking against experimental datasets with flow‑cytometry‑established composition [1]. The plan to compare estimated tumor‑context expression profiles against published measurements of immune cells isolated from TIME [1] is a particularly valuable external check.

**Aim 2 evaluation.** For the prognostic aim, the primary evaluation metric is cross‑validated C‑index, comparing the joint model against separate expression‑only and methylation‑only models and against the conventional two‑stage approach [1]. This prediction‑oriented evaluation is well‑matched to the prognostic goal. The proposal honestly acknowledges that TCGA survival data may be heavily censored and not cancer‑specific, and states that other datasets will be explored [1], but does not name those datasets, specify event‑count requirements, or discuss how censoring patterns might affect model comparison. Additionally, the use of BIC for selecting the fused‑lasso tuning parameter [1] is a practical choice, but BIC is known to be conservative in high‑dimensional survival settings and may lead to over‑shrinkage that masks genuine cell‑type‑specific prognostic effects. These are limitations in evaluation design — not fatal, but they reduce confidence that the prognostic comparisons will be fully informative within the project period.

## Feasibility

**A credible two‑year plan supported by appropriate expertise, identified data, and computational resources; the main risk is the strong sequential dependency between aims without a documented contingency provision.**

The proposal outlines a two‑year schedule with Aim 1 (deconvolution method development) planned for year 1 and Aim 2 (joint survival modeling) for year 2 [1]. This sequential structure is logical given that the survival model depends on the latent‑fraction estimates from the deconvolution framework, but it creates a critical path where any delay in Aim 1 directly compresses Aim 2. The proposal does not discuss this dependency or specify fallback strategies — for example, whether preliminary single‑modality estimates could seed Aim 2 development in parallel, or whether the scope of Aim 2 could be adjusted if the joint deconvolution requires more development time than anticipated. For an exploratory R21 project, this tight coupling is a material feasibility concern that warrants acknowledgment, though it does not render the plan implausible.

**Investigator expertise.** The team is well‑matched to the required tasks. Wei Sun contributes statistical genomics expertise, experience with copy‑number and expression data, and a track record of software development. Michael Wu brings specific knowledge of methylation analysis and association methods. The clinical collaborators — Ulrike Peters in colorectal cancer and McGarry Houghton in lung cancer — provide domain context and access to disease‑specific interpretation, though their role is advisory rather than data‑generating [1]. The small team size (two primary methodologists plus clinical collaborators) is appropriate for a computational methods project and avoids unnecessary coordination overhead.

**Data and computational resources.** The data resources are already identified and publicly available. Reference expression data comprise more than 800 samples covering 28 cell types from published datasets; methylation references combine Illumina 450k data from six studies using control samples from case–control datasets [1]. Cell‑type‑specific quantile normalization is planned to harmonize datasets, and preliminary principal‑component analyses support the feasibility of removing major study effects while retaining cellular differences [1]. These reference resources are substantial and appropriate for initial method development. The primary application data — TCGA expression, methylation, and survival data across multiple cancer types — are also publicly accessible and well‑characterized. The plan to begin with colon and lung cancer and extend across cancer types [1] provides a staged approach that limits initial complexity while retaining a path to broader evaluation.

Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center provides the statistical and computing infrastructure for TCGA‑scale analysis [1]. The plan to implement computationally intensive components in C or C++ with Rcpp integration [1] is a pragmatic approach that should keep the software practical for users working with large omics datasets.

**Timeline assessment.** The two‑year timeline is ambitious but not unrealistic for a methods development project of this scope. Aim 1 encompasses model specification, algorithm implementation, simulation studies, and comparison against existing methods; Aim 2 adds the joint survival likelihood, the fused‑lasso regularization, and cross‑validated prognostic evaluation across cancer types. The preliminary work — including initial TCGA colon‑cancer analyses, the methylation mixture experiment, and the distributional checks \[证据12–14\] — provides a starting point that reduces the cold‑start burden, though the gap between these preliminary analyses and a fully validated joint deconvolution pipeline remains substantial. On balance, the feasibility is good: the team has the right expertise, the data are identified and accessible, the computational environment is suitable, and the methods plan is specified in sufficient detail to begin execution. The sequential dependency and tight timeline are the principal risks, and they are proportionate to the exploratory scope of an R21 mechanism.

## Ethics and safety

**The project uses exclusively existing public, de‑identified data with no participant contact, and the proposed data‑handling arrangements are appropriate; no material ethical or safety concerns arise.**

The study relies entirely on secondary analysis of previously collected, publicly available omics and clinical data. The named sources — TCGA, ENCODE, and published immune‑cell reference datasets — are all existing resources generated under their own institutional and consent frameworks [1]. No new participant recruitment, biospecimen collection, or direct contact with individuals is proposed.

The investigators have obtained an Exemption 4 determination under the applicable human‑subjects regulations [1]. The proposal confirms that the data to be used — gene‑expression matrices, DNA methylation beta‑value tables, and limited clinical covariates such as survival time and censoring indicators — do not contain direct identifiers, and the investigators commit to not seeking identifying information. These arrangements are consistent with the exemption and with current best practice for computational biomedical research using public genomic data.

Data storage is addressed at an appropriate level. The proposal states that data will be stored on designated secured machines accessible only to project personnel [1]. No cloud‑storage arrangements, institutional data‑use agreements, or data‑transfer protocols are detailed, but for a computational project operating on public de‑identified data within the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center environment, this description is proportionate.

Two peripheral considerations merit brief mention. First, the derivative composition estimates and cell‑type‑specific molecular profiles could, in principle, be linked back to the original participants if combined with additional identifying information. The investigators’ commitment to not seek identifying information and to operate within the existing TCGA data‑access framework adequately addresses this residual re‑identification risk, which is already low for bulk‑tissue summary measures aggregated across thousands of individuals. Second, the prognostic signatures developed under Aim 2 are explicitly described as exploratory and are not intended for clinical decision‑making within the project period [1]. The proposal does not overstate the readiness of its outputs for direct patient application, which is appropriate for an R21 methods project.

No environmental, laboratory, animal, or biosafety considerations apply. The work is entirely computational. In summary, the project’s data sources, exemption determination, access restrictions, and computational character together make it ethically unproblematic.

## Management

**The small investigator team is well‑matched to the required tasks and the sequential schedule mirrors the project’s logical structure, though the reviewed material omits some administrative detail that limits confidence in contingency planning.**

The core team consists of two primary methodologists — Wei Sun (statistical genomics, copy‑number and expression analysis, software development) and Michael Wu (methylation analysis and association methods) — supported by clinical collaborators Ulrike Peters (colorectal cancer) and McGarry Houghton (lung cancer) who provide domain context [1]. This division of expertise is well‑aligned with the work: Sun’s experience with expression deconvolution and software engineering covers the Aim 1 model implementation, Wu’s methylation expertise supports the integration of the 450k reference data and the two‑component feature‑selection model, and the clinical collaborators supply disease‑specific interpretation for the TCGA analyses rather than generating new data.

The two‑year schedule assigns Aim 1 to year 1 and Aim 2 to year 2 [1], reflecting the genuine dependency of the survival model on the deconvolution framework’s latent‑fraction estimates. However, the proposal does not specify how the methodological development labor will be divided between Sun and Wu — whether, for instance, Sun leads the expression component and Wu the methylation component of the joint likelihood, or whether both investigators work across modalities. For a two‑person methods team, this division is likely to be resolved pragmatically during execution, but the absence of explicit role delineation slightly reduces confidence that coordination bottlenecks have been anticipated.

The open‑source software delivery target is appropriately specified: all methods will be implemented as R packages, with computationally intensive components written in C or C++ and integrated via Rcpp [1]. This is a realistic development plan that balances accessibility for the intended user community against the computational demands of likelihood optimization across thousands of genes and CpGs. The plan to publish methods, analyses, and software provides a concrete dissemination pathway.

The principal management limitation is the absence of a documented contingency provision for the strong dependency between aims, as discussed under Feasibility. The proposal does not discuss fallback strategies, such as parallel development of the survival‑model structure using single‑modality estimates as placeholders, or scope adjustments that could preserve the methodological contribution even if the full multi‑cancer evaluation is curtailed. This lack of contingency planning is a common feature of tightly scoped R21 proposals and does not itself indicate inadequate management capacity, but it does represent a risk that the investigator team will need to manage actively during execution.

The reviewed material does not include CVs, an itemized budget, or a formal project‑management plan. For a computational methods project relying on public data and institutional computing resources, these omissions do not constitute evidence of incapacity — the expertise is described in the narrative, the data are already identified and accessible, and the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Research Center computing environment supports TCGA‑scale analysis [1]. However, the absence of budget detail means that reviewer confidence in the adequacy of protected staff time or computational allocation must rest on institutional context rather than documented provision, which is reflected in the moderate confidence in this management judgement.
