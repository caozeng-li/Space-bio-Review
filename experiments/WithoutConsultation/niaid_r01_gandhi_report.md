# Project review

## Overall assessment

This project seeks to establish antiretroviral drug concentrations in scalp hair as a universal, non-invasive biomarker of cumulative drug exposure and adherence across three major HIV intervention modalities. Leveraging existing clinical trial platforms, it aims to correlate hair levels of oral tenofovir (from the SEARCH study), dapivirine (from the HOPE vaginal ring study), and long-acting injectable cabotegravir (from the ACTG A5359 treatment study) with plasma drug concentrations, self-reported use, and critical clinical outcomes including HIV seroconversion and renal function. The most valuable expected contributions are the first prospective validation of hair tenofovir levels against incident HIV infection in a real-world PrEP setting and, notably, the entirely novel exploration of hair as a monitoring tool for long-acting products, which urgently require objective adherence measures that reflect exposure over weeks to months.

The application is strengthened by the investigative team’s unparalleled track record in hair pharmacology and their leadership within the leveraged parent studies. The oral tenofovir (Aim 1) work is a mature, confirmatory extension built on a robust dose-response calibration and a demonstrated high-acceptance collection infrastructure. However, the core limitations critically affect the project’s innovation and feasibility. The scientific premise of measuring dapivirine in hair for a locally-acting vaginal ring is unproven and the corresponding hair assay is unvalidated; the critical exposure threshold for cabotegravir is an unsupported extrapolation. Furthermore, the feasibility of the cabotegravir work is in question, as the completed parent trial’s publications do not mention the requisite hair collection. These issues mean Aims 2 and 3 are high-risk assay and methods development projects rather than straightforward applications of a validated platform, and the “integrated approach” is more an organizing concept than a demonstrated synergy.

Weighing the evidence, the proposal’s transformative potential for long-acting products is constrained by the developmental nature of Aims 2 and 3 and unresolved questions about specimen availability. The uncertainty in measurement validation for dapivirine and the integration risk for cabotegravir create a material gap between the proposed framework and its feasible demonstration. While the sum of the work would advance the field, the immediate inferential strength of the report is limited by these gaps. The project is a worthwhile investment at the methods-development stage, but its current plan contains substantive problems that prevent strong support without clarification or modification.

**Overall conclusion: Conditional support**

## Scientific value

The project addresses a question of substantial practical importance for HIV prevention and treatment: whether antiretroviral concentrations in scalp hair can serve as a non-invasive, cumulative exposure biomarker across three major delivery modalities—oral, vaginal ring, and injectable. The attainable knowledge spans descriptive exposure characterization, pharmacokinetic validation, and outcome prediction.

**Aim 1: oral tenofovir PrEP.** For oral tenofovir-based PrEP, the project would provide the first prospective validation of hair tenofovir levels against incident HIV seroconversion in a real-world delivery setting. This builds on published dose-response calibration that established a log-linear relationship between directly observed dosing and hair concentrations (76% increase per twofold dose increase) [1], and on prior work associating hair antiretroviral levels with virologic suppression in treatment [2] [3] [4]. Demonstrating that hair TFV/FTC can discriminate seroconversion risk in the SEARCH community-based PrEP cohort—where 25 incident infections were observed at 0.35 per 100 person-years over 7,150 person-years of follow-up [5]—would advance hair monitoring from a research adherence measure to a clinically interpretable prognostic tool. The concurrent renal toxicity analysis, though exploratory, would supply exposure-outcome data for renal safety monitoring in African populations where such evidence remains scarce [6].

**Aims 2 and 3: long-acting products.** For the long-acting modalities, the value is both more novel and more contingent. No published systematic study has evaluated hair dapivirine from the vaginal ring or hair cabotegravir from injectable administration at the time of this proposal. The 2016 Stalter review explicitly identified drug levels in hair as a high-priority approach for vaginal ring adherence monitoring, while noting that research on systemic absorption of vaginally delivered antiretrovirals and subsequent hair incorporation “is needed” [7]. If successful, Aim 2 would fill this gap by characterizing whether dapivirine released locally from the ring reaches systemic circulation in measurable quantities and how hair levels relate to plasma, residual ring drug, and self-report. Aim 3 would extend the hair matrix to a depot injectable with fundamentally different pharmacokinetics, using segmental analysis to examine week-to-week exposure changes between monthly injections [6]. This would address a recognized limitation of short-term measures such as plasma, which poorly characterize exposure for long-acting products where concentrations change slowly and dosing intervals span weeks to months.

**Developmental constraints.** The primary limitation on scientific value is that Aims 2 and 3 represent assay and methods development as much as methods application. For dapivirine, the vaginal ring produces cervicovaginal tissue concentrations approximately 1,000-fold higher than plasma [8]; whether the small systemic fraction incorporates into hair at detectable or interpretable levels is an unresolved preliminary question. No validated lower limit of quantification from clinical samples has been demonstrated at this stage. The hair-to-plasma correspondence for cabotegravir—the proposed 66 ng/mg threshold corresponding to 4× PA‑IC₉₀—is an extrapolation unsupported by published evidence and appears inconsistent with the single available clinical data point (0.190 ng/mg at a plasma concentration of 3.37 µg/mL, approximately 20× PA‑IC₉₀) [9].

These gaps do not negate the importance of the questions, but they frame the attainable knowledge from Aims 2 and 3 more as feasibility demonstration and initial characterization than as fully validated monitoring tools. The “integrated monitoring approach” described in the proposal is an organizing concept rather than a demonstrated synergy: the three aims are operationally independent validation studies that share a conceptual framework and specimen platform but do not produce a unified data product.

Weighing the confirmatory strength of Aim 1 against the developmental nature and unresolved validation questions of Aims 2 and 3, the project can reasonably deliver a well-powered demonstration that hair TFV/FTC predicts HIV seroconversion in a real-world PrEP cohort, exploratory exposure-outcome data for renal safety, and initial evidence on whether hair monitoring is feasible for long-acting products—a contribution that would inform subsequent validation studies and adherence intervention design. The overall importance is high, but the immediate inferential strength in the most novel domains is constrained.

## Innovation

The proposal derives considerable innovation from its systematic extension of hair pharmacology beyond the well-characterized domain of oral antiretrovirals.

**Aim 1: confirmatory extension with methodological refinement.** Hair tenofovir measurement has an established evidence base: a validated log-linear dose-response relationship [1], published correlations with virologic suppression across multiple treatment cohorts [2] [3] [4], and demonstrated feasibility in African populations through SEARCH itself [5] [10]. The directly observed dosing study established that 2, 4, and 7 doses per week produced median hair tenofovir concentrations of 0.012, 0.023, and 0.038 ng/mg respectively [1]. Aim 1 therefore represents a confirmatory extension—the first direct, prospective linkage of hair TFV/FTC to HIV seroconversion in a real-world PrEP delivery setting—rather than a fundamentally new measurement concept. The innovation here lies in the outcome-enriched case-cohort design with inverse-probability weighting, which efficiently leverages approximately 5,200 person-visits from 100 expected seroconverters and 300 controls to estimate population-level hazard ratios that would otherwise require assaying over 83,000 specimens [6].

**Aim 2: first application of hair pharmacology to a locally-acting vaginal product.** Hair dapivirine measurement from a vaginal ring represents an unexplored application. The Stalter et al. (2016) landscape review of ring adherence biomarkers identified drug levels in hair as a high-priority approach but explicitly noted that research on systemic absorption of vaginally delivered antiretrovirals and subsequent hair incorporation “is needed” [7]. The proposal would provide the first systematic evaluation of whether a predominantly locally-acting drug, achieving cervicovaginal tissue concentrations roughly 1,000-fold higher than plasma [8], incorporates into hair at measurable levels. This extends the conceptual scope of hair pharmacology from systemic oral dosing to mucosally-delivered products—a transition whose feasibility depends on whether the small systemic dapivirine fraction (geometric mean plasma concentrations of approximately 220–280 pg/mL from the 25 mg monthly ring [11]) produces a quantifiable hair signal.

**Aim 3: segmental analysis for an injectable depot.** Aim 3 applies segmental hair analysis to an injectable depot formulation for the first time. Prior segmental work examined daily oral maraviroc using mass spectrometry imaging [12]; extending this principle to monthly intramuscular cabotegravir involves fundamentally different pharmacokinetics—a depot release profile rather than daily oral absorption—and finer temporal sampling with 0.25 cm segments to approximate weekly exposure [6]. Only a single case report of hair cabotegravir measurement exists (0.190 ng/mg from one individual [9]), and no systematic study of the hair-to-plasma relationship after injectable administration has been published. A validated LC–MS/MS assay for cabotegravir in hair does exist with an LLOQ of 0.00625 ng/mg [9], providing a technical foundation for this work.

**Cross-platform validation.** Beyond the individual aims, applying a common collection protocol, analytical chemistry platform, and conceptual framework—pairing long-term hair measures with short-term plasma measures—across oral, vaginal ring, and injectable delivery establishes hair as a potentially universal antiretroviral exposure matrix. This methodological generalization has substantial conceptual value even though the three aims remain operationally independent studies rather than an integrated data product.

The innovation is tempered by the high-risk developmental status of Aims 2 and 3: the dapivirine hair assay lacks published validation parameters, and the cabotegravir exposure threshold of 66 ng/mg lacks empirical support [6]. These are innovations in measurement concept and application rather than in proven tools, and the project’s originality must be assessed at the methods-development stage that corresponds to this evidence base.

## Rigor

The proposal’s inferential strength varies sharply across aims. Aim 1 is the most mature component, resting on a validated assay, an established dose-response calibration, and a methodologically appropriate case-cohort design with inverse-probability weighting. Aims 2 and 3 are at a substantially earlier methods-development stage, and specific measurement choices place meaningful limits on the inferences the study can currently support in its most novel domains.

### Aim 1 design and analysis

**The case-cohort design is appropriate and efficient.** The outcome-enriched case-cohort sampling plan assays all seroconverter visits and three randomly sampled controls per case, yielding approximately 5,200 person-visits from an expected 100 cases and 300 controls, with inverse-probability-of-sampling weights recovering population-level hazard ratios in weighted pooled logistic regression [6]. This is a well-established two-phase design that correctly avoids treating the assayed subsample as a simple random cohort. The same assayed data support both HIV outcome and renal analyses through appropriate weighting. The discrete-time Cox approach implemented through pooled logistic regression accommodates time-varying repeated hair measures across up to 13 visits and allows comparison of hair, plasma, and self-report individually and together [6]. The directly observed dosing study anchors interpretation, having established median hair TFV values of 0.012, 0.023, and 0.038 ng/mg at 2, 4, and 7 doses per week respectively [1].

**Power and renal analysis constraints.** Two inferential constraints warrant attention. First, the power calculation is a simplified single-observation estimate that does not incorporate the repeated-measures structure; the stated 80% power for an odds ratio of at least 2.1 comparing the lowest hair-exposure quartile with the upper three (2.6% versus 1.2% cumulative seroconversion) targets a large effect [6]. For more nuanced analyses—the independent contribution of hair beyond plasma, or the renal dose-response relationship—power is uncertain. The renal analysis, with up to 100 additional participants with substantial renal decline, is likely underpowered to detect a dose-response relationship between hair TFV and decline after adjusting for age, sex, baseline renal function, hypertension, diabetes, concomitant drugs, and site [6]. Data from the US PrEP Demo suggest that only 5.1% developed new-onset eGFR below 70 mL/min/1.73 m², largely driven by age ≥40 and baseline eGFR below 90 [13]; the younger SEARCH population may have even lower event rates. The renal analysis is therefore better framed as exploratory and hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory.

**Adherence pattern resolution.** The proposal’s capacity to distinguish sustained from intermittent use patterns using combined hair and plasma measures is conceptually well-grounded—the pharmacology distinguishing recent dosing (plasma) from cumulative exposure (hair) is established [6]—but the planned analyses of scatterplots, correlations, and longitudinal mixed models remain descriptive rather than a pre-specified classification algorithm. The 12-week visit spacing limits the temporal resolution with which between-visit patterns can be resolved. Hair sampled at each visit reflects the preceding 4–8 weeks, so intermittent use between visits could produce intermediate levels that are difficult to classify definitively. The three-way comparison of hair, plasma, and self-report is a strength: when all three agree, classification confidence is high; disagreement patterns are themselves informative about the limitations of each measure.

### Measurement validation in Aims 2 and 3

**Cabotegravir: unvalidated protective threshold.** For cabotegravir, the proposed interpretive threshold of 66 ng/mg in hair corresponding to 4× PA‑IC₉₀ (0.66 µg/mL in plasma) is presented without derivation [6]. The single published clinical data point—hair 0.190 ng/mg at plasma 3.37 µg/mL (approximately 20× PA‑IC₉₀) [9]—implies a hair-to-plasma ratio roughly 1,800-fold lower than the proposal’s estimate. While the validated LC–MS/MS assay with an LLOQ of 0.00625 ng/mg [9] ensures that even low hair concentrations can be measured, the threshold linking hair concentration to protective plasma exposure remains uncalibrated. Aim 3 is therefore a threshold-development exercise rather than a threshold-testing one.

**Dapivirine: a fundamental measurement premise.** The dapivirine measurement in Aim 2 raises a more fundamental concern. Dapivirine from the vaginal ring achieves cervicovaginal tissue concentrations approximately 1,000-fold higher than plasma [8], with geometric mean plasma concentrations of 220–280 pg/mL from the 25 mg monthly ring [11]. Hair incorporates drug from the systemic circulation via the blood supply to the hair follicle. Whether the very small systemic dapivirine fraction produces a quantifiable and pharmacologically meaningful hair signal has not been demonstrated. The proposal provides chromatograms of dapivirine spiked into hair [6] but no validation data from clinical ring users—no linear range, LLOQ, or precision metrics for the dapivirine hair assay are published at this stage. Without establishing that hair dapivirine is detectable above the assay LLOQ in actual ring users, the entire aim’s analytic plan—the weighted logistic models relating hair dapivirine to seroconversion, the comparisons with plasma and residual ring drug, and the estimation of protective thresholds [6]—rests on an unvalidated measurement premise. This does not make Aim 2 unsupportable, but it places it squarely in the methods-development stage.

**Segmental timeline interpretation.** Across all aims, the segmental hair timeline (1 cm ≈ 1 month of exposure) inherits uncertainty from inter-individual growth rate variability and uncharacterized effects of African hair morphology on drug incorporation—limitations acknowledged in the directly observed dosing study that established this calibration [1]. The 0.25 cm segments proposed for cabotegravir amplify this sensitivity to growth-rate assumptions. These sources of variability do not prevent useful group-level inferences but constrain the precision of individual-level temporal interpretation.

## Feasibility

The feasibility of this project varies substantially across aims. Aim 1 is well-supported by demonstrated infrastructure and acceptability; the feasibility of Aims 2 and 3 depends critically on unresolved specimen availability questions. The parent-study integration model is inherently efficient, but creates dependencies that the proposal does not fully resolve for its two most novel components.

**Aim 1 (SEARCH): strong operational foundation.** The SEARCH study has already demonstrated that hair collection is feasible in the target East African populations: in the early PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit, and a separate community study collected samples from 837 participants with approximately 95% acceptability [6]. Collection occurred across facility-based, home-based, and community-based settings [10], confirming that the procedure can be integrated into varied delivery models. The existing staff training protocols, room-temperature storage with desiccant, and batch shipment procedures are established and reproducible [6]. The expansion from 16 to 32 communities in SEARCH Phase 2 increases scale but does not introduce fundamentally new operational challenges beyond those already managed. The case-cohort sampling design further enhances feasibility by reducing the assay burden from approximately 83,200 potential specimens to roughly 5,200 person-visits, concentrating laboratory resources on informative cases [6].

**Aim 2 (HOPE): unresolved specimen status.** HOPE enrolled 1,456 women at 14 clinical research centers across Malawi, South Africa, Uganda, and Zimbabwe between July 2016 and October 2018 [14]. The critical unknown is whether hair specimens were collected prospectively during the parent study. If specimens were banked under the described room-temperature storage conditions, the multi-country specimen chain logistics are retrospective—the specimens either exist in usable condition or they do not—and the MTN network’s demonstrated specimen management infrastructure (89.3% ring return rate with residual drug measurement in HOPE [14]) provides reassurance. If, however, hair collection was not embedded in the original HOPE protocol and would require re-contacting participants across four countries, feasibility would be substantially compromised. The proposal states that HOPE has “parent-study ethics approvals and monitoring boards” and that “consent includes hair collection” [6], but this language does not confirm that specimens were actually collected.

**Aim 3 (ACTG A5359): significant integration risk.** The LATITUDE trial enrolled participants from March 2019 to February 2024, with Step 2 randomization stopped early for superiority [15]. The published trial results do not describe hair specimen collection, focusing instead on plasma pharmacokinetics for virologic failure cases [15]. The proposal describes planned hair sampling at weeks 20, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, and 76 [6], but the timing of this ancillary proposal relative to the trial’s completion raises the possibility that specimens were not integrated into the protocol. Without prospective collection, the proposed analyses cannot be conducted.

**Analytical timeline.** The outcome-enriched laboratory strategy—collecting all specimens, waiting for parent study outcomes, then assaying only informative cases—is methodologically sound but concentrates analytical work in the later project period. For SEARCH, the three-year follow-up means outcome data become available only in the project’s latter half. For HOPE, parent study completion in 2018 is advantageous, as outcomes should already be available. The five-year R01 mechanism can accommodate this timeline provided specimen availability is confirmed. The expertise distribution is well-matched to each aim: Gandhi’s laboratory for hair pharmacology, Havlir and Koss for SEARCH, Baeten for HOPE, and Castillo-Mancilla for A5359 [6]. The principal feasibility constraints are therefore not in team capacity or analytical methods, but in the binary question of specimen availability for the two aims that constitute the project’s principal innovation.

## Ethics and safety

The ethical framework and safety management are excellent. The added intervention—collection of approximately 50–100 occipital hair strands—is a minimal-risk procedure that has already demonstrated high acceptability in the relevant populations. The proposal provides proportionate and comprehensive arrangements for consent, confidentiality, monitoring, and community engagement, building on the mature ethical infrastructure of the established parent studies.

**Risk and burden.** Hair collection adds roughly 90 seconds to study visits and involves cutting a small thatch of hair from beneath the upper hair layer close to the occipital scalp [6]. The physical risks are negligible: minor accidental scalp injury from scissors is the most foreseeable harm, and the proposal appropriately acknowledges this. The exclusion of individuals with completely bald scalps—for whom axillary or pubic hair is not an accepted substitute—is a necessary biological limitation rather than an ethical deficiency [6]. The practical burden on participants is minimal, and the procedure requires no phlebotomy, avoids the discomfort and infection risk of blood draws, and uses room-temperature storage that eliminates cold-chain complexity for sites [6].

**Acceptability evidence.** The acceptability data are reassuring. In the early SEARCH PrEP rollout, 112 of 113 participants provided hair at the one-month visit, and a separate SEARCH community study collected samples from 837 participants with approximately 95% acceptability [6]. These rates, achieved across facility-based, home-based, and community-based delivery settings in rural Kenya and Uganda [10], indicate that hair collection, when accompanied by appropriate staff training and participant education, imposes no meaningful barrier to research participation in these populations. The acceptability evidence also addresses a potential cultural concern: hair carries varied cultural significance across communities, and the demonstration that collection is broadly accepted in the target East and Southern African settings is an important ethical safeguard.

**Consent and oversight.** The consent and oversight framework is robust. All three parent studies—SEARCH, HOPE (MTN-025), and ACTG A5359—have existing ethics approvals and monitoring boards, and the proposal confirms that consent documents include hair collection [6]. The SEARCH study permits participation of adolescents aged 15–17 only with parental co-consent, a proportionate additional protection for minors [6]. The LATITUDE trial (A5359) protocol received central and local institutional review board approval, with NIAID Division of AIDS providing regulatory sponsorship and clinical monitoring [15]. The proposal’s reliance on these parent-study governance structures is appropriate for an ancillary study that adds a single low-risk specimen to an already-approved protocol.

**Data stewardship.** Confidentiality and data security receive appropriate attention. The proposal describes labeling and orientation checking with sites, monthly completeness reviews by coordinators and statisticians, and reporting of adverse events or confidentiality breaches through study leadership [6]. The commitment to making blinded hair-concentration datasets publicly available after planned analyses and to sharing findings with collaborating communities and policy stakeholders reflects good practice in data stewardship and community accountability.

A minor limitation is procedural rather than ethical: the proposal does not describe specific procedures for managing incidental findings from hair analysis, nor does it detail how participants will be informed of their individual hair drug concentration results. In the treatment context (Aim 3), hair cabotegravir levels indicating low exposure could have potential clinical relevance. However, given that the assays are performed in batch after outcome ascertainment—with a research-grade rather than clinical turnaround time—the proposal’s implicit model of aggregate analysis rather than real-time individual feedback is consistent with the stated research purpose. This should be made explicit in the consent materials.

## Management

The management arrangements are strong—a well-matched investigator team, clear role allocation, and an efficient analytical strategy—but are contingent on specimen availability in the two parent studies that constitute the project’s principal innovation.

**Investigator expertise.** The investigator team is complementary and possesses the specific expertise required for each aim. Monica Gandhi leads the hair pharmacology program with over 15 years of published work on hair antiretroviral measurement in both treatment and prevention contexts, including the foundational dose-response calibration for tenofovir [1], the demonstration that hair ARV levels are the strongest independent predictor of virologic suppression [2] [3] [4], and the direct collaboration with SEARCH that established hair collection feasibility in the target populations [5] [10]. Diane Havlir leads the SEARCH platform and has co-authored the SEARCH PrEP outcomes and hair tenofovir analyses with Gandhi [5], providing seamless coordination for Aim 1. Jared Baeten led both the ASPIRE and HOPE dapivirine ring trials [14] and provides the MTN network access essential for Aim 2. Jose Castillo-Mancilla is a co-investigator on ACTG A5359 and has an established research program in pharmacologic adherence measures including hair tenofovir and dried blood spot tenofovir-diphosphate [16] [17] [18], positioning him to bridge the parent trial and the hair ancillary study for Aim 3. The additional expertise—Leslie Benet and Roy Gerona for pharmacokinetic and analytical chemistry support, and Peter Bacchetti for statistical design—fills the remaining technical requirements [6].

**Analytical workflow and timeline.** The analytical workflow is coherently organized around an outcome-enriched, two-phase sampling strategy that selects specimens for assay only after parent-study endpoints are ascertained. This concentrates laboratory resources on informative cases—all seroconverters plus weighted controls—while recovering population-level inference through inverse-probability-of-sampling weights [6]. The strategy is methodologically efficient and correctly aligns analytical work with the availability of outcome data. The proposal further schedules capacity-building work with African laboratories associated with SEARCH and MTN-025 in the final project year [6], which is a logical extension of the team’s existing collaborations and appropriately sequenced after the primary analyses.

**Critical dependency: specimen availability.** The critical management dependency lies not in team capacity or analytical planning but in the prerequisite that specimens exist to be analyzed. For Aim 1 (SEARCH), this is well-established: hair collection is already embedded in SEARCH operations with documented high acceptability [6] [10]. For Aim 2 (HOPE), the proposal states that consent includes hair collection and that HOPE has parent-study ethics approvals [6], but does not confirm that hair specimens were actually collected during the trial, which completed enrollment in 2018 [14]. For Aim 3 (A5359), the completed LATITUDE trial publication does not describe hair collection [15], and the proposal’s description of planned sampling timepoints [6] requires confirmation that the ancillary protocol was integrated before trial completion. These are binary feasibility questions—either the specimens are banked or they are not—that the management plan cannot resolve through coordination or contingency planning alone. Support should be conditioned on verification of specimen availability for these two aims, which together carry the project’s principal innovation in long-acting product monitoring.
