## nci_hdrp_r01_coronado

Predicting and Addressing Colonoscopy Non-adherence in Community Settings (PRECISE)

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports; truncated first display of A/B recovered in subsequent reads.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Targets loss to follow-up after a positive FIT, testing risk-stratified navigation rather than merely re-demonstrating that navigation is effective; distinguishes predicting non-adherence from predicting intervention benefit. (proposal lines 8–27, 32–34)
- **V2**: External validation on a historical cohort, recalibration if necessary, then randomization within low/medium adherence strata can yield locally generalizable average intervention effects and model transfer insights. (proposal lines 75–81, 102–116, 138–142)
- **V3**: Cost, risk-stratum allocation, implementation by real clinical staff, and qualitative research together provide knowledge for sustainable deployment, without treating mechanistic breakthroughs as the threshold of value. (proposal lines 100, 144–168, 172–174)
- **L1**: The high-adherence stratum is observational only, so the gain of navigation for that stratum cannot be directly inferred; weak discrimination and a large detectable interaction limit claims about optimal targeting configurations. (proposal lines 34, 79–81, 104–112, 166)
- **L2**: Clinical contacts in different arms and out-of-hospital colonoscopy records may affect measurement and contamination; with blinded audits and a 10% usual-care spot check already in place, one cannot say there is no control at all, nor arbitrarily specify the overall direction of bias. (proposal lines 104, 114–118)
- **L3**: Feasibility and generalizability depend on local resources, reachable patients, navigator hours, and endoscopy capacity; one-year costs are interpreted per completed colonoscopy, and precision for rare cancers and subgroup conclusions is limited. (proposal lines 63–71, 85, 100, 142–152, 166–174)

### WithoutConsultation (alias A)

Overall: 2/5; Value: 3/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The proposal contains actual scientific analysis, but nearly the entire text uses post-project results to argue for the original plan's value and feasibility. Line 36 calls P=.16 confirmation of the null hypothesis, and line 54 confuses the later top tertile of a randomized population with the top quintile from the applicant's follow-up only, constituting a decisive factual/inferential error. Cost and implementation value are still recognized.


### smolagents (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Accurately identifies the lack of random comparison in the high-adherence group and insufficient interaction precision, and retains the average effect and economic research value. However, line 56 changes stratification by adherence level/age/sex to stratification by county and Sealed Envelope, and line 59 replaces ITT with modified ITT; although line 28 claims to isolate subsequent results, it still uses post-implementation facts to supplement the application in multiple places.


### ThorneSectionWise (alias C)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 4/5.

Faithful to the application version, clearly distinguishes the navigation effect in low/medium adherence populations from the superiority of targeted strategies over universal navigation, and conditionally supports what is commensurate with obtainable knowledge. Calling an independent three-arm trial the only required design is somewhat absolute, and the space devoted to management/ethics shortcomings is slightly heavy but does not dominate as a veto.


### SingleResearchAgent (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The scientific value trade-off is better than A, retaining average effect and cost evidence; but line 7 claims contamination is not mitigated, while the main text acknowledges a 10% spot check. Line 66 directly pushes multiple potential biases into an upper bound of the effect, with neither direction nor necessity established. Lines 32/46/70–78 etc. use subsequent results of this project to support the original plan, reducing the reliability of the basis.


### SpaceBioReview (alias E)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 4/5.

Most coherently balances the value of the main effect, the precision of targeted understanding, and practical implementation capacity, and largely does not treat subsequent results of the same project as proof of success. Still, line 112 claims that restricting to the high-adherence population is necessary to answer the question, and line 124 states that 20 months accommodates 18 months of recruitment and one year of follow-up, which are overly strong assertions, hence not a perfect score.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

Overall useful and retains exploratory value, but lines 32/46 overclaim that the main effect can prove who benefits most, and line 36 calls non-significance under a smaller true effect uninterpretable; 13 percentage points is also not the upper end of the cited 10–24 point range. Multiple external model differences and trial conclusions point only to application [1], with an incomplete chain of evidence.


### Source checks

- [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13007779/): Primary article checked earlier in this same conversation: published trial uses randomized-population tertiles; these do not equal the proposal's surveillance-only top quintile. Current scoring also directly verifies allocation against proposal lines 79–112.

## nhgri_r01_brothers

Addressing Ethical Challenges in Networked Biorepositories

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports; missing portions of initial A/B displays recovered by line ranges.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Local commitment and shared governance conflicts in cross-institutional networks constitute an independent knowledge issue; general single-repository ethics literature cannot be equated with having resolved this. (proposal lines 9–19,27–31)
- **V2**: Triangulation of inventories, interviews/documents, and sustained observation can reveal actual decision-making processes and organizational configuration differences, rather than merely aggregating stakeholder opinions. (proposal lines 43–91)
- **V3**: Empirical issues are integrated with ethical/legal normative arguments, using focus groups and expert discussions to formulate governance recommendations that are usable and adaptable to configurations. (proposal lines 103–115)
- **L1**: Qualitative case studies and leader introductions may involve selection/power biases; existing opposition sampling and lack of direct donor access necessitate limiting representativeness rather than automatically dismissing the entire project. (proposal lines 51–55,77–91)
- **L2**: Empirical acceptability cannot automatically prove normative legitimacy; traceable fact–norm reasoning, handling of dissent, and applicability conditions across configurations are needed. Having a normative core does not mean no normative analysis has been conducted. (proposal lines 105–111,119)
- **L3**: Access, reflexivity, and identity inference risks in embedded observation affect the visibility and credibility of real issues; in addition to two annual field visits, there are ongoing meeting/phone observations, with existing reflection and safeguards. (proposal lines 87–101,165–169)

### ThorneSectionWise (alias A)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 4/5.

Explains the independent value of network research and on-site process knowledge; the donor data boundary and normative transparency constraints are proportionate, and no confirmatory experiment was required. The information is complete but unusually verbose, with repeated management omissions. Minor errors such as misspelling Rothstein as Rothwell at line 201 do not determine the judgment.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias B)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 4/5.

Adequately identifies the multi-method contribution; the gap in normative integration is not escalated to infeasibility. The overall assessment at line 9 highlights two field visits as a major limitation, while the main text at line 70 supplements with sustained remote observation, causing slight distortion in the abstract; the current value argument still relies heavily on historical originality and does not sufficiently demonstrate a present-day gap.


### WithoutConsultation (alias C)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Provides in-depth comparison but upgrades the N/A in Table 4 to a lack of observation access for a specific institution; at line 80, infers actual progress issues from never having seen an observational paper, and uses the project's outcomes to validate the original plan. At line 127, claims Rivera found no peer-reviewed papers, yet at line 148, the self-listed references include Rivera as the first author; this reveals over-interpretation of evidence search results.


### SingleResearchAgent (alias D)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 4/5.

Offers substantive explanation of the value of the research object and methods, asymmetric observation, and the relationship between empirical and normative reasoning, while retaining supporting conclusions. The repeated qualifications not provided in the budget/CV and the wording that the normative framework is not yet fixed, thus reducing certainty, are slightly heavy-handed, but this is not treated as a fundamental failure.


### smolagents (alias E)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

Effectively isolates subsequent publications from the same project and evaluates according to the actual research type. However, the clearly shared coding and normative team is still summarized as insufficient main evidence chain, with overly stringent conditions; at lines 77/80, Q3–Q14 spanning three years is counted as two years, and at line 100, it states data management was not discussed while acknowledging a shared secure workspace, indicating mild information compression distortion.


### SpaceBioReview (alias F)

There remains a tendency to use the same project's 2024 outcomes to support claims of gaps/value; at line 102, describing limited proxy representativeness as unreliable is too strong, and at line 116, referring to Q11–Q16 as the final year is inaccurate, thus the overall quality and grounding do not receive full marks.

## nci_epi_r03_prizment

Immune-Regulating MHC Class I-Like Proteins and Colorectal Cancer Risk

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports, including tails. Checked the decisive 2023 article and the official grant linkage.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Pre-diagnostic risk associations in cancer-free populations differ from prognosis in those with cancer, and the distinct roles of membrane-bound versus soluble proteins constitute a specific question. (proposal lines 8–18,23)
- **V2**: Repeated measures of six proteins and lifestyle/genetic determinants can provide insights into temporal trajectories and sources of variation, not merely a cross-sectional correlation. (proposal lines 18,23–25,77–89)
- **V3**: Leveraging existing ARIC multi-omics and follow-up for an R03 preliminary association study has reusable discovery value even if it cannot confirm mechanisms or immediately improve screening. (proposal lines 10,59–63,99–107)
- **L1**: SOMAscan reproducibility does not equal specificity for target proteins/soluble forms; RFU, genetic variant effects on binding, and shared factor heterogeneity limit biological interpretation, with orthogonal validation to follow. (proposal lines 14–16,31,63,68,89,101)
- **L2**: Follow-up start has been reset to Visit 3/5 with a 2/5-year lag; reverse causality, time-varying confounding, long-term survival/selection, and precision with only 56 post-Visit 5 events must still be addressed, though it cannot be said that temporal protection is entirely absent. (proposal lines 61,70–81)
- **L3**: MR is a secondary exploration; selecting instruments based on protein and CRC concurrent associations, MHC linkage/pleiotropy, and binding artifacts mean F>10 is insufficient to establish causality, and this cannot be used to negate the independent observational objective. (proposal lines 85–96)

Asserting that the factor must be dominated by MICA/MICB and that future ULBP work must demonstrate distinct signaling as a value threshold lacks sufficient grounding; line 58 also incorrectly refers to a nested case-control design.


### SpaceBioReview (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Substantive insights were gained regarding measurement, temporality, weighting, and protein differences, and the R03 value is retained; however, lines 40/54 treat a subsequent paper from the same project as a deduction for originality. Line 72 claims F>10 stems from a two-sample MR error, line 74 upgrades the failure to find ULBP pQTLs into non-existence, and line 76 directly asserts six months is insufficient. These overly strong inferences undermine the main rationale.


### WithoutConsultation (alias C)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 3/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Captures the genuine issue of the original paper selecting instruments based on CRC association and retains the observational objective. However, treats a subsequent paper under the same funding as prior work; line 80's claim that factors cannot be applied externally with fixed loadings is overly absolute, line 86 categorically dismisses exclusion restrictions, line 100 estimates without basis that a lag would lose 30–50% of cases, and line 142 writes six months as two months, resulting in an imbalanced strength of argument.


### smolagents (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The main line is largely accurate, emphasizing that replicability does not equal specificity and that exploratory results are permissible. Line 78 confuses v4 with v4.1/7K, thereby questioning whether the six targets explicitly specified in the original application are on the panel; line 123 upgrades orthogonal validation into a necessary pre-publication condition, which is overly strict. The absolute claim without literature at line 38 is also unreliable, hence not a high score.


### ThorneSectionWise (alias E)

Overall: 2/5; Value: 3/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Contains valid methodological concerns, but lines 14/48/115 treat the factor score as the core of Aim 2, whereas the application's lines 83–89 model each protein individually in Aim 2. This exaggerates the impact of a single-factor failure on the entire plan and leads to a revise recommendation; line 76 treats an exploratory immune variation study directly as a clinically actionable incidental finding, and ethical requirements are also elevated.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The trade-off between the exploratory stage and independent value is well weighed, and the measurement-genetics integration issue is specific. However, lines 34/40/50 generalize the paper's male sMICA subgroup results as confirming both MICA and MICB, and treat the project's outcomes as pre-existing foundation; the more direct problem that the original paper's instruments were selected based on results is omitted, lowering the evidence score.

## nci_epi_r21_sun

Estimation and Association Analysis of Biomarkers for Tumor Immune Microenvironment

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports, including separately recovered reference/end sections.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Dual-omics joint deconvolution, allowing reference profiles to adapt to the tumor environment and correcting for RNA contribution and cell number differences, is a testable methodological contribution. (proposal lines 14-21,55-75)
- **V2**: Incorporating cell proportions as shared latent variables into the survival model and comparing with two-stage approaches can investigate the impact of measurement uncertainty and compositional constraints on prediction; this is not equivalent to treatment efficacy prediction. (proposal lines 78-99)
- **V3**: Even if no clinically usable markers are obtained, R21 exploration can still deliver insights into error/identifiability, publicly available software, and cross-cancer benchmarks. (proposal lines 8-10,71-75,99-105)
- **L1**: Identifiability and model misspecification: flexible cell profiles, fixed tumor purity, similar cell profiles, scale/batch differences; having more samples than cell types is not a sufficient condition. (proposal lines 36-65)
- **L2**: Validation with real mixtures and strict hold-out must cover the entire joint fitting/feature and tuning pipeline, avoiding self-verification through simulation and survival information leakage. (proposal lines 71-75,80-99)
- **L3**: The number of survival events, censoring/risk models, zero proportions, and tree-sequence fusion affect predictive reliability; these should be matched to the two-year exploratory scope rather than requiring immediate clinical validation. (proposal lines 89-105)

### SingleResearchAgent (alias A)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 3/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The comments on uncertainty calibration and scale identification are valuable; however, using ICeD-T/EMeth—produced under the same R21 grant—as prior work, and even requiring an explanation at line 120 for why the proposal retreats from an approximation that preceded later results, is problematic. Line 34 lacks sufficient justification for the expected C-index gain of 0.01–0.04 and restricts value accordingly, while directly interpreting high omics dimensionality as implying a weak joint effect on survival estimation. Several strong assertions reduce credibility.


### WithoutConsultation (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The suggestions on direct multi-omics baselines, identifying required changes, and methylation anchoring are specific and valuable. The main issue is that line 24 does not support the clearly inconsistent claim that the risk is manageable and the R21 scope is reasonable throughout the text, and it fails to specify which gap is sufficient to reject the proposal. Additionally, some model validation risks are stated too definitively, without analyzing hold-out leakage in actual fitting.


### smolagents (alias C)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The identification of methodological risks is useful, and the conditions are mostly moderate, but the evaluation explicitly applies the state of the art from 2015–2017 to today's project; repeatedly treats the exponential distribution example as the only established survival setting, and uses the project's subsequent EMeth results to demonstrate existing capability. The hold-out and prediction leakage of the full joint pipeline have not yet been addressed.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The judgments on value and exploratory stage are reasonable, but the review mainly reiterates the proposal's strengths, and line 58 nearly accepts the condition that having more samples than cell types is sufficient for identifiability; the hold-out leakage risk in joint fitting is not examined. The innovation section makes a general claim that no current joint method exists without providing adequate support; internal reference markers remain in the formal text.


### SpaceBioReview (alias E)

Overall: 4/5; Value: 5/5; Grounding: 4/5.

This review most fully recognizes the value of methodological and error insights even if the C-index improvement is limited, and separately compares current tool capabilities. The identifiability argument is specific, and the exploration risk is connected to the funding judgment. It still rather directly equates joint maximum likelihood with realized uncertainty propagation and does not check training/test information isolation, so the highest overall score is not assigned.


### ThorneSectionWise (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 3/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The ablation suggestion to distinguish sources of predictive gain is good, and purity errors are also identified. However, treating the lack of prior demonstration of dual-omics advantages—still to be investigated—as a core funding barrier weakens the R21 exploratory value; it claims that simulations necessarily follow model assumptions without any approximate diagnostics, ignoring existing empirical distributions and preliminary checks. The absence of local data archiving information is also exaggerated into a substantive dependency.

## nci_hdrp_r01_braithwaite

Personalized Screening for Lung Cancer: Importance of Co-existing Chronic Conditions to Clinical Practice and Policy

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports, with end sections read separately.

### Reference points

- **V1**: High lung cancer risk does not equate to large net screening benefit; jointly quantifying competing mortality, treatment applicability, and procedural harms has practical scientific value. (proposal lines 8,35-41)
- **V2**: Real-world screening cohorts from three systems can generate independent empirical knowledge on stratified complications, function, and lung function distributions, complementing healthier trial populations. (proposal lines 10,45-46,62-96)
- **V3**: Linking cohort parameters with established microsimulation allows comparison of strategies and preference-sensitive thresholds; it can provide conditional decision insights without requiring a new RCT to be valuable. (proposal lines 47,112-120)

Materials examined: Full proposal and all six reports; verified CAB case-report timing, parent protocol hair collection, and HOPE event counts. This assesses review reasoning, not clinical advice.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Combining long-term hair with recent plasma in real-world PrEP services to study exposure/use patterns, effectiveness, and renal outcomes, making full use of existing cohorts. (proposal lines 7–17,37,46–48,64–76)
- **V2**: Extending hair measurement to topical rings and cross-comparing with plasma, residual drug, and self-report to examine the incremental value of different biomarkers. (proposal lines 15–17,38,92–100)
- **V3**: Studying individual PK of long-acting CAB and dosing interval changes; Aim 3 is a treatment cohort, and the resulting calibration can provide methods for future prevention studies rather than directly proving preventive effectiveness. (proposal lines 7,39,115–125)
- **L1**: Hair concentration is simultaneously influenced by use, PK, and hair biology; observational exposure–infection relationships and drug use varying with risk do not automatically yield a causal protective threshold. (proposal lines 13–17,66–76,94–100,121–125)
- **L2**: Drugs and routes of administration require independent validation; spiked hair does not equal reliable detection in real subjects; 0.25 cm ≈ one week, plasma-to-hair conversion, and local tissue protection all await calibration. (proposal lines 15,48–59,98,119–125)
- **L3**: Effective event numbers and sparse subgroups constrain thresholds and predictive precision; the overlap of cases plus random non-case sampling and renal events must be handled according to the committed weights, and missing hair/refusals and incomplete visits may still introduce selection bias. (proposal lines 68–76,94–100,123,146–148)

### ThorneSectionWise (alias A)

Overall: 2/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The scientific questions and calibration dependencies are generally correctly identified, but the review uses generalized ethical concerns as the primary reason for rejection, overlooking the 95% acceptance rate, training, and governance of the parent study. Line 96 describes sampling beneath the upper hair layer as implying visible sampling; line 97 treats the weekly-level time series—which line 63 itself deems not yet accurately recoverable—as already capable of precisely reconstructing individual behavior, rendering the risk argument self-contradictory. The questioning of the cyclical nature of the renal analysis at line 61 ignores the clearly defined outcome enrichment and weighting design, and line 27 should not restrict correctly weighted inferences to only the sampled population.


### smolagents (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The scope and exploratory value are reasonably grasped, and line 166 proactively excludes outcomes from the same parent trial. A key factual error is that lines 17 and 63 repeatedly state that all three Aims use a case–control sampling design with seroconversion as the outcome, whereas Aim 3 is actually a full PK follow-up of the HIV-positive treatment arm. Line 77 claims that consistency across multiple adherence indicators reduces the likelihood of shared confounding, which lacks evidence; line 15 refers to random non-cases as matched controls. Sufficient usable calibration, event numbers, and temporal analyses remain available.


### SpaceBioReview (alias C)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The relationship between measurement maturity and research value is well articulated, and the necessary calibration studies are retained. No risk-behavior confounding is identified; line 60 treats repeated visits as inevitably increasing power, line 75 describes the total sample of 240 as 240 controls, lines 32 and 50 use the same SEARCH post-hoc outcome as an independent prior study, and line 93 uses 150×7 while ignoring that segmentation increases the number of analyses. Conditional support for the core rationale is reasonable, but the plan does not yet fully establish a causal protective threshold.


### SingleResearchAgent (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The analysis of sampling weights, single-arm exposure–infection confounding, and the value of new measurements is relatively specific, and the study is not rejected on grounds of being exploratory. However, decisive evidence contains errors: line 76 pairs the hair window from the two weeks before diagnosis in cases with plasma from 14 days after diagnosis as simultaneous, and the concentration unit ratios are miscalculated twice; line 88 writes 16 PrEP intervention communities as 32. Lines 68 and 78 also directly substitute the parent trial’s later actual outcomes for the prospectively planned event numbers, failing to distinguish post-hoc available data from design risk, although the outcome numbers themselves are verifiable.


### WithoutConsultation (alias E)

Overall: 2/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

It correctly points out that new drug calibration and a unified platform do not equal a unified data product, but lines 7, 80–84, and 108 elevate the parent study paper’s omission of hair sampling to a major integration failure risk, whereas the public A5359 protocol explicitly lists optional hair sampling. Since the historical parent-study status is adopted, the protocol should have been read before making a judgment. Line 78 again fabricates that SEARCH expanded from 16 to 32 PrEP communities; line 30 cites 25 post-hoc cases, while line 38 still claims sufficient power for this Aim, without reconciling the two. The arithmetic of the ratio at line 68 is correct, but non-contemporaneous cases cannot calibrate the steady-state correspondence.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The distinction between innovation and validation phases is well made, and sampling culture issues are not over-amplified; factual restatements are more stable than those in C/B. The main weakness is an almost unconditional endorsement of Aim 1, lacking analysis of risk-behavior confounding and threshold extrapolation, and being too certain that weekly segmentation can recover temporal sequences. Line 38 writes 16 PrEP communities as 32; line 78 claims that single-time-point power is necessarily conservative and line 80 claims that mixed models have already addressed selective samples, both without conditions; lines 100 and 132 are also not fully consistent regarding the A5359 approval status.


### Source checks

- [Source](https://academic.oup.com/ofid/advance-article-pdf/doi/10.1093/ofid/ofae468/58919526/ofae468.pdf): The hair concentration in cases corresponds to the two weeks before diagnosis, while the cited plasma is from 14 days after diagnosis; these cannot be described as simultaneous measurements. This case supports detection feasibility, not a steady-state conversion standard.
- [Source](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/88/NCT03635788/Prot_ICF_001.pdf): The public A5359 protocol/informed consent includes optional hair drug measurement; the main results paper’s omission of this item does not justify concluding that the ancillary study is unintegrated. This does not, however, retroactively guarantee that every actual sample is already available.
- [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33539762/): HOPE indeed had 1456 participants and 35 infections, but these are post-hoc results of the parent study and must be separated from simulated prospective judgments.

## nhgri_r21_stern

Demographic Patterns of Eugenic Sterilization in California

Materials examined: Read the full application and six reports; verify the sources and uses of the team's subsequent outputs.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Reconstructing institutional population denominators and distinguishing differences in entry into institutions from differences in being recommended for sterilization within institutions adds comparable group evidence to the historical narrative. (proposal lines 20–22,30–32,88–104,126–132)
- **V2**: Nine institutions over thirty-plus years and 212 variables support analysis of differences across time, institutions, and diagnostic categories, providing more granular understanding than merely repeating the known existence of discrimination. (proposal lines 10–14,30,40–45,120–138)
- **V3**: Combining group patterns with close reading of texts on refusal, resistance, and coerced consent preserves the agency of those recorded and constitutes an ELSI and public history contribution; the archive development is a separately supported companion effort. (proposal lines 24–26,34–36,140–156)
- **L1**: Recommendation records do not equal performed surgeries; ten-year recommendation flows versus single-time-point population stocks, repeat residents/recommendations, and annual variations affect denominators and risk interpretation, and the case-cohort label does not automatically resolve this. (proposal lines 49–66,76–82,88–104,128–132)
- **L2**: Hispanic surnames are only an ethnic proxy; cross-era classification, spelling variations, missing data, and institutional diagnostic recording biases must be addressed, and official allegations should not be treated as objective behavioral facts. (proposal lines 49–51,92–104,134–148)
- **L3**: Surviving protest letters and explicit refusals select for those who could express themselves and left records; silence/signatures cannot simply be treated as voluntary or non-resistant, and the interpretation of qualitative coding and quantitative associations must retain this point. (proposal lines 24,142–148)

### smolagents (alias A)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

Value judgments and major methodological issues are fairly balanced. Line 49 correctly identifies outputs from the same project and does not treat them as independent priors. However, line 25 treats the number of recommendations divided by the number of surgeries as a coverage rate, and line 27 still attributes an OR from an already within-hospital comparison to differential admission; line 15 claims the full database has been entered while line 85 acknowledges it is yet to be completed. Insufficient attention to selective focus on refusal records.


### SpaceBioReview (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

Allows descriptive and exploratory results to have independent value, handles archival dependencies accurately, and the discussion of missing data and misclassification is useful. However, lines 5 and 30 describe California's total as one-quarter of the national total (the original text states approximately one-third); line 82 treats the 24% missing rate in a later published sample as a known state in the current application. Line 38: differences in the distribution of diagnostic labels alone cannot prove that labels are used unequally. Line 78: the classification performance for Filipino surnames cannot directly represent the classification performance for Hispanic surnames, and the cited context requires more caution.


### WithoutConsultation (alias C)

Overall: 2/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

There is substantive discussion of quantitative methods, but line 68 upgrades a later Asian immigrant sub-analysis that cannot simultaneously adjust for age and institution into a definitive limitation of the entire application, and line 72's assertion that population rates cannot be estimated is too strong. Subsequent project outputs are repeatedly used to demonstrate benefits. Line 9 states that value and feasibility outweigh resolvable weaknesses, yet gives a non-support score at line 24, lacking a coherent trade-off; and treats recommendation records as surgical coverage volume.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The distinction between the project's knowledge value and the responsibilities of the parallel archive is well made, directly pointing out the temporal mismatch between flows and snapshots. Still treats the detection of a significant pilot association as proof of validity (lines 58, 78). Line 62 infers that spurious associations are unlikely based solely on an OR greater than 2 and the same classification method, without sufficient basis; line 74 describes planned data entry as completed, and line 84's assertion that there are no pre-existing dependencies is too strong. Line 104 contains residual internal evidence markers.


### SingleResearchAgent (alias E)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

Clearly presents knowledge value and two-level comparisons, with the main contribution not being a simple endorsement of the project. The issue is that lines 9, 44, 72, and 76 use clearly identified subsequent outputs of this project to justify the feasibility of the current proposal; line 56 directly deduces bias toward the null from high specificity, lacking conditions for differential misclassification. Lines 76 and 100 reassign Aim 4, which explicitly provides content only to the parallel archive, as requiring the project itself to build the archive, thereby creating a major feasibility gap. The endorsement of the adequacy of the quantitative design is too strong.


### ThorneSectionWise (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

More completely identifies denominators and qualitative material coverage, explicitly notes that data entry is not yet complete, and does not use subsequent outputs to endorse the proposal. Overall leans toward a document completeness review, with community engagement and anonymization technical details repeatedly elevated as reasons for non-support; line 64 describes the qualitative corpus as only 50 letters, ignoring all refusal cases and other communications. Line 154 directly requests a sub-cohort, which may solidify an imprecise design label into a new task; the priority should be to clarify the actual estimation target.


### Source checks

- [Source](https://academic.oup.com/sf/article/102/2/706/7147029): The 2023 Asian immigrant analysis explicitly acknowledges R21-HG009205-01 and is a subsequent output of this project. Its missing rate and small cell limitations cannot automatically become established features of all comparisons in the application.
- [Source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37483944/): The 0.10–0.21 sensitivity is the performance of the Filipino surname list against Census Asian classification, not the sensitivity for identifying Latinos using Hispanic surnames.

## nasa_evs3_farrar_smode_2018

Submesoscale Ocean Dynamics and Vertical Transport (S-MODE)

Materials examined: Read the full proposal and six reports; verify the applicability of LASER observations, satellite scatterometer error analysis, and Wave Glider capabilities.

### Reference points

- **V1**: Multi-platform repeat observations distinguish frontal processes from waves and test models with different resolutions and parameterizations; the benchmark data themselves hold value. (proposal lines 5,35–51,58–66,239–241)
- **V2**: Links centimeter-layer radar currents to drifter and ADCP depth currents, forming a physically conditioned remote sensing interpretation and validation. (proposal lines 74–78,129,243)
- **V3**: Explores vertical velocity and tracer covariances, and surface flux proxies; even if the proxies are only partially valid, the threshold objective and mechanistic understanding retain independent value. (proposal lines 14,80–90,243)
- **L1**: Spatiotemporal aliasing of rapidly evolving structures, wave separation, and the limitations of a few strong-front observations for extrapolating regional/seasonal net fluxes. (proposal lines 37,51,62–66,88–90,144–148,239)
- **L2**: D multiplied by the vertical scale h does not automatically represent net flux; the conditional dependence of h, optical quantities versus true material fluxes, error covariances, and the independence of calibration and validation need to be addressed. (proposal lines 80–84,118,153,243,311)
- **L3**: Joint operations and actual observation accuracy are key dependencies: the pilot excludes drifters and Seagliders, weather and platform losses, grid points do not equal effective resolution; consequences must be evaluated against the threshold task. (proposal lines 51,131,144–148,160,239,247–249,293–305)

### SpaceBioReview (alias A)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 5/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The report recognizes the value of exploration, model testing, and threshold-level outcomes. It states that the models themselves require validation and correctly identifies 6 features per season. Its main error is at line 68: it turns a discussion of 6 m/s winds in satellite scatterometer simulations with a particular footprint and noise level into a stricter practical threshold for airborne DopplerScatt, without establishing applicability. Line 40 inaccurately describes SWOT as directly measuring surface currents; the preceding discussion largely assesses novelty at the proposal's historical date. Overall, the report is useful, but its application of external evidence needs improvement.


### smolagents (alias B)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

Concise and generally balanced, with specific discussion of gradient resolution and wave separation, and without using later project results to support feasibility. Line 28 summarizes the baseline's 6 realizations per season as 6 realizations; line 38 treats the need for calibration itself as a separate limitation on novelty, without distinguishing training from independent validation. The independent scientific value of Goal 3's surface-to-depth interpretation is insufficiently developed. Line 88 first describes the risk ratings as overly optimistic and then the failure probabilities as too high, producing inconsistent wording.


### SingleResearchAgent (alias C)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 5/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The report explains scientific value in detail, particularly the knowledge gained from complex depth relationships and partial success with flux proxies. Its central derivation at line 98 equates fitting a regression coefficient h with pointwise w/D and incorrectly concludes that all calibration points must be reproduced exactly. Line 86 turns approximately 6 features per season into 6 across both seasons; lines 7 and 74 turn at least one 75×300 km wide-area scan into a daily scan. Lines 94, 110, and 118 use later project results to justify the proposed approach. Line 154 misstates platform loss C4/L1 as C1/L3; line 150 directly equates being 300 km offshore with being in international waters.


### ThorneSectionWise (alias D)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The report usefully identifies that the pilot did not exercise all platforms and recognizes the threshold mission's contribution. However, lines 17, 39, and 47 treat final data delivery as the start of all integrated analysis and conclude that only 9 months remain, overlooking continuous analysis in 2021–2022 and synthesis in 2022–2024. Line 15 expands the one-hour requirement to simultaneous coverage of the entire region by all airborne and ocean platforms. The assessment emphasizes scheduling and missing administrative materials while overlooking key wave-attribution issues and the depth-interpretation question in Goal 3.


### StaticResearchCommittee (alias E)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 4/5; Grounding: 3/5.

The report correctly distinguishes threshold objectives from exploratory proxies and treats uncertainty in h as a research question. In its discussion of external literature at line 52, it describes LASER as lacking Lagrangian vertical-velocity observations, although the original study explicitly includes direct drifter and ADCP observations. Line 46 describes the 75×300 km wide-area search as daily; line 112 summarizes all L3 products as due within 6–12 months, although the final MASS delivery is two years later. The methods section largely restates the proposal and gives limited attention to the representativeness of a few strong fronts.


### WithoutConsultation (alias F)

Overall: 3/5; Value: 5/5; Grounding: 2/5.

The report offers valuable discussions of why point measurements and area averages need not coincide, why models can validate sampling without establishing their own physical truth, and how flux estimates at different scales compare. However, lines 64 and 106 describe the Wave Glider as unable to hold station actively, whereas manufacturer documentation explicitly specifies conditional station-keeping capability. Line 62 directly treats a ±30% concentration error as satisfying a 0.05 mg/m3 difference requirement. Lines 118 and 146 repeatedly read residual likelihood L1 as consequence C1, although the original table retains consequences of 3–5. Line 94 turns a 55% mass margin into use of only 55% of the mass budget. Lines 32, 50, and 64 use subsequent project results to endorse the current proposal.


### Source checks

- [Source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5819445/): The LASER study explicitly includes independent vertical velocity observations from Lagrangian drifters and ADCPs, as well as aircraft surveys. C equates the data used in a drifter paper with the full LASER capability.
- [Source](https://doi.org/10.1175/JTECH-D-23-0052.1): Chelton analyzed a simulated satellite scatterometer with a 5 km footprint and 1800 km swath; the 6 m/s corresponds to a specific measurement noise discussion and cannot be directly adopted as a hard threshold for an airborne instrument. Full text at 403, verification based on publisher-indexed abstracts and retrieved relevant original passages.
- [Source](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/boeingdotcom/defense/autonomous-systems/wave-glider-sharc/wave_glider_product_sheet.pdf): The manufacturer lists a 30 m station-keeping capability, subject to sea state and navigation mode constraints. This refutes the claim of absolute inability to hold station, but does not prove that the array will necessarily be maintained under any currents in this project.
